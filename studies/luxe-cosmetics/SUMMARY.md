# Luxe Cosmetics: what the four products are, and how to sell them (summary)

Pulled and checked 2026-10-31. Five research helpers wrote one report each (`01`–`05`); five more re-checked a sample of every report against the sources (`checks/`). Their corrections are applied below and noted at the top of each report. **[M]** = measured from a source; **[R]** = our reading.

**What couldn't be seen:** Amazon blocks automated page loads from here, so only the eyelash serum's Amazon page was read. For the mascara, pads and balm, the Amazon price, stars, review count and review text are "not found". The operator should look at those three pages by hand.

---

## 1. The short version

1. **Who the brand is [M].**
   - One small business behind two web shops:
     - **luxe.com**: Luxe Cosmetics B.V., Helmond, Netherlands, a one-person firm founded April 2023, with a German phone number. It sells lashes, brows and mascara.
     - **luxeresearchlab.com**: "Luxe Research LLC", a Wyoming registered-agent address. It sells the pads and the eye balm, aimed at "women over 40".
   - Both shops give the same Casper, Wyoming legal address in their terms, share testimonials and images, and sell each other's products.
   - On Amazon, one seller ("LuxeCosmetics", 4.2★ from 1,386 seller ratings, selling since 2023) sells the serum, the mascara and the balm. The pads' Amazon listing couldn't be tied to that seller.
2. **The products are mostly ordinary formulas with big marketing [M].**
   - The pads use the same 45 ingredients as Anua's PDRN pad, and their images are Anua's, recoloured pink.
   - The balm's claim images show a generic "Wrinkle Core Stick" sold on eBay and Walmart.
   - The serum is a peptide, biotin and ginseng serum.
   - The mascara's ingredients aren't published anywhere.
3. **The brand's own pages have serious red flags. Never repeat them on camera [M]:**
   - The "Rated 4.86 (10,839 reviews)" badge sits next to review widgets that count 0 reviews.
   - The same six "Verified Buyer" women appear on two products, with rewritten stories and a changed age. All six are dated before the pads launched.
   - Contradictory customer counts: 97K, 197K and 1,197K.
   - A fake "LIVE: 2,847 orders yesterday" banner.
   - "Zero side effects" and "clinically proven", with no study published.
   - A false "James Charles officially partnered" claim. His video shows the Luxe *foundation*, which he called "a nightmare" before deciding he liked it. His mascara in that video was Essence.
   - Drug-type claims, e.g. "stimulate natural hair growth" and "triggers collagen synthesis".
   - Store reviews: Trustpilot 3.8 for luxe.com (4,322 reviews, 17% one-star) and 3.5 for Luxe Research (2,485 reviews, 28% one-star). Complaints are about refunds, hard-to-cancel subscriptions and missing buy-one-get-one items.
4. **What this means for selling [R].** These products can be sold honestly, but only with your own claims and your own testing, never with the brand's. Your 50+ audience is primed to spot this kind of ad. The most-liked comments on mature-skin videos attack "botox in a stick", AI ads and young models selling to older women. Honesty is the selling point.
5. **Ranking of the four for your audience [R]:**

| Rank | Product | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Eyelash serum** | Cheapest ($13), Amazon's Choice, "1K+ bought in past month" and 4.0★ from 495 ratings [M]. It is prostaglandin-free, which answers this audience's biggest lash fear head-on. |
| 2 | **Mascara** | Pitched exactly at your viewer ("Built For Sensitive Eyes & Fine Lashes Over 50"), and it answers real pains: smudging, hard removal, sensitive eyes. But the ingredients are unknown and the page contradicts itself on waterproofing. You must test it yourself first. |
| 3 | **Under-eye balm** | The biggest search pool (under-eye videos for this age group reach 2–9M views). But the evidence is thin, the claims are inflated and the review signals are fake-looking. Sell it only as a "concealer base and moisturiser". |
| 4 | **PDRN toner pads** | Highest risk: a copy of Anua's pad with Anua's images. Anua sells the same formula cheaper ($0.38 a pad against $0.43). Topical PDRN is mostly unproven. And it's made from salmon while the sister shop says "all our products are vegan". |

## 2. Product by product

### 2.1 Eyelash serum (B0C21JM5PY), file `04-eyelash-serum.md`

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is [M]** | 3 mL brush-on serum for the upper lash line, applied nightly. Ingredients: water, glycerin, panax ginseng, biotin, vitamin E, arginine/lysine polypeptide, 1,2-hexanediol, tripeptide-1, caprylhydroxamic (octyl hydroxamic) acid, myristoyl pentapeptide-4. "Luxe is 100% prostaglandin-free." |
| **Amazon [M]** | $13.00 (typical $15.99), 4.0★ from 495 ratings, Amazon's Choice, "1K+ bought in past month", 25 other sellers from $12.99. It works out at **$4.33 per mL**, among the cheapest in the category. The brand site charges $29.95. |
| **In a routine** | Last step at night, on a clean, dry lash line, after removing mascara and cleansing. |
| **Who for** | Short, sparse or brittle lashes. Women 50+ whose lashes have thinned with age. People who fear prescription-strength serums, including those on glaucoma drops or after cataract surgery, who are warned about Latisse. |
| **Problem and how it differs [R]** | The category's big fear is side effects, not price. The strong serums (Latisse, GrandeLASH-MD, Babe Essential, neuLASH, RevitaLash Advanced) contain prostaglandin-type ingredients [M]. Those can darken the iris permanently, darken the lids and hollow the eye area. Luxe avoids that. The trade-off: peptide serums have "a lack of formal evidence" for growth. An ophthalmologist: "you're not going to see the tremendous growth that you do with the prostaglandins". |
| **Feedback [M]** | Little independent feedback exists. Two YouTube reviews split: "it burned every time I put it on… I wouldn't spend my money on this one" (a 10-year Latisse user, 47 views), and "I don't have any stinging" (701 views, an Amazon affiliate). In 148 category comments, 27 report side effects, mostly from prostaglandins. The top wishes: "Can you share brands that are safer…" (334 likes) and "recommend a few lash serums that work, and don't have any of the harmful ingredients?" (128 likes). That gap is where Luxe fits. |
| **The category [M]** | Latisse (prescription bimatoprost): 78% improved against 18% on the dummy solution at 16 weeks, lashes 25% longer and 106% fuller. Iris darkening is "likely to be permanent", and lashes go back to how they were when you stop. Hidden prostaglandins are common: in a Japanese test, 3 of the 4 bimatoprost serums found didn't declare it. **The EU is moving to ban prostaglandin analogues in cosmetics** (sent to the Council on 22 Sep 2026; adoption expected Q4 2026; not yet law). That strengthens the prostaglandin-free pitch. |
| **Honest pitch** | "A gentle, prostaglandin-free conditioning serum that helps lashes look fuller and healthier over 8–12 weeks." Don't say "grows", "regrowth" or "stops lash loss" (drug claims). Don't say "zero side effects". Do say "patch-test, stop if it stings, ask your eye doctor if you've had eye surgery or use eye drops". Photograph the Amazon box's ingredient list before saying "prostaglandin-free" on camera. |

### 2.2 Mascara (B0GSXJCZ93), file `01-mascara.md`

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is [M]** | "Luxe Cosmetics Conditioning Peptide Mascara, Buildable Volume & Length, Long-Lasting Clump-Free Black, Lash Extension Friendly". Black only. Brand site: "Built For Sensitive Eyes & Fine Lashes Over 50", water-based, "pH balanced", "hypoallergenic", safe for contact lenses, comes off with warm water. A Luxe ad calls it "Korean Lash-Casting", which describes a **tubing** formula: polymer tubes around each lash that slide off in warm water. **The ingredient list is not published anywhere**, and the peptide isn't named. The same page says "Water Resistant", "Waterproof" and "comes off with warm water". |
| **Price [M]** | Brand site $39.95 against a "$59.95" regular price. Amazon UK snippets show £14.99–18.99. The Amazon US price is not found. Stars from search snippets: UK 4.0 (73), Canada 3.6 (93). The brand's own widget shows 11,790 reviews with zero 1–3★ reviews; its UK widget shows 83 one-star reviews. |
| **In a routine** | Last step of morning makeup; removed first at night. It pairs with the serum: mascara by day, serum by night. |
| **Who for** | Women 50+ with short, sparse, straight or light lashes, hooded lids, and sensitive or watery eyes. |
| **Problems it targets [M]** | Lashes shrink with age: 7.98 mm at 22–29 against 6.39 mm at 50–65, and get thinner (Glaser 2014, 179 women). The top complaints in 159 coded comments from over-40/50/60 mascara videos: hard removal (19), smudging and "raccoon eyes" (14), price (11), gave up mascara (10), tubing failures (9), sensitive or watery eyes (8), wants brown (8), won't hold a curl (7). |

The "conditioning peptide" name echoes Grande GrandeMASCARA ($25), which does name its peptide. The "for older women" angle is PrimeLash's (about 26K Amazon reviews). Cheap benchmarks: Maybelline Sky High ($10.82, 187.9K reviews) and Essence Lash Princess ($4.97, 401K reviews). Luxe's difference is the bundle of promises in one tube: over-50, sensitive-eye, warm-water removal. |
| **Strongest viewer lines [M]** | "If i have to scrub my eyes to get it off that is a deal breaker"; "Every single mascara I've tried over the last 30 years plus ends up transferring onto the foundation under my eyes" (age 66); "I am 69 and just recently decided to ditch masacra… I am sick of the mess!"; "At 64 years old, it makes me feel pretty." |
| **Honest pitch** | Test it on camera on a 50+ face: a full day on hooded lids, warm-water removal, and a contact-lens wearer. Then say what you saw. Don't say "heals your lashes", "fast growth", "100% natural", "#1 Best Seller" (a snippet shows #197 in Mascara), "James Charles partnered" or "hypoallergenic" as a promise (FDA: the term has no legal meaning). Say "replace it every 2–4 months" (FDA). |

### 2.3 Korean Under Eye Balm (B0GYK3T766), file `03-under-eye-balm.md`

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is [M]** | A twist-up balm stick: "Lift & Firm 3-in-1 Multi Balm Stick… for Dark Circles, Puffiness, and Fine Lines, with PDRN Collagen Extract". The brand names adenosine (the lead), niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, bisabolol and vitamin E. luxeresearchlab.com lists argan oil instead of niacinamide. "No retinol. No fragrance." The new pack says "PDRN extract"; the old pack said "collagen extract". **No full ingredient list** or size was found. $39.95 on luxe.com (buy one get one free), $34.99 on luxeresearchlab.com. Many look-alike "Luxe Korean Under Eye Balm" listings exist on Amazon (the first helper listed 9, 7 of which the check couldn't confirm; the check found 5 more). |
| **In a routine** | After toner, before sunscreen and concealer, morning and night. Keep it below the lower lashes. |
| **Who for** | Dry, crepey under-eyes; concealer that creases; women who can't tolerate retinol. |
| **What it can and can't do [M]** | There are several kinds of "tired eyes". **Can:** moisturise, soften the look of crepey lines for hours, and smooth the base for concealer. Adenosine has one trial: women 45–65 had smoother crow's-feet skin at 3–8 weeks. **Can't:** remove fat-pad bags (that needs surgery), fill hollows (filler), or fix vascular circles. In a Korean study of 100 people, 35% had vascular dark circles and 54% mixed. Collagen can't soak into skin as collagen. |
| **Positioning [M]** | The Korean multi-balm sticks (Dr.Melaxin $19/9 g, d'Alba $21/10 g, Medicube $22, Kahi) cost about $2.10–2.68 a gram. Cheap proven options: The Ordinary caffeine (~$0.35/mL), Beauty of Joseon retinal eye serum ($17/30 mL), RoC retinol ($21.99/15 mL). Luxe can't be priced per gram, because its size isn't stated. |
| **Red flags [M]** | The 10,839-review badge against 0 reviews in the widget. Testimonials shared with the pads. Two different "study" sizes (2,431 and 2,844). Guarantees of 30 or 90 days. "Send it back even empty" against a policy where the buyer pays return shipping to the Netherlands. The "44.9% wrinkle reduction" image shows a different (blue) stick. |
| **Feedback [M]** | Independent feedback is thin: 10 statements, of which 4 put off buying, 2 saw no effect, 3 had shipping or refund problems and 1 was positive. "Is it a scam?" videos appeared from 9 April 2026. |
| **Honest pitch** | "A fragrance-free, retinol-free moisturising stick that gives concealer a smooth base." A script frame: "There are four kinds of tired eyes; a balm fixes one layer." Show before and after under the same light, at day 1 and week 4. Don't say "lift", "firm", "erases bags", "fixes collagen loss" or "botox in a stick". |

## 3. How the four work together (routine and cross-sell) [R]

| When | Step | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Cleanse | the viewer's own cleanser (Luxe sells none) |
| | Tone | PDRN pad, 5-minute mask then a wipe |
| | Eyes | under-eye balm, below the lower lashes |
| | Sunscreen | the viewer's own (Luxe sells none) |
| | Lashes | mascara |
| Night | Remove mascara, cleanse, pad, balm | as morning |
| | Last | lash serum on a clean, dry upper lash line |

- **Fast plus slow:** the mascara and balm make you look fresher **today**; the serum and pads are the 4–12-week **long game**.
- **Morning and night:** the mascara is for the day and the serum for the night, so they never compete.
- **One zone, one worry:** all four are about the eyes and the face around them, the "I look tired, I look older" zone this audience complains about most.

## 4. The audience: women 50+, in their own words [M]

From 180 top comments on 9 big mature-beauty videos, plus 159 mascara comments and 148 lash-serum comments:

1. **Dark circles, and concealer making them worse (14):** "Every sales rep at every major cosmetics counter just sold me a concealer… which, of course, emphasized the bags." (509 likes)
2. **Bags and hollows (8):** "When I turned 48, my face just fell."
3. **Crepey, delicate eye skin (6):** "I must apply product which floats over it rather than drags."
4. **Mascara smudging and flaking**, and removal that pulls lashes out.
5. **Thin, invisible lashes**, with fear of serum side effects alongside: "I have light blue eyes and I'm scared to ruin my eye color just for longer lashes."
6. **Wasted money and distrust of hype:** "If something sounds too good to be true, it probably is." (201 likes)
7. **Budget:** "I'm 67 and retired. Just can't afford it." Link Amazon, where prices are 2–3× lower than the brand site.
8. **Cruelty-free matters.** The brand claims it, but no Leaping Bunny or PETA certification was found, so say "the brand says".

**The biggest pools [M]:**
- Under-eye videos: "Eye Doctor Explains How to Get Rid of Under EYE BAGS", 9.09M views.
- Mascara for older women: 50K–350K views.
- Lash serums: mostly doctors warning against them ("Why I Don't Use Lash Growth Serums as a Dermatologist", 539K).

**Channel fit [R]:**
- **Scarlett Starling:** "look rested and polished". But these are $13–60 Amazon products, below the channel's luxury bar.
- **Diane de Chambray:** "one balm, less makeup, less is more" fits the French story. The products are sold as Korean, so never call them French.

## 5. Selling them legally [M]

- **Say the disclosure out loud in the video**, at the first mention, not only in the description: e.g. "the links are affiliate links; I earn a small commission". "Paid link" is adequate wording; "commissionable link" is not.
- If Luxe gave you anything, say so, and tick YouTube's "Paid promotion" box.
- **Amazon:**
  - Put "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" in the description.
  - **Don't speak or show prices**: Amazon only allows prices shown through its own links or data feed.
  - **Don't show Amazon star ratings or reviews** except through Amazon's tools.
  - The Beauty commission is **3%**, so the $13 serum earns about **$0.39 a sale**.
  - Luxe's own affiliate programme pays 15–30%, but it sends viewers to the shops the complaints are about.
- **Fake reviews:** the FTC's rule (in force since 21 Oct 2024) bans fake or misleading testimonials, at up to $53,088 per violation. **Never quote the brand's testimonials.** The FTC also counts **older adults** as the "ordinary consumers" a disclosure must be clear to.
- **Drug claims (FDA):** "grows lashes", "restores hair growth", "boosts collagen", "regenerates cells" and "fades pigment" turn a cosmetic into a drug. Say "looks fuller", "looks smoother", "moisturises" instead. Each report has a full never-say / say-instead table (`05` §4.6 and §8 of each product file).
- A ready-to-use spoken and written disclosure is in `05` §4.9.

## 6. Before you film: the to-do list [R]

1. Open the three Amazon pages yourself (mascara, pads, balm). Note the price, stars, review count, "bought in past month" and the top critical reviews. Confirm the pads' listing is from the same "LuxeCosmetics" seller.
2. Buy all four and photograph each box's ingredient list, above all the serum (to confirm "prostaglandin-free") and the mascara (none is published).
3. Ask Luxe in writing for the full ingredient lists, the "clinical testing" reports, and whether they're registered with the FDA under MoCRA.
4. Test on camera, on a 50+ face: the mascara over a full day on hooded lids plus warm-water removal; the balm under concealer, at day 1 and week 4.
5. Decide about the pads, given the Anua copy (section 2.4).
