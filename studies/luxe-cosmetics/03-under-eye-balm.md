## 0. The short version (for the operator)

1. **What it is.** A twist-up balm stick for under the eyes, sold by Luxe Cosmetics. The company behind it is Luxe Cosmetics B.V., Helmond, Netherlands; its returns go to a Dutch warehouse.
   - Brand-site price: **$39.95**, with a "Buy 1, Get 1 Free" banner.
   - Amazon price, rating and review count for B0GYK3T766: **not found**, because Amazon was not loaded.
   - Size: **not found**. Copycat listings are 0.38 oz.
2. **What's in it, by the brand's account.** Five "actives": **adenosine, niacinamide, bisabolol, vitamin E, hyaluronic acid**. The Amazon title and the current packaging say **"PDRN (collagen) extract"**.
   - It has **no retinol and no caffeine** (brand claim).
   - It has no bakuchiol, peptides or shea that I could find named anywhere.
   - **No full ingredient list was published anywhere I could reach.**
3. **When it launched [M/R].**
   - First product images went onto the brand store on **16 March 2026**.
   - The first independent write-up appeared on **8 April 2026**.
   - "Is it a scam?" YouTube videos appeared from **10 April 2026**.
   - The current store listing was re-created on **26 October 2026**.
   - The Amazon listing date is not found.
4. **The marketing has serious red flags you must not repeat on camera.** Full list in §8.
   - The page says "Rated 4.86 (10,839 reviews)", but the page's own review widget reports **0 reviews**.
   - The page's testimonials are dated **before** the product's first images existed.
   - It quotes two different "survey/study" sizes (2,431 vs 2,844).
   - It promises "send the tube back (even empty), get every dollar back", but the refund policy makes the buyer **pay return shipping to the Netherlands**.
   - The claim images show a **different product**: a blue "Lift & Firm Multi Balm, Wrinkle Core Stick". That is the name of a generic Chinese-made stick sold on eBay and Walmart.
5. **What a balm like this can honestly do.**
   - Moisturise.
   - Make fine lines and crepey texture look softer for a while.
   - Give concealer a smoother base.
   - Possibly give a modest improvement in fine lines over weeks: adenosine has one 2006 randomised trial in women aged 45 to 65.
   - **It cannot** remove fat-pad bags, fill hollows, fix genetic or vascular dark circles, or "fix collagen loss".
6. **The audience is primed to distrust exactly this kind of ad.** On the mature-skin creator videos, the most-liked comments attack:
   - young models selling to older women;
   - AI adverts;
   - "botox in a stick" claims.
   Honesty is the selling point (see §6.3).

## 2. Where it fits in a routine

- **Step:** the eye-area treatment and moisturiser step, after cleansing (and serum, if one is used) and before sunscreen and concealer. The brand pitches it as a replacement for the whole eye routine ("One Tube Replaces The Whole Routine") and as a primer under concealer [M].
- [R] Realistic placement:
  - **Morning:** a thin layer of balm, then sunscreen around (not on) the lash line, then concealer. Its main everyday value is as a smooth, hydrated base so concealer creases less.
  - **Night:** fine on its own. If a retinoid eye cream is used, alternate nights or put the balm on top as a buffer.
- [R] Stick-specific use: glide it on, then pat. Do not drag the stick across thin lower-lid skin. Keep it out of the waterline (see §7.5). Do not share the stick.

## 7. The under-eye category: causes, science, evidence, myths, safety

### 7.1 What causes under-eye problems, and what a topical can and can't do

| Cause | What it is | Topical can help? | What actually works | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pigment dark circles** (brown) | Extra melanin, which can be genetic, from sun, or post-inflammatory (rubbing, eczema) | **Somewhat, slowly**: brighteners (niacinamide, vitamin C, retinoids), plus daily SPF | Topicals and peels; lasers | Systematic review, Dermatol Surg 2021 (PMID 32740208): "Various topical creams and chemical peels are useful in treating pigment-based POH" [M] |
| **Vascular / thin-skin circles** (blue-purple) | Blood vessels and pooled blood showing through thin lower-lid skin | **Little.** Caffeine and vitamin K are claimed. One 12-week open-label trial (n=40, maker-run) reported less "microvasculature congestion" | Lasers "mildly to moderately beneficial" | PMID 32740208; Rajabi-Estarabadi 2024 (PMID 38112168, Revision Skincare-funded) [M] |
| **Shadow from hollows / tear trough** | Loss of volume and bone; the groove casts a shadow | **No.** At best a temporary blurring film | HA filler, fat grafting | PMID 32740208: "Soft tissue fillers and autologous fat grafting are most effective in treating dark circles due to volume loss"; practitioner survey: patients' concerns were hollows 91%, dark circles 80%, looking tired 60% (Aesthet Surg J 2024, PMID 38489829) [M] |
| **Mixed circles** | Pigment plus vascular | Partial | Combined | Korean study, 100 subjects: **vascular 35%, mixed 54%** (PMID 26346687) [M]. [R] That implies about 11% purely pigmented or other. |
| **Puffiness / fluid** (worse in the morning) | Fluid retention from salt, sleep, allergies, crying, lying flat | **Temporarily**: cold, caffeine, massage | Less salt and evening fluid, raise the head, cool compresses, treat allergies | Mayo Clinic "Bags under eyes" (WebSearch summary, 2026-10-31); Cleveland Clinic: creams with caffeine or retinol "offer only temporary relief" [M] |
| **Fat-pad bags** | Orbital fat pushes forward as the retaining tissue weakens, often genetic | **No** | Blepharoplasty; filler to blend the edge | Cleveland Clinic: genetic bags from "fat pad prominence, bone structure or skin laxity ... can be difficult to fully correct without medical intervention" [M] |
| **Crepey skin and fine lines** | Thin, dry, sun-damaged skin with less collagen and elastin | **Yes, partly**: moisturisers smooth at once; retinoids, adenosine and some peptides give modest smoothing over 4 to 12 weeks | Plus lasers and resurfacing | PMID 18489289 (adenosine); 19735523 (niacinamide + peptide eye cream improved Ra at 4 weeks, P&G); 38873621 (review of eye-cream actives) [M] |
| **Laxity / hooding** | Excess loose skin | **No** | Blepharoplasty | PMID 32740208: "Blepharoplasty surgery is best when excessive skin laxity is the underlying cause" [M] |
| **Milia** | Tiny keratin cysts, common on lids | **No** (heavy products are often blamed) | Extraction by a professional; lasers for "milia en plaque" | Case reports only pulled (PMID 24703259, 21250787). **I found no medical source in this pull proving rich eye creams cause milia** [R: plausible and widely said, but unsourced here] |

[R] **Plain-language summary for scripts.** There are four kinds of "tired eyes":

- Brown (pigment): a cream can help slowly.
- Blue (veins): a cream barely helps.
- Shadow (hollow): only filler helps.
- Bags (fat): only surgery helps.

Dry, crepey, fine-lined skin on top of any of these is the part a balm genuinely improves.

### 7.4 Myths to address in videos

| Myth | Reality [M source / R] |
|---|---|
| "Collagen cream puts collagen back" | It can't absorb as collagen (PMID 10839713). It moisturises. |
| "A balm can lift bags or replace filler / Botox" | Bags are fat or fluid and hollows are volume loss; only procedures change those (PMID 32740208). |
| "Eye cream fixes all dark circles" | Most circles are vascular or mixed (PMID 26346687); creams mainly help pigment. |
| "'Clinically tested' means it works" | A tested product is not a proven one. Luxe's "study" figures have no lab, no n for the wrinkle test and no control. |
| "Korean = better science" | Adenosine and niacinamide are standard global ingredients. This seller is a Dutch B.V. and the Korean origin is unverified. |
| "You need a separate eye cream" | [R] A gentle fragrance-free face moisturiser often does the same; eye products mainly differ in irritancy and texture. |

### 7.5 Safety

- **Keep it out of the eyes and the waterline.** A commenter with 662 likes: her eyes watered "all day long" until she stopped product 4 mm from the waterline (Doctorly comments).
- **Retinoids near the eyes** raise the odds of burning, dryness and pain (PMID 42708560). Luxe being retinol-free is a real plus for sensitive or dry-eye viewers. Medicube's multi balm contains retinol, and its INCI lists fragrant essential oils even though the page says it is "free from ... fragrance" (medicube.us, 2026-10-31) [M].
- **Allergy.** Without a full INCI, a viewer with known allergies cannot check this balm. Patch-test behind the ear or on the jaw first [R]. Eyelid skin reacts readily to cosmetic allergens (case reports: PMID 28612445, 11244140).
- **Hygiene.** A stick touches the skin directly. Don't share it, and wipe the tip [R; Bankson raises sanitation].
- **See a doctor when** puffiness is new, one-sided, painful, itchy or red, or comes with vision changes. Mayo lists thyroid eye disease and kidney disease among the causes of bags (WebSearch summary of mayoclinic.org, 2026-10-31).
- **Milia-prone viewers:** use a thin layer [R; unsourced, see §7.1].

### 8.4 Disclosure and review rules (for the operator's own videos)
