### 4.6 Cosmetic or drug: what can and can't be said (FDA)

**The law, from FDA's own pages:**
- A **drug** is an article "intended to affect the structure or any function of the body". A cosmetic is for "cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering the appearance". Intended use is shown by "claims on labels, in advertising, on the Internet, or in other promotional materials". Claims that a product will "restore hair growth, reduce cellulite" make it a drug. Source: "Is It a Cosmetic, a Drug, or Both?", https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap (content current as of 09/11/2024).
- A product meant to "remove wrinkles or increase the skin's production of collagen" is "a drug or a medical device". One that makes "lines and wrinkles less noticeable, simply by moisturizing the skin" is a cosmetic. Source: https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/wrinkle-treatments-and-other-anti-aging-products (content current as of 02/25/2022).
- Precedent: in 2011 the FDA sent a warning letter to Lifetech Resources over eyelash-growth claims for RapidLash and NeuLash, which contained isopropyl cloprostenate (cosmeticsandtoiletries.com / law360, search snippets). The only FDA-approved lash-growth drug is Latisse (bimatoprost), and the operator should not compare the serum to it.

### 4.7 MoCRA (Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022) basics

Source: FDA MoCRA page, https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra (content current as of 10/29/2026), plus law-firm and EcoMundo summaries via search.

- **Facility registration** with the FDA, renewed every two years. **Product listing** of every cosmetic and its ingredients, updated yearly. The **responsible person** is the manufacturer, packer or distributor named on the label.
- **Serious adverse events** must be reported to the FDA "within 15 business days".
- **Safety substantiation** records are required. "Animal testing is not a requirement".
- The FDA now has **mandatory recall** authority. It issued final guidance on mandatory cosmetic recalls on **29 October 2026**.
- **Small-business exemptions do not cover products that contact the eye's mucous membrane.** *Reading:* mascara and lash serum used at the lash line fall in that group, so Luxe gets no exemption for them.
- **Still pending:** fragrance-allergen labelling and GMP rules are proposals, not yet obligations (Crowell, Haynes Boone, Informa summaries). Whether Luxe has registered and listed is **not found**: the FDA's listing database is not public.

### 4.8 Safety lines to say on air (reading, based on FDA eye-cosmetic guidance)

From https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/eye-cosmetic-safety (content current as of 06/28/2022): "Manufacturers usually recommend discarding mascara two to four months after purchase." "Never apply or remove eye cosmetics in a moving vehicle." "Avoid using eye cosmetics if you have an eye infection or the skin around the eye is inflamed." "Don't share your cosmetics."

Suggested spoken lines:
1. "Patch-test first, and stop if your eyes sting or turn red."
2. "If you've had eye surgery (cataracts, for example) or you're being treated for an eye condition, ask your eye doctor before using any lash serum." *(The audience raised cataract surgery directly.)*
3. "Keep the serum on the lash line, not in the eye."
4. "Replace mascara every few months."
5. If file 04 finds a prostaglandin analogue in the serum: name it, and say plainly that eye doctors link it to darkening of the eyelid skin and, rarely, a change in eye colour.
