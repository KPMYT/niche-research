## 0. The short version

1. **No prostaglandin. It is a peptide, biotin and ginseng serum.** The brand's own ingredient list is: Purified Water (Aqua), Glycerol, Panax Ginseng Extract, Vitamin B7 (Biotin), Vitamin E (Tocopherol), Arginine/Lysine Polypeptide, 1,2-Hexanediol, Tripeptide-1, Octyl Hydroxamic Acid, Myristoyl Pentapeptide-4. The US product page says "Luxe is 100% prostaglandin-free". Source: luxe.com product pages (measured). The list printed on the Amazon box could not be read in any video, so it has not been checked against the website list.
2. **What that means:** it should not cause the prostaglandin side effects (iris darkening, eyelid darkening, fat loss around the eye). It also should not be expected to "grow" lashes the way Latisse does. For peptide, biotin and botanical serums there is "a lack of formal evidence" (J Cosmet Dermatol 2024 review). An ophthalmologist on YouTube said: "you're not going to see the tremendous growth that you do with the prostaglandins" (Dr Rupa Wong, 180K views).
3. **Amazon (measured, from the saved page):** $13.00, against a typical price of $15.99. Rated 4.0 stars from 495 ratings. Amazon's Choice. "1K+ bought in past month". Works out at **$4.33 per mL**, among the cheapest per mL in this category. The brand's own site charges $29.95 for the same 3 mL.
4. **Who makes it:** the seller of record on luxe.com is Luxe Cosmetics B.V., Helmond, Netherlands (KvK 89968336), with a German phone number. The footer says "© Luxe Cosmetics LLC". The brand mainly sells at-home lash-lift kits. It is the same brand as products #3 (Korean Under Eye Balm) and #1 (the "Conditioning Peptide Mascara"), both of which are in its catalogue.
5. **Very little independent feedback on this product.** The two YouTube reviews were split. A woman aged about 45 to 55 who has used Latisse for 10 years said: "it burned every time I put it on… I wouldn't spend my money on this one" (47 views, 2026-09-15). Ashley Barfuss said: "I don't have any stinging in my eyes… gentle enough for somebody that has sensitive eyes" (701 views, 2026-03-30, Amazon affiliate). Amazon review text could not be loaded.
6. **Problems with the brand's marketing:** its customer counts contradict each other (97,000+, 197,000+ and 1,197,000+). The page claims "Zero side effects", "Clinically proven" and "70% more length by week 12" without citing any study. It runs a fake-urgency banner ("LIVE: 2,847 orders yesterday | Low inventory"). It says it contains "no preservatives" while listing two preservative-type ingredients. The German page says it is safe "for cancer patients". The Amazon bullets say it will "stimulate natural hair growth" and "reduce hair loss". Under the FDA's intended-use test, wording like that is a **drug claim**. Brand-wide Trustpilot reviews (snippets) complain about refunds and unanswered emails.
7. **The category's biggest fear is side effects, not price.** The most-viewed lash-serum videos are doctors' warnings: Dr Sam Ellis 539K views, Dr Dray 401K, Dr Dray 183K, Dr Anil Rajani 112K. Several top rivals contain a prostaglandin analogue on their own ingredient lists: **GrandeLASH-MD, Babe Essential and neuLASH** (isopropyl cloprostenate) and **RevitaLash Advanced** (DDDE). The EU's safety committee published a final opinion on 10 Feb 2026 on these substances in lash products.
8. **Fit for women over 50:** being prostaglandin-free is a real selling point for this audience. Older eyes are more likely to be hollow, hooded, on glaucoma drops or after cataract surgery, which are exactly the groups the Latisse label warns about. The honest pitch is "a gentle conditioning serum that helps lashes look fuller, used for 8 to 12 weeks", not "regrow your lashes".

### 1.4 How to use (brand)

luxe.com:
1. Remove all eye makeup with an oil-free cleanser or micellar water.
2. Dip the wand once. That is enough for both eyes.
3. Paint a thin line along the upper lash line, "like you're applying liquid eyeliner".
4. Let it dry for 1 to 2 minutes, then go to sleep.

Use once a night. "Don't apply to lower lashes." "If you miss a day… don't double up." Full results take 8 to 12 weeks. The German page instead says once or twice a day, twice a day for the first 2 months.

Note from a buyer: the Amazon box "does not come with instructions" (Ashley Barfuss, YouTube JW3zXgn1hgU, 2026-03-30).

### Women over 50 and 60 (Scarlett Starling and Diane de Chambray audience)

- **Lashes do thin with age.** "Eyebrow and eyelash thinning and whitening can occur as a presentation of physiologic aging" (Am J Clin Dermatol 2023 review, PMC9870835). "Reduction in the length, thickness, and pigmentation of eyelashes and eyebrows can be a physiological phenomenon due to aging" (Dermatol Ther 2023, PMC10264316).
- **They are already looking for help.** "Lashes over 60 - full and beautiful?" by Barbara Formann, a German channel, had 116,582 views in 5 days (NexLev, 2026-10-31). A 66-year-old commenter there wrote, in German: "lash serum… worked noticeably better and at times my lashes were really much thicker and longer again. But you have to apply it daily and that's a bit tedious" (my translation, @susannemirjan3959, 2026-10-27). A woman over 70 wrote: "My hands aren't as steady any more; applying mascara is a challenge for me" (my translation, @brigitteschadeberg1584).
- **Prostaglandin-free matters more at this age:**
  - Hollow, hooded upper lids are common, and prostaglandin fat loss deepens them: "I had very heavy hooded eyes and lost all the fat in my upper lid so became hollow with tons of loose skin" (@staceyo1723, 33 likes).
  - People on glaucoma drops (often older) already take a prostaglandin. The Latisse label warns that doubling up "may decrease the intraocular pressure lowering effect" (Latisse PI 5.1).
  - After cataract surgery: the label says use "with caution in aphakic patients, in pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or in patients with known risk factors for macular edema" (Latisse PI 5.6).
  - None of this applies to a prostaglandin-free serum. That is the honest advantage of the Luxe formula for this audience (reading).
- **The older-age voice is real on both sides.** Some long-term prostaglandin users over 55 report no problems: "I'm 61 and have been using Grande Lash for 3 years. I have had NO PROBLEMS" (@loreej2479, 307 likes). "I've been using Latisse for 17 years… I started using it in my late fifties" (@carolynhunt7333, 45 likes). Older viewers also blame aging on serums: "This explains my eye aging, redness and irritation" (@deniseshore9637, 142 likes).
- **Application:** a liner-style brush along the upper lash line needs a steady hand and good near vision. Reading: show it close-up on camera.

## 7. The category as a whole

### 7.1 What a lash serum is

A liquid painted along the upper lash line at night. There are two families:

| | Prostaglandin-analogue serums | Peptide / vitamin / botanical serums |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Latisse (bimatoprost, prescription); GrandeLASH-MD, Babe Essential, neuLASH (isopropyl cloprostenate); RevitaLash Advanced (DDDE) | Luxe, The Ordinary, Vegamour, Lashfood, GrandeLASH Peptide+, Babe Renewing, VieBeauti |
| How it works | Prostaglandin F2α analogues (originally glaucoma drugs) "extend the anagen phase and… induce telogen follicles into anagen", meaning more lashes in the growing phase for longer (PMC10264316) | Proposed: conditioning, less breakage, keeps lashes on the lid longer. Peptides "give strength to the lashes but not shown in scientific studies… as to an actually enhancing lash growth" (Rupa Wong) |
| Evidence | Strong for bimatoprost (an FDA trial, below). Other analogues "show potential benefits, but further studies are encouraged" (J Cosmet Dermatol 2024, PMID 38475901) | "promising data, more studies are needed due to a lack of formal evidence" (same review) |
| Main risks | Iris and eyelid darkening, fat loss around the eye, redness, itch, dry eye, stray hairs (see 7.4) | Irritation or allergy. Oil-based ones can clog lid glands and cause styes (Rupa Wong) |
| Legal status (US) | Bimatoprost is a prescription drug. The others are sold as "cosmetics" (see 7.5) | Cosmetic |

### 7.2 The lash cycle in plain words (measured, PMC9870835)

- A lash lives about **4 to 11 months** in total.
- It grows about **0.12 to 0.14 mm a day** while in the growth phase, which lasts only **about 4 to 10 weeks**.
- Then comes a roughly 15-day transition, then a **4 to 9 month resting phase** before it falls out.
- "It is believed that eyebrow and eyelash length is limited by the short anagen phase."
- Reading, for scripts: lashes are short because they only grow for a few weeks. Any serum needs at least 1 to 3 months to show anything, because you are waiting for new lashes to come through.

### 7.3 Why lashes thin, especially after 50

| Cause | What the source says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Normal aging | "thinning and whitening can occur as a presentation of physiologic aging"; "reduction in the length, thickness, and pigmentation" | PMC9870835; PMC10264316 |
| Menopause | No peer-reviewed numbers found for lashes. The reviews mention post-menopausal frontal fibrosing alopecia (brow loss). Viewers report it: "they all fell out during the menopause transition" (brows) | PMC9870835 references; YouTube MC4gZn2xjWc |
| Thyroid | "Hypothyroidism can present with loss of the lateral third of the eyebrow… Severe hypothyroidism has also been reported to present with eyelash alopecia" | PMC9870835 |
| Chemotherapy | Taxanes, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide cause loss "approximately 1 week to 1 month after initiation"; "Eyebrow and eyelash hair loss has been reported to be permanent in approximately 5% of cases"; older patients may regrow more slowly | PMC9870835 |
| Blepharitis or Demodex mites (lid inflammation) | Blocked oil glands "can actually cause something called madarosis which is lash loss"; staphylococcal blepharitis can destroy follicles | Rupa Wong 9ehrWmpxQZQ; PMC9870835 (snippet) |
| Lash extensions | "traction alopecia" (natural lashes lost to the pull of synthetic ones), keratoconjunctivitis, allergic blepharitis | eyewiki.aao.org/Eyelash_Extensions (snippet); 16.0% of 400 Nigerian students using extensions noticed side effects (PMC10895549, snippet) |
| Lash lifts and tints | "avoid things like lash tints lash lifts and extensions those are all going to cause lash drop out eventually" | Rupa Wong 9ehrWmpxQZQ (opinion, not a study) |
| Trichotillomania (pulling) | "repetitive hair pulling or plucking"; often only upper or only lower lashes | PMC10264316; PMC9870835 |
| Cataract or other eye surgery | Not found as a cause of lash loss. It matters for *prostaglandin* serums (macular edema caution, Latisse PI 5.6) | Latisse PI |
| Alopecia areata, nutrition, infections, tumours | All listed causes of madarosis. "identifying the underlying condition… is the mainstay of the correct management" | PMC10264316; PMC9870835 |

Reading: sudden, patchy or one-eye lash loss is a "see a doctor" sign. No serum fixes it.

### 7.4 Latisse: the evidence and the warnings (measured, Latisse prescribing information, rxabbvie.com/pdf/latisse_pi.pdf, "Revised: 7/2024")

The trial: 278 adults, four months, Latisse against a dummy solution (vehicle). Share of patients whose overall lash prominence improved by at least one grade:

| Week | Latisse | Vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 15% | 8% |
| 8 | 50% | 15% |
| 12 | 69% | 20% |
| 16 | **78%** | **18%** |
| 20 | 79% | 21% |

At week 16: length +25% (vehicle +2%); fullness/thickness +106% (vehicle +12%); darkness +18% (vehicle +3%).

Other label points:
- **When you stop:** "Upon discontinuation of treatment, eyelash growth is expected to return to its pre-treatment level."
- **More is not better:** "Additional applications of LATISSE® will not increase the growth of eyelashes."
- **Common side effects:** eye itching, eye redness, skin darkening, irritation, dry eye and redness around the eye, each in "less than 4% of patients".
- **Iris:** "Iris pigmentation is likely to be permanent." It may take "several months to years" to show.
- **Eyelids:** darkening is "reported to be reversible upon discontinuation… in most patients".
- **After approval** (voluntary reports): "periorbital fat atrophy and skin tightness resulting in deepening of eyelid sulcus and eyelid ptosis", hordeolum (styes), loss or breakage of lashes, trichiasis (lashes growing toward the eye), blurred vision.
- **Glaucoma interaction:** can reduce the effect of glaucoma prostaglandin drops (5.1).
- **Cataract surgery:** caution about macular edema in aphakic or pseudophakic patients with a torn capsule (5.6).
- **Contact lenses:** remove before applying; contains benzalkonium chloride (5.8).
- The FDA approval year (2008) is background knowledge and was not re-verified in this pull.

### 7.5 What regulators say

- **US FDA, cosmetic or drug.** "Whether a product is a cosmetic or a drug under the law is determined by a product's intended use." Claims that a product will "restore hair growth" are listed as examples of claims that "establish the product as a drug" (fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap, measured). Reading: Luxe's Amazon bullet ("RESTORE NATURAL GROWTH… stimulate natural hair growth… reduce hair loss") is drug-type wording. Safer cosmetic wording is "longer-, fuller-*looking* lashes".
- **FDA enforcement history** (snippets):
  - In 2007 the FDA seized Age Intervention Eyelash (Jan Marini) as an unapproved drug and adulterated cosmetic because it contained bimatoprost, warning about optic-nerve risk with glaucoma drugs (drugs.com/fda-alerts/382-0.html; healio.com).
  - The FDA issued a warning letter to Lifetech Resources over growth claims for RapidLash and NeuLash (cosmeticsandtoiletries.com).
  - In 2009 it sent a letter to Allergan for minimising Latisse's risks on its website (hbw.citeline.com).
- **No pre-approval.** "Current United States law also requires no efficacy or safety assessments by the Food and Drug Administration before approving products for consumer use" (J Cosmet Dermatol 2024, PMID 38475901).
- **EU:** the Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety published a **Final Opinion on 10 February 2026** on MDN, isopropyl cloprostenate and DDDE (health.ec.europa.eu, measured). According to search summaries (snippets: cirs-group.com, chemlinked.com), it concluded that none "can be considered safe for use in cosmetic products intended for promoting the growth of eyelashes and eyebrows". A legal EU ban or restriction adopted after that: not found.

### 7.6 Hidden prostaglandins (why "prostaglandin-free" claims need checking)

- **Japan, 2022:** 64 eyelash serums bought online were lab-tested. 4 contained bimatoprost, **3 of them not declared on the label**. 8 more contained other prostaglandin analogues (J Pharm Biomed Anal 2022, PMID 35882176).
- **France, 2026:** of 79 lash-lengthening cosmetics, "About 30% of them contain a prostaglandin analogue". DDDE and isopropyl cloprostenate were the most common (Ann Pharm Fr 2026, PMID 42269767).
- **UK oculoplastic surgeons, 2022:**
  - A 35-year-old developed "thin, wrinkled, darker skin, and periorbital hollowing after 10 months" of Lash Boost (isopropyl cloprostenate), which "reversed 6 months after discontinuation".
  - Blinded graders scored makers' own before-and-after photos for these changes: 8.2 out of 19 for prostaglandin serums against 2.3 for non-prostaglandin ones (P < 0.001).
  - "Often the customer-facing product ingredient list contains no mention of PGAs." (Aesthet Surg J 2022, PMID 35700523.)
- **Spain, 2026:** 19 women used a DDDE serum for 6 weeks. Tear-film stability fell, tear volume fell, and eye redness rose (Eye Contact Lens 2026, PMID 42578722).
- **Germany, 2017:** a woman developed discolouration around the eye from an isopropyl cloprostenate serum, which "gradually remitted after the discontinuation of use" (J Cosmet Dermatol 2017, PMID 27595988).

## 8. Selling it honestly on YouTube

This section is my reading of the sources above. It is not legal advice.

### 8.1 Claims that are supportable

- "A prostaglandin-free lash serum." The brand's list contains none. Check the list on your own box first.
- "Peptides, biotin, ginseng and vitamin E to condition lashes."
- "Helps lashes look longer and fuller" or "longer-looking lashes". This is cosmetic, appearance-based wording.
- "Vegan and cruelty-free, according to the brand."
- "Give it 8 to 12 weeks. Lashes grow in cycles" (PMC9870835 cycle data; the brand's own FAQ).
- "It's not Latisse. If you want maximum growth, that's a prescription drug with known side effects. This is the gentle option."
- Price facts with the date: "$13 on Amazon when I checked".

### 8.2 Claims to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Grows lashes", "stimulates growth", "restores natural growth", "stops lash loss" (even though Amazon's own bullets say this) | FDA: "restore hair growth" claims make a product a drug (fda.gov) |
| "Clinically proven", "70% longer lashes", "results in 4 weeks guaranteed" | No study cited anywhere. Not found |
| "Zero side effects", "safe for everyone", "safe for cancer patients", "safe for contact lens wearers" | Brand claims with no evidence. One of two independent reviewers had burning |
| "Treats lash disease", chemotherapy or alopecia | Drug claim. Lash loss from disease needs a doctor (PMC10264316) |
| "Same results as Latisse" | No comparison study. Latisse's trial is not transferable |
| "100% natural" | It contains synthetic peptides and 1,2-hexanediol |
| "Permanent results" | Not supported. Latisse results disappear after stopping |
| Brand counts ("97,000+ women", "4.8 stars") | The brand's own pages contradict themselves (97,000 / 197,000 / 1,197,000; 4.8 / 4.97 / 5.0 from 49 / 6 reviews). Amazon shows 4.0 from 495 |

Background, not pulled in this round: US affiliate and sponsorship disclosure rules apply to paid product mentions.

### 8.3 Safety lines worth saying on camera

- Patch test first. Stop if it burns, stings or reddens. Rinse if it gets in the eye (brand: "avoid getting the formula into the eyes… rinse thoroughly with water").
- Apply only to the upper lash line, with makeup and contact lenses removed.
- If you are being treated for an eye condition, use glaucoma drops, or have had recent eye surgery: ask your eye doctor first. The brand says to use it only under a doctor's supervision if you are being treated for an eye condition (luxecosmetics.de FAQ).
- Pregnant or breastfeeding: ask your doctor (brand FAQ).
- Sudden, patchy or one-eye lash loss: see a doctor. It can signal thyroid disease, alopecia areata, blepharitis or mites (PMC9870835).

### 8.4 Who should not use it

- Anyone allergic to any listed ingredient.
- Anyone with an active eye infection, stye or inflamed lids.
- Anyone who had burning on a first try. The Ageless reviewer kept going for 5 nights and it "didn't start to taper down".
- Anyone looking for a medical treatment for disease-related lash loss (chemotherapy, alopecia, thyroid). Send them to a doctor.

### 8.5 Red flags in the reviews and the brand

1. The ingredient list on the Amazon box has not been checked against the website list. The website list leaves out two advertised "key" ingredients (rosemary, magnolia).
2. "No preservatives" is claimed while 1,2-hexanediol and octyl hydroxamic acid are listed.
3. Fake urgency on luxe.com: "⏰ LIVE: 2,847 orders yesterday | Low inventory", a countdown timer, "Free Lashlift Set included for the next 100 orders!", "Eyebrow Serum included for the next 11 orders!".
4. Guarantee terms are inconsistent (30 days / 60 nights / 90 days on one page), and Trustpilot snippets show refund disputes ("the best they would give me was 20%!").
5. The brand's own product video has a non-delivery "SCAM" comment. Buying through Amazon (ships from Amazon, free 30-day returns) avoids the website order issues.
6. The company presents itself as American ("from an American company", "Luxe Cosmetics LLC"), but its imprint shows a Dutch B.V. with a German phone number.
7. The only independent reviewer close to the target audience rejected it for burning. One case, but worth knowing before promoting it to sensitive older eyes.
