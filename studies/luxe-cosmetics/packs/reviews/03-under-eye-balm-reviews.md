## 0. What the reviews tell us (simple English)

I collected **253 reviews and comments**, word for word: 28 about the Luxe balm itself, 84 about rival balm sticks, 77 about eye creams and 64 about under-eye care in general. 28 of them come from people who say they are 50 or older. [M]

1. **There are very few real reviews of the Luxe balm yet.** I found 21 independent comments about it (YouTube and Trustpilot), plus 7 written testimonials on the brand's own two shops. No Amazon reviews could be read, and no rival-type review widget on the Luxe shops holds any reviews. [M]
   - Of the 21 independent comments, **1 says it worked** ("It did take care of the bags", #4), **4 say it did nothing**, and 4 compare it to Vaseline or lip balm ("a glorified Vaseline", "It feels like chapstick"). 10 are viewers who decided not to buy after a review video. 8 are about ordering, delivery or refunds rather than the balm. [M count, R coding]
   - The brand's testimonials are all 5 stars, from women aged 43 to 63, and tell the stories the brand is selling: puffiness "less visible" by day 10, concealer that stops creasing after two weeks, dark circles "visibly lighter" at 25 days. They are the brand's own text, not independent reviews. [M]
2. **The same formula sold elsewhere: not found.** No full ingredient list is published, so no twin product can be matched. [M]
3. **Rival balm sticks (Medicube, Kahi, Dr.Melaxin, the RoC under-eye stick) get the most useful real reviews** (84 comments). The same story repeats [R]:
   - It **feels nice and moisturises**, and it is handy (in the bag, on the lips, over makeup).
   - Its clearest everyday win is **as a base under concealer** (concealer: the skin-coloured makeup dabbed under the eyes to hide darkness). 13 stick comments mention makeup or concealer. "This eye balm has been a game changer for 59 year old me to be able to put Concealer on under my eyes without adding creases and wrinkles" (#107).
   - It **does not shift dark circles or bags** for most people: "I'm not convinced it's helping my dark under eyes - I agree it's a good base for my concealer" (#94). 13 stick comments say no change; 23 say it helped.
   - Many people **don't know how or when to use it** (16 questions: before or after sunscreen? fingers or stick? under the eyes too?).
4. **Eye creams show what does change the under-eye area, and how slowly.** Quick wins are cold and caffeine for morning puffiness ("Such a big difference within a few minutes!", #120). Slow wins take months: dark circles fading "after about 3 months" (#155) or "in the first six months" (#123), and smoother eyes after years of a retinal (vitamin A) cream (#153). [M quotes, R summary]
5. **Side effects worry this audience.** 30 comments mention burning, watery eyes, allergy or tiny white bumps (milia). Retinol near the eyes is the usual suspect ("my eyes were getting so dry", #194). Product touching the waterline (the wet inner rim of the eyelid) made one woman's eyes "water all day long" (#230). [M]
6. **Women over 50 talk about three things above all** [R, from 28 stated 50+ comments and the general threads]:
   - **Bags from fat pads that no cream fixed.** "no amount of topical products made a meaningful difference for the bags themselves ... surgery was the only thing that truly addressed the fat pads" (age 55+, #219). 8 comments say only a procedure works.
   - **Crepey lower lids and concealer that creases**, fought with sunscreen, sunglasses, a gentle cleanse and a greasy base ("Your Vaseline trick has been the biggest game-changer", #198).
   - **Anger at ads**: young or AI models selling to older women, and "botox in a stick" claims (45 comments distrust ads or hype; the top one has 1.5K likes).
7. **Value is judged against Vaseline.** It comes up again and again as the cheap yardstick ("a glorified Vaseline", "I will save $ and stick with Vaseline", "a more solid version of vaseline"). 42 comments talk about price or value. [M count]

## 1. Words you will see in these reviews

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Under-eye balm stick / multi balm | A solid moisturiser in a twist-up tube, like a big lip balm, that you rub straight onto the skin. Korean brands (Kahi, Medicube, Dr.Melaxin, d'Alba) made them popular. |
| Eye cream / eye serum | A small pot or tube of cream (thicker) or serum (thinner, watery) made for the skin around the eyes. |
| Concealer | Skin-coloured makeup dabbed under the eyes to hide darkness. It "creases" when it collects in the fine lines. |
| Crepey | Thin, finely wrinkled skin that looks like crepe paper. Common under the eyes after 50. |
| Dark circles | Darker skin under the eyes: brown (extra pigment), blue-purple (veins showing through thin skin) or shadow (from a hollow). |
| Puffiness / bags | Swelling under the eyes. Morning puffiness is mostly fluid and goes down. "Fat pads" are fat that pushes forward with age; creams don't move it. |
| Hollows / tear trough | A dip or groove under the eye that casts a shadow. |
| Milia | Tiny hard white bumps, often around the eyes. Rich creams are often blamed. |
| Waterline | The wet inner rim of the eyelid, next to the eyeball. Product there can sting or make the eyes water. |
| Retinol / retinal / tretinoin | Forms of vitamin A. They smooth lines over months but can dry, sting or redden thin eye skin. Tretinoin is prescription-only. |
| Caffeine (eye serum) | Narrows small blood vessels for a while, so puffiness and darkness look less for some hours. |
| PDRN | Short pieces of DNA (often from salmon) used in Korean skincare; the Luxe pack names it. |
| SPF / sunscreen | Protection from the sun, which is a main cause of fine lines. |
| Blepharoplasty ("bleph") | Eyelid surgery that removes or moves skin and fat. "Lower bleph" = under-eye bags. |
| Holy grail, repurchase, dupe | Beauty slang: a favourite product; buying it again; a cheaper copy of a dearer product. |
| K-drama | Korean TV drama; balm sticks were advertised inside these shows. |
