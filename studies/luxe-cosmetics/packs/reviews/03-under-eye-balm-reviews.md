**How to read this**

- **[M]** = measured: copied from a page or a tool result, with source and date. **[R]** = my reading.
- Every quote is copied word for word, spelling mistakes included. "..." marks where a long comment was cut. " / " marks a line break in the original. Emojis are kept.
- **Dates** on YouTube comments are NexLev's estimate from "x months ago", so they are approximate (~). Shop reviews carry the date the shop shows.
- **Likes or stars.** For YouTube, the comment's thumbs-up count when pulled (2026-10-31). For shop reviews (iHerb, Vitacost, Lookfantastic), the star rating out of 5.
- **Age or skin type** is shown only when the person stated it. "—" means not stated.
- Each quote has a number and tags in square brackets. The tags are my coding [R]; the counts of tags are measured from that coding [M].
- Ten round-1 comments are no longer counted (listed in §4.6). They held nothing about the balm, or only ordering and refund matters, which the operator asked to leave out. Their numbers are not reused.

They tell the stories the brand is selling: puffiness "less visible" by day 10, concealer that stops creasing after two weeks, dark circles "visibly lighter" at 25 days. They are the brand's own text, not independent reviews. [M]
2. **The same formula sold elsewhere: not found.** No full ingredient list is published, so no twin product can be matched. [M]
3. **Rival balm sticks give the most useful real reviews (158).** The same story repeats, and round 2 makes it clearer [R]:
   - **It moisturises and feels nice.** It is handy in a bag, on the lips and over makeup. "Doesnt do anything except hydrate like vaseline would be" (#371); "This is a dupe for Aquaphor  Healing Balm Stick. Doesn't work for wrinkles or lines; but is moisturizing for skin." (#338).
   - **Its clearest everyday win is as a base under concealer** (concealer: the skin-coloured makeup dabbed under the eyes to hide darkness). 22 stick comments talk about makeup or concealer. "I put it on under my concealer around my eyes and i find my make up goes on smooth and no creases!" (#374).
   - **It is greasy or shiny for many people.** 17 stick comments talk about grease, oil or shine. Several say to use it at night.
   - **It does not shift dark circles or bags for most people.** 37 stick comments say it helped with something; 23 say no change.
   - **It runs out fast.** "It barely lasted a week." (#438, KAHI); "the stick runs out quickly" (#395, d'Alba).
   - **A warning from thin, pale skin:** "if you have very pale thin skin as mine , I would not recommend to use it under eyes area, it just to heavy consistency for thin under eye area. It might become swollen and give unneeded eye bags." (#447, KAHI Eye Balm).
   - Many people **don't know how or when to use it** (19 stick questions: before or after sunscreen? fingers or stick? how long to leave it on?).
4. **Eye creams show what does change the under-eye area, and how slowly.** Quick wins are cold and caffeine for morning puffiness ("Such a big difference within a few minutes!", #120). Slow wins take months: dark circles fading "over a period of about two months" and crow's feet better "After about 4 months" (#405), or "after about 3 months" (#155). [M quotes, R summary]
5. **Side effects worry this audience.** 67 comments mention burning, watery eyes, allergy, twitching or tiny white bumps (milia). Retinol near the eyes is the usual suspect ("my eyes were getting so dry", #187). A rich eye cream "caused milia" (#424). A caffeine serum was followed by weeks of eye twitching (#413). Product touching the waterline (the wet inner rim of the eyelid) made one woman's eyes "water all day long" (#231). [M]
6. **Women over 50 talk about four things above all** [R, from the 40 stated 50+ comments and the over-50 video threads]:
   - **Bags from fat pads that no cream fixed.** "no amount of topical products made a meaningful difference for the bags themselves ... surgery was the only thing that truly addressed the fat pads" (age 55+, #219). "I had a lower lid blepharoplasty done at age 66 and never regretted it" (#259). 14 comments say only a procedure works.
   - **Concealer that creases in fine lines.** "I am 65, and no matter what I do, the fine and so fine lines show up." (#301). They fight it with a greasy base, less concealer and little powder ("Saw another influencer recommend tiny bit of Vaseline under eye ... Never thought it would work but it did!", #296).
   - **Thin, dry, crepey skin**, helped a little by rich, fragrance-free creams. "I am 68 and have noticed how much more it moisturizes ... it does soften my fine lines nicely" (#281).
   - **Anger at ads**: young or AI models selling to older women, and "botox in a stick" claims. 55 comments distrust ads or hype; the top one has 1.5K likes. "I’ll believe It when I see A WOMAN OF AGE try it" (#340).

## 1. Words you will see in these reviews

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Under-eye balm stick / multi balm | A solid moisturiser in a twist-up tube, like a big lip balm, that you rub straight onto the skin. Korean brands (KAHI, Medicube, Dr.Melaxin, d'Alba) made them popular. |
| Eye cream / eye serum / eye gel | A small pot or tube made for the skin around the eyes. Cream is thicker; serum is thinner and watery; gel is a cool jelly. |
| Concealer | Skin-coloured makeup dabbed under the eyes to hide darkness. It "creases" when it collects in fine lines, and looks "cakey" when it sits thick and dry. |
| Colour corrector | A peach, orange or pink makeup put on before concealer. It cancels blue or purple darkness. |
| Brightener | A sheer, light-reflecting cream that makes the under-eye look lighter without thick cover. |
| Primer / eye base | A thin layer put on before makeup so the makeup goes on smoother and stays put. |
| Setting powder / setting spray | A fine powder, or a mist, used to stop makeup moving. Too much powder under the eyes looks dry and lined. |
| Bronzer | A brown-toned powder or cream. One makeup trick puts it on a puffy bag to make the bulge look less raised. |
| CC cream | A light, tinted cream between a moisturiser and a foundation. |
| Crepey | Thin, finely wrinkled skin that looks like crepe paper. Common under the eyes after 50. |
| Crow's feet / smile lines / "11 lines" / smoker's lines / marionette lines | Lines at the outer corners of the eyes; lines from the nose to the mouth; the two upright lines between the eyebrows; fine lines above the top lip; lines running down from the corners of the mouth. |
| Dark circles | Darker skin under the eyes: brown (extra pigment), blue-purple (veins showing through thin skin) or shadow (from a hollow). |
| Puffiness / bags | Swelling under the eyes. Morning puffiness is mostly fluid and goes down. "Fat pads" are fat that pushes forward with age; creams don't move it. |
| Herniation | Fat pushing forward through the thin "wall" that normally holds it behind the eye. A doctor's word for fat-pad bags. |
| Hollows / tear trough | A dip or groove under the eye that casts a shadow. |
| Milia | Tiny hard white bumps, often around the eyes. Rich creams are often blamed. |
| Syringoma | A small, flat, harmless bump of a sweat gland, common under the eyes. |
| Waterline | The wet inner rim of the eyelid, next to the eyeball. Product there can sting or make the eyes water. |
| Eczema | A long-lasting skin condition: dry, itchy, red, sometimes cracked skin. On the eyelids it can bleed and swell. |
| Rosacea | A long-lasting skin condition with redness and flushing on the face; the skin stings easily. |
| Periocular dermatitis | A rash of small red bumps around the eyes, often set off by creams or steroid creams. |
| Contact dermatitis | A rash caused by something touching the skin. |
| Vitiligo | Patches of skin that lose their colour and turn white. |
| Perimenopause / menopause | The years before a woman's periods stop (perimenopause), and the time after they stop (menopause). Skin gets drier and thinner then. |
| Retinol / retinal (retinaldehyde) / tretinoin / adapalene | Forms of vitamin A. They smooth lines over months but can dry, sting or redden thin eye skin. Retinal is a stronger relative of retinol. Tretinoin is prescription-only. "Sandwiching" or "buffering" means putting moisturiser under and over it so it stings less. |
| Hyaluronic acid (HA) | A sugar-based molecule that holds a lot of water, so it plumps the top layer of skin for a while. |
| Niacinamide | Vitamin B3. It helps the skin barrier, redness and uneven brown pigment. |
| Peptides / Matrixyl | Short chains of protein building blocks used in anti-ageing creams. Matrixyl is one well-known peptide. |
| Ceramides | Fats that are a natural part of the skin's outer barrier; creams add them to help skin hold water. |
| Squalane / dimethicone / petrolatum | A light oil; a silky silicone; petroleum jelly (Vaseline). All three help skin hold water and feel smooth. |
| Centella | A plant extract (often called "cica") used to calm skin. |
| Caffeine (eye serum) | Narrows small blood vessels for a while, so puffiness and darkness look less for some hours. |
| EGCG | An antioxidant from green tea, in The Ordinary's caffeine serum. |
| PDRN | Short pieces of DNA (often from salmon) used in Korean skincare; the Luxe pack names it. |
| Volufiline | A plant-based ingredient sold as "plumping", named on Medicube's stick. |
| Collagen | The protein "scaffolding" that keeps skin firm. In a cream it only moisturises; it is too big to soak in. |
| SPF / sunscreen | Protection from the sun, which is a main cause of fine lines. |
| Botox | Injections that relax muscles so lines soften. Only a doctor or nurse can give them. |
| Filler | A gel (usually hyaluronic acid) injected to fill a hollow. |
| PRP / PRF | Platelet-rich plasma or fibrin: your own blood is spun and injected to improve skin. |
| IPL (intense pulsed light) | A light treatment in a clinic that can fade small visible veins and brown marks. |
| Blepharoplasty ("bleph") | Eyelid surgery that removes or moves skin and fat. "Lower bleph" = under-eye bags. |
| Upneeq | A prescription eye drop that lifts a droopy upper eyelid for some hours. |
| Lymphatic drainage | Gentle stroking massage meant to move fluid out of puffy areas. |
| Derm | Slang for dermatologist (skin doctor). Ophthalmologist = eye doctor. |
| T-zone | The forehead, nose and chin, which tend to be oilier. |
| Glass skin | A Korean beauty term for very smooth, dewy, shiny-looking skin. |
| Double cleanse | Washing the face twice: first with an oil or balm cleanser to melt makeup, then with a normal cleanser. |
| Holy grail (HG), repurchase, dupe | Beauty slang: a favourite product; buying it again; a cheaper copy of a dearer product. |
| K-drama | Korean TV drama; balm sticks were advertised inside these shows. |

- The Luxe sample is tiny (15 independent comments) and comes mostly from "is it a scam?" videos, so it leans negative. Don't treat it as a rating.
