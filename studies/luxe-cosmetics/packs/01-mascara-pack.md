## 1. The basics from zero

### 1.1 What mascara is, in one minute

- **Mascara** is a thick black (or brown) liquid that you brush onto your **eyelashes** (the short hairs that grow from the edge of the eyelid). It coats each hair, so the lashes look **darker, longer, thicker and more curled**.
- It comes in a small tube. The cap is attached to a thin stick with a little brush on the end. That brush is called the **wand**.
- Think of it as **paint for eyelash hairs**. A light-coloured, thin, short lash almost disappears against the skin. Painted black and made a little thicker, it shows up. That outlines the eye, so the eye looks bigger and more "awake".
- It is the most-used single piece of eye makeup. A professional makeup artist of 25 years: "if I only had one thing of makeup to take, what would you take? mascara" (Michelle Spieler, **Y3**) [M].
- It washes off. Nothing in a normal mascara changes the lash itself for good [R].

### 1.2 Eyelashes: what they are and how they grow

Before mascara makes sense, you need to know what it is painted on.

| Fact | Plain meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The upper lid has **90–160 lashes** in 5–6 rows; the lower lid has **75–80** in 3–4 rows. | The top row is the one mascara is mainly for. The bottom row is sparser and shorter. | [M] **S13** (Aumond & Bitton 2018, a review in *Journal of Optometry*) |
| A lash grows **0.12–0.14 mm a day**. | About 1 mm in a week. Much slower than you'd notice. | [M] **S13** |
| Each lash lives through 3 phases: **growing** (anagen), **shrinking** (catagen), **resting** (telogen). Then it falls out and a new one starts. | Like a queue: each lash grows, stops, rests, drops out, and is replaced. | [M] **S13** |
| The growing phase lasts **4–10 weeks**; one whole life cycle takes **4–11 months**. | Because growing is short, lashes stay short. Scalp hair grows for years; lashes for weeks. | [M] **S13** |
| A lash rarely gets longer than **12 mm**. | About half an inch. | [M] **S13** |
| Lashes are naturally **curved** in everyone. Curl is stronger on average in white people than in Asian people (the review says "Caucasians as compared to Asians"). | Why some women's lashes point straight out or down and need a curler. | [M] **S13** |
| Each lash root has little glands beside it (**Zeis** and **Moll** glands) that make an oily and a watery secretion. Behind the lash line sit about 50 **meibomian glands** on the top lid and 30 or so on the bottom, which make the oil layer of your tears. | The lash line is a busy, oily, wet place. That is why mascara can smudge there, and why gunk left there can upset the eye. | [M] **S13**; meibomian counts from ophthalmologist Dr Rupa Wong, **Y14**, **Y15** |
| Lashes are there to **protect the eye**. In a wind-tunnel study using model eyes (based on the eyes of many animals), the best lash length was about **one-third of the eye's width**: it halved the particles landing on the lashes and halved tear evaporation (tears drying off the eye). | Lashes are not only for looks. Damaging them matters. | [M] **S13** |

**What happens to lashes with age**

| Change | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shorter.** Average upper lash length was **7.98 mm** at ages 22–29 and **6.39 mm** at 50–65 (179 women). | That is about **20% shorter** [R, my sum: 6.39 ÷ 7.98 = 0.80]. | [M] **S12** (Glaser 2014; Allergan is among the authors' affiliations. Allergan makes **Latisse**, a prescription lash-growth drug whose active ingredient is **bimatoprost**, a prostaglandin; see the glossary and pack 4) |
| **Thinner.** Average thickness fell from **1.62** (ages 20–29) to **1.17** (50–65) in the study's units. | Each hair carries less colour and less bulk. | [M] **S12** |
| **"Darker or lighter" is unclear.** The study concludes lashes get lighter, but its own darkness numbers seem to point the other way. | So "lashes go lighter with age" is not proven. | [M] **S12**, as noted in `checks/01-mascara-check.md` |
| Lashes **turn grey only at a very advanced age and rarely go white**, unlike scalp hair. | Most women over 60 still have dark lashes; they are just fewer and finer. | [M] **S13** |
| The curve of the **lower** lashes near the nose and middle increases with age. | Lower lashes can point more downward or inward. | [M] **S13** (citing Procianoy) |
| Makeup artist Scott Barnes, as quoted in a video: lashes "get thinner and less dense as women get older because hormones shift and the follicles spend more time resting than growing". | A second-hand quote; no study given. | [M] quote in **Y2**; [R] treat as an expert's view, not proof |
| The skin around the eye thins and folds over: **hooded lids** (extra skin that hangs over the **crease**, the fold line between the eyelid and the brow area, and sometimes touches the lashes). | Lashes touch skin, so mascara prints onto the lid. | [M] Nina Ubhi, **Y7**: "your eyes are maybe becoming more and more hooded… you've got excess skin around that area. So, things are touching now" |
| Tears get less stable; eyes can be drier **and** water more. | Watery eyes break down some mascaras. | [M] **Y2** ("Tear production also drops, which sounds backwards, but means mature eyes water more"); Dr Rupa, **Y15** |
| Eyesight changes (reading glasses, **cataracts**: the clear lens inside the eye going cloudy, fixed by an operation) make close work harder. | People can't see their lashes well enough to apply. | [M] comment: "When I had my cataract surgery, I bought a 10X mirror so I could see up close." (@adriennekushner1968, **Y5**, 42 likes) |

**Can mascara damage lashes?**
- In 36 Japanese women aged 20–70, **the more often they wore mascara, the more the lash surface was cracked** (the **cuticle**, the thin outer layer of overlapping scales on every hair, like roof tiles) and the more porous (full of tiny holes) the inside of the lash was. **Using a lash curler made no significant difference.** [M] **S14** (Fukami 2014).
- The same review notes long-term mascara users can lose lashes, "possibly due to the rubbing" when removing it [M] **S13**.
- [R] So the main risk to lashes is **how mascara comes off**, not the colour sitting on them. That is why removal is such a big theme with older women.

### 1.3 How mascara works: the science in kitchen-table words

**What is in almost every mascara** [M] **S25**, **S13**, **Y8**, **Y11**:

| Part | What it is | Kitchen comparison | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pigment** | Usually black iron oxide (on labels: "Iron Oxides (CI 77499)"); sometimes a little blue (ultramarines) to make the black look deeper. Pigments are about 0.5–10% of a mascara. | Like the soot in shoe polish. | Gives the colour. |
| **Waxes** | Beeswax, carnauba (from palm leaves), paraffin and similar. | Like candle wax softened into a paste. | Give body. Thicken the lash. Help it hold its shape. |
| **Film formers** (polymers) | Plastic-like ingredients that dry into a thin, flexible skin. Usually 1–8% of the formula. | Like PVA school glue or hairspray drying into a clear film. | Hold the colour on, stop it moving, help hold curl. A cosmetic chemist: these are "borrowed from hairspray… it's like hair gel, it's holding the lashes where you want them" (Y8). |
| **Liquid base** | Water (most mascaras) **or** a fast-drying oil-like solvent such as **isododecane** (a clear, thin, oil-like liquid made from petroleum that evaporates within minutes) (waterproof mascaras). | Water vs. nail-polish thinner. | Keeps it runny in the tube, then evaporates on the lash. |
| **Emulsifiers** | Ingredients that let oil and water mix. | Like egg yolk in mayonnaise. | Hold water, waxes and oils together as a cream. |
| **Preservatives** | Ingredients that stop bacteria and mould. | Like the salt in jam. | Vital: a mascara wand goes from eye to tube every day. |
| **"Care" extras** | Oils, panthenol (vitamin B5), peptides, vitamin E. | The "added vitamins" on a cereal box. | Marketing appeal; may soften lashes. |

- "Mascaras are generally **emulsions**, meaning that they're mixtures of water and oil or lipid based substances like waxes" (cosmetic chemist Ramón Pagán, **Y8**) [M].
- An eye-care review: "Water-based mascara is made of several waxes, types of pigment and resins dissolved in water, whereas solvent-based mascara, known as waterproof, has its pigments and waxes added in petroleum distillates" [M] **S13**.

**How it's made** [M] **Y11** (Refinery29, inside Maybelline's Great Lash factory, 2.26M views):
1. Waxes and stiffening agents are melted so the mix isn't too runny.
2. Oils and pigment are added for colour and texture. Waterproof versions get a "water-rebuffing" ingredient.
3. Everything is **mixed and heated** until it becomes the dark paste.
4. The batch is **tested** (Maybelline uses a light-splitting machine, a spectrometer, to check the make-up of each batch).
5. Tubes stand upright on a conveyor; the mascara is **pumped in**.
6. A clear plastic plug called the **wiper** goes into the neck. It scrapes extra mascara off the wand as you pull it out, so the brush isn't overloaded.
7. The wand goes in, labels go on, more checks, then shipping.

[R] The wand and the wiper matter as much as the liquid. A chemist says brands hold **patents** on how wand bristles are spaced and shaped (**Y8**) [M].

### 1.4 History and trend: why people are buying it now

**History** [M] **S21** (sources disagree on some dates; disagreements shown)
| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Ancient times | People darkened eyes and lashes with soot-based mixtures for thousands of years. |
| 1800s | Eugène Rimmel sold a petroleum-jelly-based lash darkener. Dates given vary: 1830s, 1860, about 1880. In several languages "rimmel" still means mascara. Queen Victoria's perfumer is also said to have mixed coal dust and petroleum jelly (**Y11**). |
| 1915–1917 | Maybel Williams darkened her lashes with a similar mix; her brother T.L. Williams sold a version by mail. The company became **Maybelline** (Mabel + Vaseline). Most sources say 1917; the Refinery29 video says 1915. |
| 1957 or 1958 | Helena Rubinstein's **Mascara-Matic**: the first tube with a built-in wand. Most sources say 1957; Wikipedia says 1958. |
| 1971 | **Maybelline Great Lash** (pink and green tube), often called the first water-based mascara. Maybelline said in 2018 that one is sold every two seconds (**Y11**). |
| About 1995 | **Blinc** introduces the first **tubing** mascara (sold in Japan as "Kiss Me"). Some sources say 1999. |
| 2017 | Thrive Causemetics launches Liquid Lash Extensions (tubing), now a cult product with over-50 women. |
| 2018–2026 | Big brands pile into tubing: L'Oréal Double Extend (2018), Merit and Caliray (2021), Tarte Tartelette Tubing (2022), Too Faced and Urban Decay (2025), CoverGirl (Feb 2026). |

**Why now** [M] **S19** (Beauty Independent, a beauty-business news site, 6 Feb 2026). The article takes its figures from four companies: **Allied Market Research** (the $6.4bn) and **Market Intelo** (the $1.42bn), both market-research firms; **Spate** (the search figures), a company that tracks what people search for on Google; and **Quilt AI** (the 51%), a company that uses AI to read what people say online.
- Mascara sales were estimated at about **$6.4 billion in 2022**, projected to more than double by 2032. The tubing slice alone was estimated at about **$1.42 billion in 2024**.
- Google searches for "tubing mascara" rose **35.2%** year on year; from Oct 2024 to Sep 2025 searches rose **9.1%**.
- The top theme in what shoppers say online is **confusion** (Quilt AI): 51% of the conversation is basic questions like "tubing mascara what is". Tubing mascaras at Sephora (a large make-up shop chain) cost about **$14–$34**.
- In 159 comments on over-40/50/60 mascara videos, the top complaints were hard removal (19), smudging (14), price (11) and giving up mascara (10) [M] **S5**.

[R] Three forces meet: an ageing audience whose lashes and lids have changed, a newly popular formula type (tubing) that solves smudging and hard removal, and a "skincare in makeup" fashion (peptides, conditioning) borrowed from lash serums. Luxe's mascara is pitched at exactly that meeting point.

### 1.5 The types of mascara

**By formula (what's in the tube)**

| Type | How it works, plainly | Good for | Bad for | How it comes off | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular ("washable")** | A water-and-wax cream. Dries as a soft coat of coloured wax on the lash. | Volume, cheap, easy to layer. | Oily lids, rich eye creams, tears and heat soften the wax → smudges and flakes. | Soap and water, micellar water, cleansing oil. | Essence Lash Princess, L'Oréal Lash Paradise Washable, Maybelline Sky High Washable, Grande GrandeMASCARA |
| **Waterproof** | Little or no water. The wax and pigment sit in a fast-drying solvent; once it evaporates, what's left won't dissolve in water. "Waterproof mascaras are completely water-free, meaning they are anhydrous" (**Y8**). | Crying, swimming, humidity, holding a curl. | Removal needs an oil or two-phase remover and rubbing; can dry lashes; makeup artist: "really harsh on the lashes over time and very drying" (**Y3**). Still breaks down with very oily lids: "oil loves oil" (**Y8**). | Oil cleanser or bi-phase remover. | Bobbi Brown No Smudge, Lash Princess Waterproof, Sky High Waterproof |
| **Tubing** | Lots of water-based film formers. As it dries it wraps **each lash in a tiny plastic-like sleeve**. "Think of hollow spaghetti… putting little straws around your lashes because it uses a polymer blend" (makeup artist, **Y3**). | Oily or hooded lids, smudging, flaking, gentle removal, sensitive eyes. | Less volume; can break down with warm tears, humidity, sweat or eye drops; some versions dry out; some only allow 1–2 coats. | **Warm water** and gentle pressure: the sleeves slide off in little tubes. "It will not come off with… micellar water… it will come off with warm hot water" (**Y3**). | Thrive Liquid Lash Extensions, Tarte Tartelette Tubing, Clinique Lash Power, Blinc, L'Oréal Double Extend, Essence "Bye Bye Panda Eyes", Hourglass Unlocked |
| **Fibre** | Regular mascara with tiny nylon, rayon or bamboo threads that stick to the lash tips to add length. | Length on short lashes. | Fibres shed into the eye; trouble for contact lenses and sensitive eyes. Video: "Fiber formulas shed, and those loose fibers fall into the eye, where they can scratch the cornea" (**Y2**). (The **cornea** is the clear front window of the eye.) | Like regular, but watch for fibres in the eye. | MAC Magic Extension 5mm Fibre; Sky High says it has bamboo "fibers" (**Y6**) |
| **"Conditioning" / serum-infused** | Any of the above plus "care" ingredients: peptides, panthenol, oils, vitamins. | Appeal for people who worry about lash health. | No mascara-level study shows growth; the care ingredients are usually tiny amounts and wash off at night. | Depends on the base. | Grande GrandeMASCARA (regular base), Tarte Tartelette XL "with Peptide" (tubing base), **Luxe** (base not published) |
| **Primer / base coat** | A white or clear coat put on first. | More length; makeup artist saw longer lashes on the primed eye (**Y3**). | An extra step; can make clumps. | Like the mascara on top. | Lancôme Cils Booster, Grande Primer, L'Oréal Voluminous Superstar (two-ended) |

**By job (what it is trying to do)**
- **Lengthening**: thinner formula, comb-like brush; separated, longer lashes.
- **Volumising**: thicker formula, fuller brush; more bulk, risk of clumps.
- **Curling / lifting**: stiffer film and often a curved brush to hold lashes up.
- **Defining / separating**: clean, "natural" lashes, no clumps.
- [R] Most products claim several at once. What you actually get depends mostly on the **brush** and how much **wax and film former** is in the formula. A commenter: "I've always thought it's not the mascara, it's the brush!" (@BMc-hm2pb, 21 likes, **Y6** comments) [M].

**Waterproof vs water-resistant vs washable** [R]
- **Waterproof**: won't come off with water; needs an oily remover.
- **Water-resistant**: survives a bit of water and sweat; still comes off with warm water or normal cleanser. Thrive calls its tubing mascara "water-resistant" (**S7**) [M].
- **Washable**: a regular formula that comes off with soap and water.
- A product cannot sensibly be both **waterproof** and "comes off with warm water". The Luxe page says both (see 2.4).

### 1.6 Brushes and wands

| Brush | What it looks like | What it does | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fibre (bristle) brush** | Twisted wire with soft nylon bristles, like a tiny bottle brush. | Holds more formula; gives volume. | Fuller look; risk of clumps on sparse lashes. |
| **Silicone / moulded brush** | Rubber-like spikes or combs. | Separates and combs; holds less formula. | Length and separation; short lashes. Sky High's "Flex Tower" brush "bends" (**Y6**). |
| **Thin / slim / "precision" brush** | Very narrow. | Reaches tiny inner-corner lashes and lower lashes without painting the skin. | Short, sparse lashes; lower lashes; hooded eyes. |
| **Tapered tip** | Narrows to a point. | Picks up corner lashes. Dominique Sachse: "I love this pointed tip… great for just grabbing these outer corner lashes" (**Y6**). | Short lashes, corners. |
| **Curved brush** | Banana-shaped. | Follows the curve of the eye; helps hold lift. Chemist: "that curled shape basically is mirroring the curve of your eyeball and eyelashes" (**Y8**). | Straight lashes. |
| **Hourglass / "number eight"** | Fat at ends, thin in the middle. | Big volume. | Drama; not ideal on sparse lashes. |
| **Flat / paddle** | Flat on one side, combed on the other. | Flat side loads the root, comb separates (Charlotte Tilbury Pillow Talk Push Up, **Y1**; Telescopic, **Y3**). | Lift. |
| **Ball tip** | A tiny ball at the end. | Corners and lower lashes (**Y18**). | Lower lashes. |
| **Tubing brushes** | Usually short, evenly spaced bristles, straight and tapered. Chemist: "the bristles are short and they're very evenly spaced so that you can get basically around every individual lash" (**Y8**). | Lays the sleeve evenly. | — |

Extra tools:
- **Spoolie**: a clean, dry mascara-style brush used to comb out clumps.
- **Lash comb**: a fine metal or plastic comb that separates lashes after mascara (**Y18**). Never use a pin: "you could inadvertently stab your eye" (dermatologist Dr Dray, **Y12**).
- **Eyelash curler**: a metal clamp with a rubber pad that bends lashes upward. **Heated curler**: a warm wand used *after* mascara (makeup artist Aran P, **Y18**).

## 3. How to use it, step by step (and how to take it off)

Luxe's own instructions are only "2 coats. That's all it took" and "Warm water. That's it." [M] **S1**. Below is the full method, built from makeup artists, eye doctors and the tubing brands. Where advice differs, both sides are shown.

### 3.1 What you need

| Item | Why | Must-have? |
|---|---|---|
| The mascara | — | Yes |
| A mirror you can put **below** your face, ideally a magnifying one (5x–10x) | Looking down stops you hitting the lid; magnification helps tired eyes | Strongly advised over 50 |
| Good light from the front (a window) | Shadows hide clumps | Yes |
| Cotton buds (Q-tips) | Fixing smudges | Yes |
| Eyelash curler (optional) | Lifts straight lashes | Optional |
| Clean spoolie or lash comb (optional) | Separating clumps | Optional |
| Eyeshadow primer or loose powder (optional) | Stops oily or hooded lids smudging mascara | Optional, useful for hooded lids |

### 3.2 Step by step: applying it

1. **Start with clean, dry, cream-free lashes.**
   - Wash your face. Keep eye cream **below** the eye and away from the lashes, and let it sink in.
   - Why: oil or cream on lashes breaks mascara down and causes clumps and smudging. Nina Ubhi: "if you have like skin care left over on your lashes… it's actually oil, right? So, it's moisture and it's going to end up causing your mascara to slide off, flake, or break down" [M] **Y7**.
   - Dermatologist Dr Dray: wash lids before putting on new eye makeup, because "pollen dirt pollutants" settle there [M] **Y12**.
2. **(Hooded or oily lids) Prime or powder the lid.**
   - Put a tiny amount of eyeshadow primer on the lid, "especially right there, that's exactly where your lashes are going to touch", and wait about 30 seconds [M] **Y9**.
   - Or dust loose powder there. Commenter: "The key for preventing smudging (for me anyway) is to make sure the eye area is powdered" (@Pamela-B, **Y9** comments) [M].
3. **(Optional) Curl your lashes, BEFORE mascara.**
   - Why before: "curl your lashes gently before the mascara goes on, never after it dries because clamping a curler onto a coated lash is how brittle lashes break" [M] **Y2**.
   - How (classic): open the curler, slide it as close to the root as is comfortable without pinching skin, squeeze gently, then move out a little and squeeze again, then once more near the middle, so the lash curves like a C, not a sharp "check mark" [M] **Y3**, **Y9**.
   - How (tips-first, from makeup artist Erin Parsons via Michelle Spieler): curl the **tips** first, then the **middle**, then the **base** [M] **Y3**.
   - Pinching means the curler's curve doesn't match your eye; try another shape: "if you're constantly giving a little [flinch]… you don't have the right curve" [M] **Y9**.
   - Heated curler alternative: used **after** mascara, held on the lashes; no pinching (makeup artist Aran P, **Y18**) [M].
   - Note the warnings: Dr Dray says curlers often contain **nickel** (a common allergy) and can break lashes; clean them with an alcohol pad [M] **Y12**. A study of 36 women found curler use did **not** significantly change lash structure [M] **S14**.
4. **Take the wand out the right way. Do NOT pump it.**
   - Twist and pull out slowly. Wipe the extra off on the tube's neck, not on a tissue (saves product) [M] **Y18**.
   - "Don't pump it… rummage it around on the inside" [M] **Y9**. Pumping "pushes air in there into the barrel and it dries out the mascara so you're going to get clumps" [M] **Y18**. Thrive: "avoid pumping the wand in and out of the tube" [M] **S7**.
5. **Position: chin up, eyes down.**
   - Put the mirror below you and look down into it. "when you're looking down you're less likely to get it on your lids" [M] **Y9**. "tilt my head down so I'm looking up my nose" [M] **Y18**.
6. **First coat, upper lashes: wiggle at the root, then pull through.**
   - Put the brush at the very base of the lashes, wiggle side to side a little, then sweep up and out to the tips. Dominique Sachse: "Kind of the little wiggle. Little wiggle and then pull. And I feel like it just deposits a little bit more of the product at the base" [M] **Y6**.
   - Thrive: "Beginning at the base of your lashes, gently guide the wand from root to tip" [M] **S7**.
   - Use the **tip of the wand** held upright for the tiny inner- and outer-corner lashes [M] **Y10**, **Y6**.
   - A salon trick: brush the **tips** first with the wand tip, then do the root-to-tip pass, "you can never get the tips as long when you skip this step" (@DreamJeanneee1111, 56 likes, **Y17** comments) [M].
7. **Second coat: timing depends on the formula.**
   - **Tubing:** add the second coat **while the first is still wet**. Thrive: "Layer additional coats while lashes are still wet"; Tarte (per Dominique): "quickly apply the second coat before the first coat dries" [M] **S7**, **Y6**. A Thrive fan: "If it gets dry and then you add a coat it is more prone to clumping" (@CarolZone8A) [M].
   - Some tubing formulas are meant as one coat: makeup artist: "they are one coat only… anytime someone has gone in with the second or third coat they'll say oh it's really chunky" [M] **Y3**. Blinc's newer formula says you can build [M] **Y10**.
   - **Regular:** let it sit a few seconds, but not dry. Nina Ubhi: "Just let it sit a few seconds before you go in with the second coat… not even semi-dry" [M] **Y7**. Mindful Midlife: "don't ever let one coat completely dry" [M] **Y9**.
   - [R] Luxe says "2 coats". Do the second coat within a minute, as for tubing, until you know how it behaves.
8. **One eye at a time.** "For optimal performance, we recommend focusing on one eye at a time" [M] **S7**.
9. **Lower lashes: go light, or skip.**
   - Film and TV makeup artists won't put mascara on women's lower lashes, as it can "cast shadows and can end up making you look more hollowed out", especially over 40 [M] **Y3**.
   - "A light hand up top, almost nothing on the bottom" [M] **Y2**.
   - If you do them: wait until the top is dry, look up a little, and **dab** or use the wand **upright** (vertical) [M] **Y9**, **Y18**; "I use the mascara brush pointed up and down rather than sideways on bottom lashes" (@LisaIsHappyToBeSane, 119 likes) [M].
   - Tissue trick: hold a tissue under the lower lashes while doing the top (@granjmy, 82 likes) [M].
   - Some use a tubing mascara only on the lower lashes because it won't smear: "works great on the lower lashes as it doesn't smear, cause raccoon eyes, or fallout" (**fallout** = bits dropping off) (@hiannahgus574, 45 likes, **Y17c**) [M].
10. **Fix mistakes the safe way.**
    - Let a smudge on the lid **dry**, then flick it off with a dry cotton bud.
    - Comb clumps with a clean spoolie or lash comb. **Never a pin or safety pin**: Dr Dray, "you could inadvertently stab your eye" [M] **Y12**. (Some viral hacks use tweezers coated in mascara for lower lashes, **Y17**; [R] for shaky hands or poor sight, skip it.)
11. **Never apply in a moving car or bus.** FDA: a bump can scratch the eye with the wand, and "Even a slight scratch can result in a serious infection" [M] **S9**.

### 3.3 Step by step: taking it off

**Tubing (which is how Luxe describes itself)**
1. **Contact lens wearers: take lenses out first** ("Take lenses out before removing makeup") [M] **S23**.
2. Soak a cotton pad or washcloth in **warm** (not hot) water and hold it on closed eyes for about **30 seconds** [M] **S7**.
3. Gently press and slide **downward** along the lashes with the pad or fingertips. The little tubes slide off. Thrive: "slowly move in downward swiping motions" [M] **S7**.
4. Rinse the sink: "it leaves a mess in the sink… I'd rather have a mess in my sink than a mess all over my eyes" [M] **Y3**.
5. Then cleanse the face as normal.

**Warnings from users** [M]:
- Use warm, not cold: "sometimes i'm too lazy to use hot water so i'll use cold water and it pulls some of my eyelashes out" (@Madilyn-sh5pd, **Y21** comments).
- It doesn't always "noodle off" neatly: Dominique Sachse wiggled her lashes with dry fingers and got nothing; at the sink it took "face soap as well as a oil-free eye makeup remover" to get Tarte's tubing off [M] **Y6**.
- In a chemist's test, one product sold as tubing just turned to "mush" in water and smudged, with no tubes [M] **Y8**.

**Regular (washable) mascara**
- Cleansing oil or balm first, then a gentle cleanser. Dr Dray: "use an oil cleanser… the oil will just dissolve that makeup… and it'll slide right off" then follow with a gentle cleanser; ophthalmologists often suggest **baby shampoo** for the lid edge [M] **Y13**.
- Micellar water on a pad works too; ophthalmologist Dr Rupa Wong warns some micellar waters contain alcohol and that she once had "a horrible allergic dermatitis reaction" to one [M] **Y16**.
- Use the **ring finger** and little circles; finish with a cotton bud along the lid edge to catch bits that could fall into the eye [M] **Y16**, **Y13**.

**Waterproof mascara**
- Needs an oil or **bi-phase** remover. Hold a soaked pad on the closed eye for 10–20 seconds, then wipe down; never scrub [M] **Y14**; L'Oréal advice for its mascara, **S8**.
- Ophthalmologist: waterproof "usually requires a biphasic makeup remover and… a lot more [rubbing]… that can cause lash loss, it can cause wrinkles" [M] **Y14**.

**For everyone**
- **Take it off every night.** Dr Dray describes a published case of a woman who slept in mascara for about 25 years until bits of it were "embedded into the eyelid", causing painful bumps [M] **Y12**, **Y13**.
- Don't use face scrubs or acne washes (benzoyl peroxide, salicylic or glycolic acid) around the eyes [M] **Y13**.

## 4. Where it fits in a routine

### 4.1 The order of steps

**Morning (makeup)**
1. Cleanse.
2. Skincare: moisturiser; eye cream **under** the eye, not on the lashes.
3. Sunscreen.
4. Contact lenses in (**before** makeup) [M] **S23**.
5. Face makeup: foundation (skin-coloured liquid that evens out the skin) or tinted moisturiser (a lighter, moisturising version), then concealer (thicker cream to hide dark circles).
6. Eyes: lid primer (a thin base cream) or loose powder (hooded lids) → eyeshadow (colour on the lid; optional) → eyeliner (a line drawn along the lashes; optional; on the skin, not the waterline) → brows (filling in the eyebrows).
7. Curl lashes (optional).
8. **Mascara: the last eye step.** Luxe goes here.
9. Blush (colour on the cheeks), lips.

[R] Mascara usually goes last on the eyes so eyeshadow dust doesn't land on wet lashes and so you can clean the lid up after.

**Night**
1. Contact lenses out first [M] **S23**.
2. **Remove the mascara** (warm water for tubing; oil for regular/waterproof).
3. Cleanse the face.
4. Skincare.
5. **Lash serum, last, on a clean, dry upper lash line** (e.g. the Luxe Eyelash Serum, pack 4).

[M] A creator (Cassi, **Y20**) describes this exact split: "I use a conditioning treatment in the morning and I use a lash serum at night" (**Y20**).

### 4.2 What it goes with, and what not to mix

| Combination | Good or bad | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lash curler before | Good | Lift, then lock it with mascara |
| Eyeshadow primer or powder on the lid | Good | Stops lid oil breaking mascara down [M] **Y9** |
| Lash serum at night | Good | Different jobs; never at the same time [R] |
| Brown liner instead of black | Good for mature eyes | Softer; "I tend to like a dark chocolate compared to a black" [M] **Y19** |
| Rich eye cream or eye oil on or near lashes before mascara | Bad | Breaks mascara down; smudging [M] **Y7** |
| Castor oil on lashes in the morning | Bad | Castor oil is for night only: "you don't want to do that right before you put on your makeup because it is kind of thick" [M] **Y5** |
| A conditioning lash treatment then mascara on top | Often bad | Makes regular mascara clumpy; "Usually your choices are just don't wear mascara or don't use your conditioning treatment" [M] **Y20**; that is the problem Luxe claims to solve |
| Tubing mascara over waterproof, or a "refresher" over tubing | Bad | MAC's refresher "is not meant to go over waterproof… and it's not meant to go over tubing" [M] **Y3** |
| Regular mascara over a tubing base for an event | Works for some | "For an event I will apply a normal mascara over and/or under it for more oomph" [M] (@lorainemcgillivray77, **Y9** comments) |
| Lash extensions | Only with an oil-free formula | Oil loosens glue [M] **Y10**; Luxe says "Lash Extension Friendly" |
| Waterproof liner in the waterline | Avoid | Blocks oil glands [M] **Y14** |
| Fibre mascara with contact lenses | Avoid | Fibres get under lenses [M] **Y2**; snippet in **S5** |
| Prescription eye drops | Watch out | Drops and tears can break tubing: "when the tubing mascaras broke up" (@bethirwin7445) [M] |

### 4.3 Example routines

**A. The 3-minute "look awake" routine (any age)**
1. Clean, dry lashes.
2. One coat of mascara on the upper lashes, root to tip.
3. Nothing on the lower lashes.
4. Night: warm water, then face wash.

**B. A woman over 60 with hooded, sensitive eyes and reading glasses**
1. Morning: moisturiser; eye cream only under the eye; let it absorb.
2. A tiny amount of eyeshadow primer (a thin base cream for the lid) or a dusting of loose powder over the whole lid, so the lid's oil doesn't break the mascara down.
3. Optional: a soft brown eyeliner pencil smudged close to the upper lashes (skin side, not the waterline, the wet inner rim).
4. Optional: curl gently (heated curler if the metal one pinches).
5. Magnifying mirror placed low; chin up, eyes looking down.
6. Mascara (tubing or water-based, fragrance-free): one coat on the upper lashes, wiggle at the root; tip of the wand for corners. Second coat straight away only if needed.
7. Skip the lower lashes, or one light dab at the outer third.
8. Cotton bud on hand for touch-ups.
9. Night: lenses out (if worn); warm-water soak 30 seconds; slide off; gentle cleanser; lash serum on a dry lash line if used.
10. Replace the mascara every 3 months (write the date on the tube).

Sources for B: **Y2**, **Y3**, **Y9**, **Y18**, **Y19**, **S9**, **S23** [M]; the combination is my reading [R].

**C. Contact-lens wearer**
1. Hands washed; lenses in **first**.
2. Water-based or tubing mascara; no fibres; tips and top lashes only.
3. Never liner inside the lash line ("Don't apply eyeliner to the inner edge of the lid") [M] **S23**.
4. Night: lenses out **first**, then remove mascara [M] **S23**.

**D. A wedding, a funeral, a swim**
- Waterproof for one day is fine: "for occasional use of course waterproof mascara and eyeliner are fine" [M] **Y14**. A makeup artist keeps waterproof "for brides or mother of the brides anyone who thinks they're going to get emotional" [M] **Y3**.
- Remove carefully with an oil remover that night.

**E. Dry-eye routine (from an ophthalmologist)**
- Warm compress on closed eyes for 10–15 minutes (a sock filled with rice, warmed 30 seconds) to open the oil glands; lid wipes (ready-soaked pads for cleaning the lid edge); preservative-free artificial tears (eye drops that copy natural tears). Add makeup back slowly, one product at a time [M] **Y15**.

### 5.5 What changes after 50 and 60, and why

1. **Fewer, shorter, finer lashes.** Measured: 20% shorter, thinner [M] **S12**. Why: lashes grow for only 4–10 weeks [M] **S13**; with age, follicles may spend longer resting (expert view, **Y2**). Result: mascara has less to grip; heavy formulas drag them down.
2. **Lids hood.** Extra skin folds over; lashes touch it [M] **Y7**. Result: transfer to the lid, "polka dots" on the brow bone (the ridge under the eyebrow) [M] **Y3**.
3. **Under-eye skin thins and creases.** Result: flakes and smudges sit in lines and look like dark circles. "tiny black confetti sits in your fine lines and dark circles" [M] **S2**; "Heavy, dry mascara emphasizes dark circles and every fine line" (makeup artist Caroline Hernandez, quoted in **Y2**) [M].
4. **More eye cream and richer skincare.** Result: more grease near lashes → smudging [M] **Y7**.
5. **Tear film changes; dry eye and eye drops.** Oil glands work less well; eyes sting and water [M] **Y2**, **Y15**. Meibomian gland problems are linked to preservatives, surfactants and essential oils near the eye in a 2026 review [M] **S17**.
6. **Allergies can start late.** "you can develop an allergic reaction to a product even if you've used that product one, ten or even a thousand times before" (Dr Rupa, **Y15**) [M]. Eyelids are a top site for cosmetic allergy [M] **S16**.
7. **Eyesight.** Hard to see lashes; cataract surgery; shaky hands [M] **Y5** comments.
8. **Less curl.** "As I got older I found that curling didn't work any longer" (@elenapizzarello9344) [M].
9. **Style shift.** Many want "less is more", softer black or brown-black: "a softer brown-black instead of a harsh jet black on fair or cool skin" [M] **Y2**. Some want to avoid looking "clownish" or "Baby Jane" [M] **S5**.

### Problem 1: "My lashes have disappeared" (short, sparse, light)
- **What:** Lashes too short or few to show; the eye looks bare and tired.
- **Why:** Lashes ~20% shorter and thinner after 50 [M] **S12**; finer hairs reflect less.

### Problem 2: Smudging, transfer and "raccoon eyes"
- **What:** Black marks under the eye or on the lid by midday.
- **Why:** Lid oil, eye cream, tears, heat and hooded skin soften wax mascaras; lashes droop and touch the skin [M] **Y7**, **Y3**, **Y8**.
- **How it feels:** "Every single mascara I've tried over the last 30 years plus ends up transferring onto the foundation under my eyes or just creating a very dark shadow underneath …" (@teddyroon, age 66, 0 likes). ### Problem 3: Flakes in fine lines
- **What:** Dry crumbs falling under the eyes.
- **Why:** Wax-and-pigment coats dry stiff and crack; old tubes dry out.
- **How it feels:** "start to crumble from your eyes around lunch time" (@unik428). "Halfway through the day, flaking" (reviewer quoted in **Y1**).
- **Tried and failed:** Prestige volume mascaras; fresh tubes that go off after weeks.
- **Luxe [R]:** Claims "zero flaking"; tubing generally flakes less ("tubing mascara is incapable of flaking", makeup artist **Y3**; though one Thrive user said "after few weeks of using it, it would start to flake", @VictoriaBC610). Untested for Luxe.

### Problem 4: Hard removal that pulls lashes out
- **What:** Rubbing and scrubbing at night; lashes on the cotton pad.
- **Why:** Waterproof and long-wear formulas need oil and friction; rubbing breaks and pulls lashes [M] **Y14**, **S13**.
- **How it feels:** "If i have to scrub my eyes to get it off that is a deal breaker" (@dfollmer1428). "whenever I take off waterproof mascara it always rips out my lashes and takes so long" (@josiejohnson_28). - **Luxe [R]:** "Warm water dissolves it cleanly" is the centre of the pitch. If true, it helps. Remember tubing can still need patience and warm water: Dominique needed soap and remover to get Tarte's tubing off (**Y6**), and cold water can pull lashes (**Y21c**).

### Problem 5: Sensitive, stinging, itchy, watering eyes
- **What:** Eyes burn, water or itch, lids go red after mascara.
- **Why:** Allergy or irritation to fragrance, preservatives, wax, nickel, alcohol; dry eye; blocked oil glands [M] **Y15**, **Y12**, **S16**, **S17**.
- **How it feels:** "Essence looked great but caused an itching and burning every time I wore it" (@JoyWinters-t4h). "some black mascaras just make my eyes water and hurt" (@AudriannaB-World-Peace). - **Luxe [R]:** Claims hypoallergenic, pH balanced, water-based. **But with no ingredient list, someone with a known allergy cannot check it.** Patch test and stop if it stings.

### Problem 6: Watery eyes and eye drops break the mascara
- **What:** Tears and drops make mascara run, even tubing.
- **Why:**
  - Tubing films are made of water-loving plastics: a chemist calls the main one "the water soluble polymer that makes tubing mascaras tube" [M] **Y8**. Warm water is exactly what brands tell you to use to take them off [M] **S7**.
  - Tears are warm water, and eye drops are watery too [R]. So a watery eye slowly does to the mascara what the warm cloth does at night.
  - Older eyes water more, because the tear film (the thin tear layer over the eye) becomes less stable: "mature eyes water more" [M] **Y2**; Dr Rupa, **Y15**.
  - Heat, humidity and sweat add more warm water [R].
- **How it feels:** "Tears are warm so they cause the mascara to smear, just as warm water does" (@JoyWinters-t4h). "Victoria Beckham's Vast Lash… holds up to the multiple medicated eye drops I have to use when the tubing mascaras broke up" (@bethirwin7445). "Tubing mascaras smudge and run down my face from May-December in the heat and humidity of Florida" (@Corrine919).
- **What they tried and why it failed:**
  - Tubing mascara: it broke up under eye drops, so one woman moved to another mascara: "Victoria Beckham's Vast Lash… holds up to the multiple medicated eye drops I have to use when the tubing mascaras broke up" (@bethirwin7445, 3 likes, **Y4** comments, review #254). Sky Tubes: "do not wear it when you think you might cry, ot runs everywhere, and starts coming off in cubes" (#183).
  - Waterproof: [R] it resists tears better, but even that failed in heat: "working in the salon in Georgia was like working in a saunafied sweat shop and even waterproof mascara would end up looking like raccoon eyes" (@NIKKIRHODES1212, review #265). And waterproof is harsh to take off: "found the irritation from removing it everyday caused issues with my rosacea at my eyes" (@lorainemcgillivray77, 2 likes, **Y9c**).
  - Skipping the lower lashes: "I've learned with using eye drops, It's better to not use mascara on the lower eyelashes or only in the outer corners" (@donkiml5805, review #257). It helps, but the top lashes still run.
- **Luxe [R]:** If it is warm-water tubing, this is its **weak spot**. One review on Luxe's own page says the opposite: "i have very watery eyes… it stays on perfectly no matter how much my eyes water" (#41); remember that 78 of the 80 Luxe reviews sit on the waterproof listing.

Flakes or fibres that fall into the eye can also get caught under a lens (fibres: **Y2**) [M].
- **How it feels:** "I wore contacts for over 50 years" and preferred no mascara (Rebecca, 70, **Y5**). Luxe ad persona: "My eyes water at everything and I wear contacts, so most mascaras are a nightmare" [M] **S2** (brand copy, not a verified customer).

### Problem 8: Straight lashes that won't curl or hold a curl; curlers that pinch

### Problem 9: Clumps and "spider legs"

### 7.2 When to pick Luxe, and when something else [R]

**Luxe could make sense if:**
- You want a tubing-style, warm-water-removal mascara **and** like the idea of "conditioning" in one tube.
- You buy it at the Amazon price or the 2-for-1 deal, not $39.95 for one.
- You have short, fine lashes, hooded or oily lids, and no known ingredient allergies.

**Pick something else if:**
- **You have known allergies or very reactive eyes** → choose a product with a published list you (or your doctor) can check: Clinique Lash Power, Thrive, Lash Paradise.
- **You want brown or brown-black** → Thrive (brown-black), Tarte (brown), Blinc (dark brown), Grande (brown).
- **Your eyes water a lot or you use drops all day** → tubing may break down; a waterproof for occasional days, or a regular formula, may last better.
- **You're on a tight budget** → Essence ($4.97) and Sky High ($10.82) performed as well as a $44 Dior in a week-long test: "It's really hard to tell the difference between the $4 one and the $44 one" (**Y6**) [M].
- **You want proof of eye-doctor testing** → Thrive and L'Oréal state it on their own pages (**S7**, **S8**).
- **You want real lash growth** → that's a serum's job, not a mascara's (pack 4).

## 11. Safety, and what you can and can't say on camera (short)

**Safety** [M]
- Patch-test new eye makeup; stop at stinging, itching, redness or swelling; see a doctor if it doesn't settle (**S9**, **Y15**).
- Don't use eye makeup during an eye infection; throw out what you were using (**S9**).
- After eye surgery, wait for the surgeon's OK [R].
- Replace 2–4 months after opening; never add water or saliva; never share; no shop testers (**S9**).
- Never apply in a moving car (**S9**).
- No pins near the eye (**Y12**).
- Contact lenses: in before makeup, out before removal (**S23**).
- Remove it every night (**Y12**, **Y13**).
- **Luxe's ingredient list is not published**: anyone with a known allergy, or worried about hidden prostaglandins (e.g. isopropyl cloprostenate, **Y15**), should read the box first.

**Can't say on camera** (drug-type claims under FDA rules, **S11**)
- "heals your lashes", "grows lashes", "fast growth", "regrows", "stops lash loss", "restores your 20-year-old lashes".
- Don't present the brand's "clinical" numbers (100%, 98%, 97%, "2,844 women") or "dermatologist-approved" as fact; no study was found.
- Don't say "hypoallergenic" or "safe for contacts" as a promise.

**Can say**
- "looks longer / fuller / darker", "separates", "didn't smudge on me after X hours", "came off with warm water in my test", "the brand says it contains conditioning peptides".
