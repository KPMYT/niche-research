## 1. The basics from zero

### 1.1 What mascara is, in one minute

- **Mascara** is a thick black (or brown) liquid that you brush onto your **eyelashes** (the short hairs that grow from the edge of the eyelid). It coats each hair, so the lashes look **darker, longer, thicker and more curled**.
- It comes in a small tube. The cap is attached to a thin stick with a little brush on the end. That brush is called the **wand**.
- Think of it as **paint for eyelash hairs**. A light-coloured, thin, short lash almost disappears against the skin. Painted black and made a little thicker, it shows up. That outlines the eye, so the eye looks bigger and more "awake".
- It is the most-used single piece of eye makeup. A professional makeup artist of 25 years: "if I only had one thing of makeup to take, what would you take? mascara" (Michelle Spieler, **Y3**) [M].
- It washes off. Nothing in a normal mascara changes the lash itself for good [R].

### 1.2 Eyelashes: what they are and how they grow

Before mascara makes sense, you need to know what it is painted on.

| Fact | Plain meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The upper lid has **90–160 lashes** in 5–6 rows; the lower lid has **75–80** in 3–4 rows. | The top row is the one mascara is mainly for. The bottom row is sparser and shorter. | [M] **S13** (Aumond & Bitton 2018, a review in *Journal of Optometry*) |
| A lash grows **0.12–0.14 mm a day**. | About 1 mm in a week. Much slower than you'd notice. | [M] **S13** |
| Each lash lives through 3 phases: **growing** (anagen), **shrinking** (catagen), **resting** (telogen). Then it falls out and a new one starts. | Like a queue: each lash grows, stops, rests, drops out, and is replaced. | [M] **S13** |
| The growing phase lasts **4–10 weeks**; one whole life cycle takes **4–11 months**. | Because growing is short, lashes stay short. Scalp hair grows for years; lashes for weeks. | [M] **S13** |
| A lash rarely gets longer than **12 mm**. | About half an inch. | [M] **S13** |
| Lashes are naturally **curved** in everyone. Curl is stronger on average in white people than in East Asians. | Why some women's lashes point straight out or down and need a curler. | [M] **S13** |
| Each lash root has little glands beside it (**Zeis** and **Moll** glands) that make an oily and a watery secretion. Behind the lash line sit about 50 **meibomian glands** on the top lid and 30 or so on the bottom, which make the oil layer of your tears. | The lash line is a busy, oily, wet place. That is why mascara can smudge there, and why gunk left there can upset the eye. | [M] **S13**; meibomian counts from ophthalmologist Dr Rupa Wong, **Y14**, **Y15** |
| Lashes are there to **protect the eye**. In wind-tunnel studies of animal eyes, the best lash length was about **one-third of the eye's width**: it halved dust landing on the eye and slowed tear drying. | Lashes are not only for looks. Damaging them matters. | [M] **S13** |

**What happens to lashes with age**

| Change | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shorter.** Average upper lash length was **7.98 mm** at ages 22–29 and **6.39 mm** at 50–65 (179 women). | That is about **20% shorter** [R, my sum: 6.39 ÷ 7.98 = 0.80]. | [M] **S12** (Glaser 2014; one co-author worked for Allergan, maker of the lash drug Latisse) |
| **Thinner.** Average thickness fell from **1.62** (ages 20–29) to **1.17** (50–65) in the study's units. | Each hair carries less colour and less bulk. | [M] **S12** |
| **"Darker or lighter" is unclear.** The study concludes lashes get lighter, but its own darkness numbers seem to point the other way. | Don't say "your lashes go lighter" on camera. | [M] **S12**, as noted in `checks/01-mascara-check.md` |
| Lashes **turn grey only at a very advanced age and rarely go white**, unlike scalp hair. | Most women over 60 still have dark lashes; they are just fewer and finer. | [M] **S13** |
| The curve of the **lower** lashes near the nose and middle increases with age. | Lower lashes can point more downward or inward. | [M] **S13** (citing Procianoy) |
| Makeup artist Scott Barnes, as quoted in a video: lashes "get thinner and less dense as women get older because hormones shift and the follicles spend more time resting than growing". | A second-hand quote; no study given. | [M] quote in **Y2**; [R] treat as an expert's view, not proof |
| The skin around the eye thins and folds over: **hooded lids** (extra skin that hangs over the crease and sometimes touches the lashes). | Lashes touch skin, so mascara prints onto the lid. | [M] Nina Ubhi, **Y7**: "your eyes are maybe becoming more and more hooded… you've got excess skin around that area. So, things are touching now" |
| Tears get less stable; eyes can be drier **and** water more. | Watery eyes break down some mascaras. | [M] **Y2** ("Tear production also drops, which sounds backwards, but means mature eyes water more"); Dr Rupa, **Y15** |
| Eyesight changes (reading glasses, cataracts) make close work harder. | People can't see their lashes well enough to apply. | [M] comment: "When I had my cataract surgery, I bought a 10X mirror so I could see up close." (@adriennekushner1968, **Y5**, 42 likes) |

**Can mascara damage lashes?**
- In 36 Japanese women aged 20–70, **the more often they wore mascara, the more the lash surface was cracked** and the more porous the inside of the lash was. **Using a lash curler made no significant difference.** [M] **S14** (Fukami 2014).
- The same review notes long-term mascara users can lose lashes, "possibly due to the rubbing" when removing it [M] **S13**.
- [R] So the main risk to lashes is **how mascara comes off**, not the colour sitting on them. That is why removal is such a big theme with older women.

### 1.3 How mascara works: the science in kitchen-table words

**What is in almost every mascara** [M] **S25**, **S13**, **Y8**, **Y11**:

| Part | What it is | Kitchen comparison | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pigment** | Usually black iron oxide (on labels: "Iron Oxides (CI 77499)"); sometimes a little blue (ultramarines) to make the black look deeper. Pigments are about 0.5–10% of a mascara. | Like the soot in shoe polish. | Gives the colour. |
| **Waxes** | Beeswax, carnauba (from palm leaves), paraffin and similar. | Like candle wax softened into a paste. | Give body. Thicken the lash. Help it hold its shape. |
| **Film formers** (polymers) | Plastic-like ingredients that dry into a thin, flexible skin. Usually 1–8% of the formula. | Like PVA school glue or hairspray drying into a clear film. | Hold the colour on, stop it moving, help hold curl. A cosmetic chemist: these are "borrowed from hairspray… it's like hair gel, it's holding the lashes where you want them" (Y8). |
| **Liquid base** | Water (most mascaras) **or** a fast-drying oil-like solvent such as isododecane (waterproof mascaras). | Water vs. nail-polish thinner. | Keeps it runny in the tube, then evaporates on the lash. |
| **Emulsifiers** | Ingredients that let oil and water mix. | Like egg yolk in mayonnaise. | Hold water, waxes and oils together as a cream. |
| **Preservatives** | Ingredients that stop bacteria and mould. | Like the salt in jam. | Vital: a mascara wand goes from eye to tube every day. |
| **"Care" extras** | Oils, panthenol (vitamin B5), peptides, vitamin E. | The "added vitamins" on a cereal box. | Marketing appeal; may soften lashes. |

- "Mascaras are generally **emulsions**, meaning that they're mixtures of water and oil or lipid based substances like waxes" (cosmetic chemist Ramón Pagán, **Y8**) [M].
- An eye-care review: "Water-based mascara is made of several waxes, types of pigment and resins dissolved in water, whereas solvent-based mascara, known as waterproof, has its pigments and waxes added in petroleum distillates" [M] **S13**.

**How it's made** [M] **Y11** (Refinery29, inside Maybelline's Great Lash factory, 2.26M views):
1. Waxes and stiffening agents are melted so the mix isn't too runny.
2. Oils and pigment are added for colour and texture. Waterproof versions get a "water-rebuffing" ingredient.
3. Everything is **mixed and heated** until it becomes the dark paste.
4. The batch is **tested** (Maybelline uses a light-splitting machine, a spectrometer, to check the make-up of each batch).
5. Tubes stand upright on a conveyor; the mascara is **pumped in**.
6. A clear plastic plug called the **wiper** goes into the neck. It scrapes extra mascara off the wand as you pull it out, so the brush isn't overloaded.
7. The wand goes in, labels go on, more checks, then shipping.

[R] The wand and the wiper matter as much as the liquid. A chemist says brands hold **patents** on how wand bristles are spaced and shaped (**Y8**) [M].

### 1.4 History and trend: why people are buying it now

**History** [M] **S21** (sources disagree on some dates; disagreements shown)
| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Ancient times | People darkened eyes and lashes with soot-based mixtures for thousands of years. |
| 1800s | Eugène Rimmel sold a petroleum-jelly-based lash darkener. Dates given vary: 1830s, 1860, about 1880. In several languages "rimmel" still means mascara. Queen Victoria's perfumer is also said to have mixed coal dust and petroleum jelly (**Y11**). |
| 1915–1917 | Maybel Williams darkened her lashes with a similar mix; her brother T.L. Williams sold a version by mail. The company became **Maybelline** (Mabel + Vaseline). Most sources say 1917; the Refinery29 video says 1915. |
| 1957 or 1958 | Helena Rubinstein's **Mascara-Matic**: the first tube with a built-in wand. Most sources say 1957; Wikipedia says 1958. |
| 1971 | **Maybelline Great Lash** (pink and green tube), often called the first water-based mascara. Maybelline said in 2018 that one is sold every two seconds (**Y11**). |
| About 1995 | **Blinc** introduces the first **tubing** mascara (sold in Japan as "Kiss Me"). Some sources say 1999. |
| 2017 | Thrive Causemetics launches Liquid Lash Extensions (tubing), now a cult product with over-50 women. |
| 2018–2026 | Big brands pile into tubing: L'Oréal Double Extend (2018), Merit and Caliray (2021), Tarte Tartelette Tubing (2022), Too Faced and Urban Decay (2025), CoverGirl (Feb 2026). |

**Why now** [M] **S19** (Beauty Independent, 6 Feb 2026, data from Spate and Quilt AI):
- Mascara sales were estimated at about **$6.4 billion in 2022**, projected to more than double by 2032. The tubing slice alone was estimated at about **$1.42 billion in 2024**.
- Google searches for "tubing mascara" rose **35.2%** year on year; from Oct 2024 to Sep 2025 searches rose **9.1%**.
- The top theme in what shoppers say online is **confusion**: 51% of the conversation is basic questions like "tubing mascara what is". Tubing mascaras at Sephora cost about **$14–$34**.
- In 159 comments on over-40/50/60 mascara videos, the top complaints were hard removal (19), smudging (14), price (11) and giving up mascara (10) [M] **S5**.

[R] Three forces meet: an ageing audience whose lashes and lids have changed, a newly popular formula type (tubing) that solves smudging and hard removal, and a "skincare in makeup" fashion (peptides, conditioning) borrowed from lash serums. Luxe's mascara is pitched at exactly that meeting point.

### 1.5 The types of mascara

**By formula (what's in the tube)**

| Type | How it works, plainly | Good for | Bad for | How it comes off | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular ("washable")** | A water-and-wax cream. Dries as a soft coat of coloured wax on the lash. | Volume, cheap, easy to layer. | Oily lids, rich eye creams, tears and heat soften the wax → smudges and flakes. | Soap and water, micellar water, cleansing oil. | Essence Lash Princess, L'Oréal Lash Paradise Washable, Maybelline Sky High Washable, Grande GrandeMASCARA |
| **Waterproof** | Little or no water. The wax and pigment sit in a fast-drying solvent; once it evaporates, what's left won't dissolve in water. "Waterproof mascaras are completely water-free, meaning they are anhydrous" (**Y8**). | Crying, swimming, humidity, holding a curl. | Removal needs an oil or two-phase remover and rubbing; can dry lashes; makeup artist: "really harsh on the lashes over time and very drying" (**Y3**). Still breaks down with very oily lids: "oil loves oil" (**Y8**). | Oil cleanser or bi-phase remover. | Bobbi Brown No Smudge, Lash Princess Waterproof, Sky High Waterproof |
| **Tubing** | Lots of water-based film formers. As it dries it wraps **each lash in a tiny plastic-like sleeve**. "Think of hollow spaghetti… putting little straws around your lashes because it uses a polymer blend" (makeup artist, **Y3**). | Oily or hooded lids, smudging, flaking, gentle removal, sensitive eyes. | Less volume; can break down with warm tears, humidity, sweat or eye drops; some versions dry out; some only allow 1–2 coats. | **Warm water** and gentle pressure: the sleeves slide off in little tubes. "It will not come off with… micellar water… it will come off with warm hot water" (**Y3**). | Thrive Liquid Lash Extensions, Tarte Tartelette Tubing, Clinique Lash Power, Blinc, L'Oréal Double Extend, Essence "Bye Bye Panda Eyes", Hourglass Unlocked |
| **Fibre** | Regular mascara with tiny nylon, rayon or bamboo threads that stick to the lash tips to add length. | Length on short lashes. | Fibres shed into the eye; trouble for contact lenses and sensitive eyes. Video: "Fiber formulas shed, and those loose fibers fall into the eye, where they can scratch the cornea" (**Y2**). | Like regular, but watch for fibres in the eye. | MAC Magic Extension 5mm Fibre; Sky High says it has bamboo "fibers" (**Y6**) |
| **"Conditioning" / serum-infused** | Any of the above plus "care" ingredients: peptides, panthenol, oils, vitamins. | Appeal for people who worry about lash health. | No mascara-level study shows growth; the care ingredients are usually tiny amounts and wash off at night. | Depends on the base. | Grande GrandeMASCARA (regular base), Tarte Tartelette XL "with Peptide" (tubing base), **Luxe** (base not published) |
| **Primer / base coat** | A white or clear coat put on first. | More length; makeup artist saw longer lashes on the primed eye (**Y3**). | An extra step; can make clumps. | Like the mascara on top. | Lancôme Cils Booster, Grande Primer, L'Oréal Voluminous Superstar (two-ended) |

**By job (what it is trying to do)**
- **Lengthening**: thinner formula, comb-like brush; separated, longer lashes.
- **Volumising**: thicker formula, fuller brush; more bulk, risk of clumps.
- **Curling / lifting**: stiffer film and often a curved brush to hold lashes up.
- **Defining / separating**: clean, "natural" lashes, no clumps.
- [R] Most products claim several at once. What you actually get depends mostly on the **brush** and how much **wax and film former** is in the formula. A commenter: "I've always thought it's not the mascara, it's the brush!" (@BMc-hm2pb, 21 likes, **Y6** comments) [M].

**Waterproof vs water-resistant vs washable** [R]
- **Waterproof**: won't come off with water; needs an oily remover.
- **Water-resistant**: survives a bit of water and sweat; still comes off with warm water or normal cleanser. Thrive calls its tubing mascara "water-resistant" (**S7**) [M].
- **Washable**: a regular formula that comes off with soap and water.
- A product cannot sensibly be both **waterproof** and "comes off with warm water". The Luxe page says both (see 2.4).

### 1.6 Brushes and wands

| Brush | What it looks like | What it does | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fibre (bristle) brush** | Twisted wire with soft nylon bristles, like a tiny bottle brush. | Holds more formula; gives volume. | Fuller look; risk of clumps on sparse lashes. |
| **Silicone / moulded brush** | Rubber-like spikes or combs. | Separates and combs; holds less formula. | Length and separation; short lashes. Sky High's "Flex Tower" brush "bends" (**Y6**). |
| **Thin / slim / "precision" brush** | Very narrow. | Reaches tiny inner-corner lashes and lower lashes without painting the skin. | Short, sparse lashes; lower lashes; hooded eyes. |
| **Tapered tip** | Narrows to a point. | Picks up corner lashes. Dominique Sachse: "I love this pointed tip… great for just grabbing these outer corner lashes" (**Y6**). | Short lashes, corners. |
| **Curved brush** | Banana-shaped. | Follows the curve of the eye; helps hold lift. Chemist: "that curled shape basically is mirroring the curve of your eyeball and eyelashes" (**Y8**). | Straight lashes. |
| **Hourglass / "number eight"** | Fat at ends, thin in the middle. | Big volume. | Drama; not ideal on sparse lashes. |
| **Flat / paddle** | Flat on one side, combed on the other. | Flat side loads the root, comb separates (Charlotte Tilbury Pillow Talk Push Up, **Y1**; Telescopic, **Y3**). | Lift. |
| **Ball tip** | A tiny ball at the end. | Corners and lower lashes (**Y18**). | Lower lashes. |
| **Tubing brushes** | Usually short, evenly spaced bristles, straight and tapered. Chemist: "the bristles are short and they're very evenly spaced so that you can get basically around every individual lash" (**Y8**). | Lays the sleeve evenly. | — |

Extra tools:
- **Spoolie**: a clean, dry mascara-style brush used to comb out clumps.
- **Lash comb**: a fine metal or plastic comb that separates lashes after mascara (**Y18**). Never use a pin: "you could inadvertently stab your eye" (dermatologist Dr Dray, **Y12**).
- **Eyelash curler**: a metal clamp with a rubber pad that bends lashes upward. **Heated curler**: a warm wand used *after* mascara (makeup artist Aran P, **Y18**).

### 2.3 Every ingredient, one by one

**Luxe's own ingredient list is not published anywhere.** Six Luxe shop feeds, the product pages' full code, Amazon snippets and eBay were searched twice by two helpers; none gave a list [M] **S5**. The brand names only "conditioning peptides" and "Luxe's ingredients" [M] **S1**. US law requires retail cosmetics to declare ingredients on the label; "If neither the package nor the display rack provides the ingredient declaration, you aren't getting the information you're entitled to" [M] **S9**. [R] The box may carry a list; photograph it when the product arrives.

So below are **two real, published lists from other brands**, explained one by one, to show what is **typically** inside the two kinds of product Luxe's copy describes. **Neither list is Luxe's.**

How to read any list [R]: in the US, ingredients above 1% are listed from most to least; those under 1% can come in any order (FDA labelling rule 21 CFR 701.3; not re-pulled today). So the first five or six ingredients are most of the product, and anything after the preservatives is a tiny amount.

#### A. A typical water-based tubing mascara: Thrive Causemetics Liquid Lash Extensions ("Brynn", rich black). TYPICAL EXAMPLE, NOT LUXE.

Source: Thrive's own product page [M] **S7**. Thrive's page says it is "water-resistant", made in the USA, $26, 10.7 g. The plain-English functions are my reading [R], except where a source is named.

| # | Ingredient (as printed) | What it is, plainly | What it does here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aqua / Water | Water. | The base. Carries everything; evaporates as it dries. That's why tubing is "water-based". |
| 2 | **Acrylates/Ethylhexyl Acrylate Copolymer** | A plastic-like polymer spread through water as tiny particles (the same family as acrylic paint and water-based glues). | **The tube-maker.** As water leaves, the particles join into a flexible sleeve around the lash. Chemist: "the acrylate co-polymer, that's the water soluble polymer that makes tubing mascaras tube" [M] **Y8**. Warm water softens it so it slides off. |
| 3 | Alcohol Denat. | Denatured alcohol (drinking alcohol made undrinkable). | Helps dissolve the polymers and speeds drying. Chemist: "it flashes off very quick so the impact on the lash and the eye is minimal to none" [M] **Y8**. [R] Some very sensitive eyes dislike alcohol near the eye. |
| 4 | Glyceryl Stearate SE | A fat-based cream-maker ("SE" = self-emulsifying). | Keeps the oils and water mixed; creamy texture. |
| 5 | Glycerin | A clear, sweet, sticky liquid that draws in water. | Keeps the formula moist in the tube and the film flexible on the lash. |
| 6 | Dimethicone | A silicone (smooth, slippery). | Glide while applying; soft feel. |
| 7 | **VP/Eicosene Copolymer** | An oil-loving film former. | Adds longer wear and water resistance. Chemist: "an oil soluble film former… helps to improve longevity" [M] **Y8**. |
| 8 | Carnauba Wax | Hard wax from the leaves of a Brazilian palm (also used on cars and sweets). | Gives body and length; firmness. |
| 9 | Stearic Acid | A fatty acid (from plant or animal fats). | Combines with #12 to make a mild soap-like emulsifier; thickens. |
| 10 | Butyrospermum Parkii (Shea) Butter | Fat from shea nuts. | Softening "care" ingredient. Thrive: "Deeply conditions" [M] **S7**. |
| 11 | Ricinus Communis (Castor) Seed Oil | Castor oil. | Softening "care" ingredient; the oil many women already put on lashes at night. |
| 12 | Tromethamine | A mild alkali. | Adjusts acidity (pH); with #9 and #14 forms the emulsifier. |
| 13 | Phenoxyethanol | A common preservative. | Stops bacteria and mould growing in the tube. |
| 14 | Palmitic Acid | A fatty acid (palm or other fats). | Same job as #9. |
| 15 | Laureth-21 | A detergent-type emulsifier. | Helps mix; helps the film release in warm water [R]. |
| 16 | **PVP** (polyvinylpyrrolidone) | A water-soluble polymer used in hairspray. | Second film former. Chemist: "PVP, that's another water soluble polymer that we normally see in tubing mascaras" [M] **Y8**. |
| 17 | Xanthan Gum | A natural thickener made by fermenting sugar (also in salad dressing). | Thickens; stops pigment sinking. |
| 18 | Sodium Dehydroacetate | A preservative. | Backs up #13. |
| 19 | Methylglucamine | A sugar-derived alkali. | Adjusts pH. |
| 20 | Ethylhexylglycerin | A mild glycerin relative. | Helps the preservatives work; softens. |
| 21 | Trisodium Ethylenediamine Disuccinate | A "chelator": grabs traces of metal in water. | Keeps the formula stable and helps preservatives work. |
| 22 | Panthenol | Pro-vitamin B5. | Holds moisture in hair; "care" claim. |
| 23 | Neofinetia Falcata Callus Culture Extract | Lab-grown cells from a Japanese orchid. | Thrive's "Orchid Stem Cell Complex"; a marketing extra in tiny amount. |
| 24 | Sodium Hyaluronate | Hyaluronic acid salt (moisture magnet). | "Care" extra. |
| 25 | Lecithin | A fat from soy or sunflower. | Helps mixing; softens. |
| 26 | Iron Oxides (CI 77499) | Black iron oxide (a mineral pigment). | **The colour.** |

What this tells us [R]:
- A tubing mascara is mostly **water + film formers + a little wax + pigment + preservatives**. The "care" ingredients sit low in the list.
- **If Luxe really is a "lash-casting" tubing formula, expect a list that looks like this**: water first, then an acrylates copolymer, probably alcohol, PVP or similar.
- Thrive's page also says: "Look for polymers in the ingredient list, as they create the tube-like effect around each lash" [M] **S7**.

What the peptide evidence is [M]:
- PubMed search "myristoyl pentapeptide-17 eyelash" found **0** studies (**S5**).
- An ingredient database reports a 90-day study of 29 people (lash length +10.52%) but the study itself, its publisher and whether it had a comparison group were **not found** (**S24**).
- The nearest published trial is of a **serum** (left on all night), not a mascara: 30 women, no comparison group, run by the maker's staff; lashes +8.3% longer at 12 weeks (**S18**).
- Grande's own claims rest on "a consumer study with 31 subjects" (what people said, not measurements): "97% saw longer looking lashes" [M] **S6**.
- [R] **Plain conclusion:** a peptide in mascara is a "nice extra". There is no good evidence that it makes lashes grow. It may help lashes feel softer, like a hair conditioner does.
