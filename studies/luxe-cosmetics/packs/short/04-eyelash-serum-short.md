# Luxe Eyelash Serum (3 mL): the short version

## 1. In one sentence

A cheap, clear liquid you paint along the edge of your upper eyelid every night, to make your own lashes look longer and fuller over weeks, without the strong drug-type ingredient that can change eye colour.

## 2. What it is, in detail

A **lash serum** (a thin liquid painted at the lash roots) is not makeup. It does nothing on the day; it works slowly, over weeks.

- **Format:** a slim black tube with a fine brush, like a **liquid eyeliner** (makeup painted as a thin line along the lash line). The liquid is clear and has no smell.
- **Size:** 3 mL, about 60 uses: about 2 months at once a night.
- **Price:** $13 on Amazon (usual price $15.99); $29.95 on the brand's site. Amazon: 4.0 stars from 495 ratings.
- **Free from:** **prostaglandins** (the strong drug-type lash ingredient, see section 3), oils, perfume and **parabens** (a common preservative).

| Ingredient | What it does here |
|---|---|
| Water | The base |
| Glycerin (a syrupy, water-holding liquid) | Keeps lashes and lid skin moist and bendy |
| Ginseng extract (a plant root) | Brand says it "revitalizes" roots; no lash trial |
| Biotin (vitamin B7) | Sold for strength; no lash proof |
| Vitamin E | Conditions: lashes less dry, less likely to break |
| Three **peptides** (tiny pieces of protein) | Coat and condition; tiny amounts |
| Two helper liquids | Keep it free of germs |

Ten ingredients in all; mostly water and glycerin.

## 3. How this kind of product works

**A lash is a hair.** It grows from a tiny root in the lid edge, in three stages: growing (about 4 to 10 weeks), switching off (about 15 days) and resting (about 4 to 9 months) until it falls. The growing stage is short, so lashes stop at about 1 cm. Most lashes are resting at any moment. So **every serum needs 1 to 3 months** to show anything, and gains fade slowly after you stop.

**There are two families:**

| | Strong, drug-type (prostaglandin) | Gentle, conditioning (peptide) |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Latisse (prescription), GrandeLASH-MD, Babe Essential, RevitaLash | **Luxe**, The Ordinary, Babe Renewing, Vegamour |
| How it works | Keeps lashes growing longer; wakes resting roots | Moisturises and coats, so lashes break less and stay on longer |
| Results | Big: Latisse helped 78% of people by week 16 in its trial | Smaller, slower; growth not proven |
| Risks | Eye colour turning brown (likely permanent), dark lids, red eyes, sunken eyes | Irritation or allergy, like any cosmetic |

Prostaglandins began as **glaucoma** drops (for an eye-pressure disease); patients grew long lashes by accident. Doctors' tip: look for **"prost"** in the ingredient list.

A peptide serum is like hair conditioner for lashes. Lashes that don't snap end up looking longer and fuller.

## 4. How it's used, and with what

**First:** patch-test (try it on a small patch of skin) for about a week.

**Every night:**
1. Remove eye makeup gently, wash, pat dry. Contact lenses out.
2. Dip the brush once and wipe off the extra. One dip does both eyes.
3. Look down into a mirror. Paint one thin line along the **upper** lash line, right at the roots. Never the lower lashes.
4. Let it dry 1 to 2 minutes, then eye cream if you use it. Cap on tight.

**Where it fits:** last thing on the eyes at night. **Mascara** (black or brown paint brushed onto lashes) is for the day; serum is for the night. Once a night is enough; missed a night, don't double up. Judge it at 8 to 12 weeks, with a "before" photo.

**Pairs with:** mascara by day (gentle removal matters); **lash extensions** (fake lashes glued on), as it is oil-free; a **lash lift** (a perm for lashes), but not on the same day.

**Avoid:** two serums at once; any "prost" product; strong creams near the lash line; a red, infected or just-operated eye.

**Older hands:** elbow on a table, magnifying mirror, bright light; lift the brow gently if lids are hooded.

## 5. Who it's for

**Good for:** short, thin or sparse lashes with no medical cause; lashes that break from curlers, rubbing or extensions; anyone scared of eye colour change or hollow eyes (especially blue, green or hazel eyes); people on glaucoma drops or after **cataract surgery** (replacing the eye's cloudy lens), with their eye doctor's OK; budget buyers.

| | After 50 | After 60 |
|---|---|---|
| Lashes | About 20% shorter and 28% thinner than in your 20s | Finer, sometimes white; regrow slowly |
| Lids | **Hooded lids** (skin folding down over the lid) begin; menopause dry eye | More hooding and hollowing; crusty lids more common |
| Eyes | Reading glasses, some eye drops | More glaucoma drops, cataract surgery |
| What she wants | Gentle, affordable, no prostaglandin | Plus easy to apply, nothing that hollows or darkens |

Older women have less fat around the eye to lose, so the sunken-eye risk of strong serums matters more.

**Not for:** anyone wanting Latisse-level growth; sudden, patchy or one-eye lash loss, or red crusty lids (see a doctor); lash loss from chemotherapy or **alopecia** (hair loss from illness); pregnancy (ask a doctor first).

## 10. What you can and can't say on camera

- Say "helps lashes look longer and fuller", never "grows", "regrows" or "restores growth".
- Say "conditions lashes so they're less likely to break", never "stops lash loss" or "for chemo".
- Say "give it 8 to 12 weeks", never "clinically proven" or "70% longer".
- Say "patch-test first and stop if it stings", never "zero side effects".
- Say "it's not Latisse; it's the gentle, prostaglandin-free kind", after checking the box list.
