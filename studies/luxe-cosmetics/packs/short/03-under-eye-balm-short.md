# Product 3: Luxe Korean Under Eye Balm (stick), the short version

## 1. In one sentence

A twist-up moisturising stick, like a big lip balm, that you swipe under the eyes to soften dry, crepey (finely crinkled, like crepe paper) skin and give concealer (skin-coloured makeup that hides dark circles) a smooth base.

## 2. What it is, in detail

- **Format:** a solid balm in a lavender twist-up tube.
- **Price:** $39.95 on luxe.com (buy one, get one free); $34.99 on luxeresearchlab.com.
- **Size:** not stated. Look-alike Amazon listings say 0.38 oz (about 10.8 g).
- **Lasts:** about 1 to 3 months with twice-daily use, by the brand's figures.
- **Base:** the full ingredient list isn't published. Rival sticks are mostly **waxes and oils**, with the "star" ingredients in small amounts. Buyers describe Luxe the same way: like Vaseline or lip balm.
- **Free from:** retinol (vitamin A), fragrance, alcohol, essential oils, parabens and artificial colours.

| Ingredient | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Adenosine** (the lead ingredient; a small molecule found in every cell) | Anti-wrinkle. In one trial of 126 women aged 45–65, adenosine cream made the skin around the eyes smoother after 3 and 8 weeks. Luxe's amount is unknown. |
| **Niacinamide** (vitamin B3) | Strengthens the skin barrier and slowly fades brown pigment. Named on one Luxe shop only. |
| **Hyaluronic acid** (a sugar-based molecule) | A "sponge": pulls water into the top layer so fine dry lines look softer for a while. |
| **Bisabolol** (from chamomile) | Calms redness and irritation. |
| **Vitamin E** | An antioxidant that keeps the oils fresh. |
| **Argan oil** (a Moroccan nut oil) | Softens and helps the stick glide. |
| **PDRN** (pieces of DNA, usually from salmon) | Fashionable. Promising when injected; too big to soak in from a cream. |
| **Collagen** (the skin's "scaffolding" protein) | A moisturising film. Too big to soak in. |

## 3. How this kind of product works

**Why the under-eye is different.** Eyelid skin is the thinnest on the face: if cheek skin is a winter coat, under-eye skin is a silk scarf. It has fewer oil glands and less collagen and elastin (the protein that lets skin snap back), so it dries and lines first.

**"Tired eyes" is up to six problems:** brown pigment; blue or purple blood vessels showing through; shadow from a hollow; morning puffiness (fluid); bags (fat pushing forward); and crepey, dry, lined skin. Most people over 50 have more than one.

**The types:** eye creams, gels, serums, caffeine serums (for puffiness), balm sticks, gel patches, instant tighteners (a film that tightens for a few hours) and retinol eye creams.

**The science, in kitchen words.** Moisturisers use three kinds of ingredient:
- **Humectants** (a sponge) pull water in.
- **Emollients** (butter in a cracked cake) fill gaps between dry flakes.
- **Occlusives** (a lid on a saucepan) seal water in.

A balm stick is mostly emollient and occlusive, so it's very good at the "lid" job. Moist skin swells slightly, like a soaked raisin, so fine dry lines flatten. Real, but temporary.

**What no cream can do:** it works on the top layer only. It can't move fat, fill a hollow or hide veins.

**The trend.** Korean sticks took off through K-dramas (Korean TV dramas) around 2022, then TikTok and Amazon in 2025–26. Luxe is a late entry.

## 4. How it's used, and with what

**Steps:**
1. Wash hands and face gently. Any thin hydrating serum goes first.
2. Twist up 1–2 mm. Warm the tip on your skin for 2–3 seconds.
3. Glide one light line along the bone below the eye, outer corner inwards. Don't press or rub.
4. Stay a fingertip below the lashes, **never on the waterline** (the wet inner rim of the lid).
5. Tap it in with the ring finger (the weakest finger). About a grain of rice per eye.
6. Wait 1–2 minutes. Morning: mineral (zinc) sunscreen, then a little concealer. Night: balm is the last step.
7. Wipe the tip, cap it, don't share it.

**In a routine:** thinnest to thickest: cleanse → serum → moisturiser → balm under the eyes → sunscreen → makeup.

**Pairs with:** a hydrating serum underneath; mineral sunscreen; caffeine serum or a cold compress for puffy mornings; colour corrector.

**Take care with:** retinol near the eyes (alternate nights, or balm first as a "buffer"); acids near the lids; heavy powder on top. Put contact lenses and eye drops in before the balm.

**What you'll see:** smoother skin in minutes, fading within hours; less dryness in 1–2 weeks; possibly softer lines in 3–8 weeks. Never: bags, hollows or blue veins gone.

## 5. Who it's for

**Good fit:** dry, crepey under-eyes; creasing concealer; eyes that sting with retinol or fragrance; stiff or shaky hands.

**After 50 and 60:**
- **50s (menopause):** skin collagen and thickness fall about 1–2% a year after menopause, and skin holds less water. Crepiness grows and fat bags often grow.
- **60s:** thinner skin shows veins and muscle, so circles look bluer. The bone around the eye recedes, so hollows deepen. Dry eye and new sensitivities are more common.

**Weak or not for:** blue circles, hollows, fat bags, morning puffiness (no caffeine), oily skin (too rich). Not on eyelid eczema, infection or after eye surgery. Nut, fish or chamomile allergies: check first (argan, possibly salmon PDRN, bisabolol).

## 10. What you can and can't say on camera

1. Say: "moisturises", "looks smoother", "a smooth base for concealer".
2. Don't say: "lifts", "firms", "removes bags", "fills hollows", "triggers collagen", "fixes collagen loss", "fixes pigment" or "replaces filler or Botox".
3. Keep it on the bone, never the waterline; patch-test first (jaw or behind the ear, 2–3 days).
4. Stop if it stings or swells. Not on an eye infection, eyelid eczema or after eye surgery.
5. See a doctor for swelling under one eye only, or sudden or painful swelling.
