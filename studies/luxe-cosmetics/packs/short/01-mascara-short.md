# Luxe Conditioning Peptide Mascara: the short version

## 1. In one sentence

A black mascara (a liquid brushed onto the eyelashes to make them look darker, longer and thicker) sold to women over 50 with fine lashes and sensitive eyes, claiming no smudging, warm-water removal and peptides that "condition" lashes.

## 2. What it is, in detail

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tagline** | "Built For Sensitive Eyes & Fine Lashes Over 50". |
| **Format** | A small, slim rose-gold tube. The cap holds a thin, tapered bristle brush (the **wand**), which Luxe calls a "precision wand". |
| **Shade** | Black on the shop page. Two reviewers mention a brown or "black-brown". |
| **Price** | $39.95 for one on Luxe's site; 2 for $54.95; 3 for $69.95. £14.99 on Amazon UK. |
| **Described as** | "Featherlight", "water-based", "pH balanced" (gentle acidity, like tears), "Korean Lash-Casting… a light, flexible cast". That is how **tubing** mascaras are described. |
| **Lasts** | Replace 2–4 months after opening, so 4–6 tubes a year if worn daily. |

**Ingredients.** Luxe names only "conditioning peptides" and has not published a full list. A typical tubing mascara holds:

| Ingredient | What it does |
|---|---|
| Water | The base. Evaporates as it dries. |
| Film formers (plastic-like polymers) | Dry into a thin, flexible sleeve around each lash. Warm water loosens it. |
| A little wax | Body and length. |
| Black iron oxide | The colour. |
| Preservatives | Stop germs growing in the tube. |
| "Care" extras (peptides, panthenol, oils) | Softening. Tiny amounts. |

A **peptide** is a short chain of protein building blocks. In mascara it may help lashes feel softer; there is no good evidence it makes them grow.

## 3. How this kind of product works

Almost every mascara is **colour + wax + film former + a liquid base**. The brush matters as much as the liquid.

| Type | How it works | Comes off with |
|---|---|---|
| **Regular ("washable")** | A water-and-wax cream. Lid oil, eye cream, tears and heat soften it, so it smudges and flakes. | Soap, micellar water (a water-like cleanser) or cleansing oil. |
| **Waterproof** | No water. Survives tears and swimming. | Oil remover and rubbing. Hard on lashes over time. |
| **Tubing** | Wraps each lash in a tiny sleeve, "like hollow spaghetti". Oil and eye cream don't smudge it. | Warm water and gentle pressure. Warm tears, sweat and eye drops can break it down too. |
| **Fibre** | Tiny threads add length. | Fibres can shed into the eye. |

"Conditioning" mascaras add care ingredients to any type. Luxe reads as tubing plus peptides. Rubbing mascara off is the main way lashes get damaged.

## 4. How it's used, and with what

**Morning (the last eye step, after eyeshadow and eyeliner):**
1. Clean, dry lashes. Keep eye cream below the eye.
2. Hooded or oily lids: a little eyeshadow primer (a thin base cream) or loose powder on the lid.
3. Optional: curl lashes before mascara, never after.
4. Twist the wand out. Don't pump it (that dries the tube).
5. Mirror low, chin up, eyes looking down.
6. Wiggle at the roots, then pull through to the tips. Use the wand's tip for corners.
7. Luxe says "2 coats". With tubing, add the second while the first is still wet, or it clumps.
8. Lower lashes: little or none.

**Night:** Lenses out first. Hold a warm (not cold) wet pad on closed eyes for 30 seconds, then press and slide downward. Cold water can pull lashes out.

**Pairs with:** a lash curler; lid primer; a lash serum at night.

**Avoid:** eye cream or castor oil on lashes before mascara; sharing tubes.

## 5. Who it's for

**Main target: women in their 50s, 60s and 70s.** What changes with age:
- **Lashes about 20% shorter and thinner** by 50–65, so heavy formulas drag them down.
- **Hooded lids** (extra skin folding over the lid). Lashes touch skin and print onto it.
- **Thin, creased under-eye skin.** Flakes sit in lines and look like dark circles.
- **Eyes water more and get drier.** Eye drops, stinging, new allergies.
- **Poorer close-up sight**, cataracts, shaky or arthritic hands.
- **Less curl**; harsh black looks stark.

**Suits:** short, fine lashes; hooded or oily lids; people who hate scrubbing; lash-extension wearers.

**May not suit:** people with known allergies (no list to check); very watery eyes or all-day eye drops; anyone wanting brown; anyone wanting real lash growth (a serum's job); thick lashes wanting big volume.

## 8. What users say

**Biggest problems before:** decades of smudges; scrubbing off and losing lashes; stinging, watering eyes; lashes too sparse to show. Many had given up mascara.

**How it (or tubing) solved them:** stays put through heat, pools and tears; no rubbing to remove; no stinging; thin lashes look longer; a skinny wand helps shaky hands.

**Main complaints:**
- Luxe: needs effort or a proper remover to take off; small tube; no navy shade.
- Tubing rivals: clumps over a dry coat; flakes as the tube ages; lashes droop; too thin on very sparse lashes; smears in humidity; price.

**Other feedback:** reviewers are mostly older (72% of Thrive five-star reviewers giving an age are 55+). Many say $5–12 drugstore tubing works as well. Side effects were rare (5, all rivals).

## 10. What you can and can't say on camera

- **Can say:** "looks longer, fuller, darker", "didn't smudge on me after X hours", "came off with warm water in my test", "the brand says it contains conditioning peptides".
- **Can't say:** "heals", "grows" or "regrows lashes", "stops lash loss". These are drug claims under FDA rules.
- Don't repeat the brand's "clinical" figures (100%, 98%, "2,844 women") as fact.
- Don't promise "hypoallergenic" or "safe for contacts".
- Do tell viewers to patch-test, remove it nightly and replace it every 2–4 months.
