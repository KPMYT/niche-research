## 1. The basics from zero

### 1.1 What eyelashes are, and what they are for

An eyelash is a hair. It is made of the same stuff as the hair on your head, a tough protein called **keratin** (the protein in hair, nails and the outer layer of skin). Each lash grows out of a tiny pocket in the skin called a **follicle** (the root pocket a hair grows from). The lashes grow along the **lash line** (the edge of the eyelid where lashes grow), also called the **lid margin**.

**How many [M]:**

| | Number | Rows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper lid | about 90 to 160 lashes | 5 to 6 rows | (S5) |
| Lower lid | about 75 to 80 lashes | 3 to 4 rows | (S5) |

The lashes are spread over several rows "so that eyelashes can be shed without leaving unprotected gaps" (S5) [M]. Think of a picket fence with three fences one behind the other: if one picket falls, there is no hole.

**What they do [M]:**

1. **A shield.** Lashes protect the eye from "sweat, rain, light, dust, microorganisms, and other particulate matter" (S5).
2. **A wind guard.** A 2015 study (Amador and others, Georgia Tech) put fake eyes in a wind tunnel. Lashes about one-third as long as the eye is wide cut the dust landing on the eye, and the drying of the **tear film** (the thin layer of tears that covers the eye), by about half. Lashes that are too short do little; lashes that are too long funnel air *into* the eye (S10). [R] So very long false lashes are not "better" for the eye.
3. **A trip wire.** Lashes are very sensitive to touch. When something brushes them, you blink. [R] This is common knowledge; I did not pull a source for it.
4. **Part of the tear system.** Small glands sit at the base of the lashes. Dr Dray (a **dermatologist**, a skin doctor) names the **glands of Zeis** (they release an oily substance, sebum) and the **glands of Moll** (sweat-type glands), both probably helping to fight germs (Y6) [M]. Behind the lashes are the **meibomian glands** (the oil glands along the lid edge that put the oily layer on your tears), about 30 on each lid according to Dr Rupa Wong, an **ophthalmologist** (a medical eye doctor and eye surgeon) (Y14) [M]. Dr Dray: "they're basically a barrier protecting your eye from the outside world and they play a key role in the tear mechanism" (Y8) [M].
5. **Looks and feelings.** Lashes frame the eyes. Their "enhancement [is] documented as early as ancient Egypt", and losing them "has been associated with significant emotional and psychologic distress" (S5) [M].

**Why the skin there is special [M].** Eyelid skin is the thinnest skin on the body (Dr Rupa Wong, Y13; Dr Sarah Blackwelder, an **optometrist**, an eye-care professional who tests sight but is not a surgeon, Y16). Dr Dray: "your eyelashes are anchored in eyelid skin which is much thinner than the skin on the rest of your body" (Y6). [R] This is why things put on the lash line can irritate easily, and why rubbing matters.

### 1.2 How a lash grows: the life cycle

Every hair on the body goes through the same three stages. The difference with lashes is that the growing stage is very short.

| Stage | Medical name | What happens | How long, for an eyelash [M] (S5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Growing | **anagen** | The root is active and pushes the lash out, about 0.12 to 0.14 mm a day | about 4 to 10 weeks |
| 2. Switching off | **catagen** | The root shrinks and its blood supply is cut off | about 15 days |
| 3. Resting | **telogen** | The lash just sits there, finished, until a new one pushes it out and it falls | about 4 to 9 months |

**A kitchen-table picture [R].** Think of each lash as a loaf of bread in a bakery that only has its oven on for a few weeks. The loaf can only get as big as the oven time allows. Then the loaf sits on the shelf for months before it is cleared away and a new one starts. A head hair has its "oven" on for 2 to 8 years (S5) [M], which is why head hair can grow down your back while a lash stops at about 1 cm.

**Key numbers [M]:**

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Growth speed while growing | 0.12 to 0.14 mm a day | (S5); Dr Dray (Y6) |
| Longest a lash usually gets | "rarely… longer than 12 mm" | Dr Dray (Y6) |
| Normal lashes lost per day | about 1 to 5 | Dr Dray (Y6); Dr Rupa Wong (Y12) |
| Total life of one lash | "approximately 4–11 months" | (S5) [M]. Other sources give other figures: Dr Rupa Wong says "the natural lash cycle is about 60 to 90 days" (Y12) |
| Time to regrow after lashes are lost | "about three months" | Dr Rupa Wong (Y12) |
| What limits lash length | "eyelash length is limited by the short anagen phase" | (S5) |

**Note:** in one video Dr Dray says the growing stage lasts "4 to 10 months" (Y7), but in another she says "4 to 10 weeks" (Y6), which matches the medical review (S5). [R] "Months" was probably a slip of the tongue.

**What this means for any lash serum [R]:**

- At any moment, most of your lashes are resting, not growing. A serum can only work on lashes that are growing or about to start.
- That is why every serum, strong or gentle, needs **at least 1 to 3 months** before anything shows. You are waiting for new lashes to come through and grow.
- It is also why results fade **slowly** after you stop: the lashes you grew stay until their natural fall-out date, months later.

### 1.3 Why lashes get short, thin and sparse

The medical word for lash or brow loss is **madarosis** (loss of eyelashes or eyebrows). The doctors' rule: "identifying the underlying condition… is the mainstay of the correct management" (S6) [M]. In plain words, find the cause first.

| Cause | What happens, in plain words | Source and evidence [M] | Can a lash serum help? [R] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal aging** | Lashes get shorter, thinner and sometimes white or grey. | "Eyebrow and eyelash thinning and whitening can occur as a presentation of physiologic aging" (S5). In 179 healthy women, average lash length was 7.98 mm at age 22–29 and 6.39 mm at 50–65, about 20% shorter (my arithmetic); thickness 1.62 against 1.17, about 28% less (S8). | A conditioning serum may help lashes break less. It won't turn back aging. |
| **Menopause** (the time when periods stop, usually around 50) | Hormone changes are widely blamed for thinner lashes and brows. | **No peer-reviewed lash figures found.** Brand blogs say falling **estrogen** (the main female hormone) shortens the growing stage (S25, unverified). Viewers report it: "they all fell out during the menopause transition" (brows, @Katepwe, 33 likes, Y7). | Unknown. Not studied. |
| **Thyroid problems** (an underactive thyroid gland, "hypothyroidism") | The body's "engine" slows down, hair grows slower. The outer third of the eyebrow often thins. | "Hypothyroidism can present with loss of the lateral third of the eyebrow… Severe hypothyroidism has also been reported to present with eyelash alopecia" (S5). Dr Dray: it "can slow down the rate of hair growth" (Y6). | No. See a doctor; treat the thyroid. |
| **Rubbing the eyes** (allergies, hay fever, tiredness) | Rubbing snaps lashes and irritates the roots. | Dr Dray: "that chronic rubbing can lead to breakage and impair the lash growth" (Y6). | A little, by conditioning. Stopping the rubbing matters more. |
| **Lash extensions** (single fake lashes glued one by one onto your own) | The weight pulls on your own lashes; glue can cause allergy; dirty build-up at the lash line causes inflammation. | EyeWiki (the American Academy of Ophthalmology's wiki) lists "traction alopecia" (lashes lost to the pull), allergic blepharitis (eyelid edges inflamed by an allergy) and keratoconjunctivitis (the surface of the eye inflamed by an allergy) (S19). Dr Rupa Wong: heavy or badly placed extensions "can cause a lot of weight onto your lashes and then cause them to break and fall off"; but "I couldn't find a single study about lash extension and whether or not it inhibits your lash growth" (Y12). | Possibly, during a break from extensions. |
| **Lash lifts and tints** (a lift is a "perm" for lashes; a tint is a dye) | Lift chemicals break the bonds inside the hair to reshape it, which may weaken it. | Dr Rupa Wong: "it's likely that it could [cause breakage]… but again nobody does these studies" (Y13). She also says "avoid things like lash tints lash lifts and extensions those are all going to cause lash drop out eventually" (Y1, opinion). | Possibly conditioning; no proof. |
| **Lash curlers** (a small hand clamp that squeezes lashes so they bend upward) | Squeezing bends and snaps lashes. Metal curlers can contain nickel, a common allergy. | Dr Dray: "it can cause direct lash breakage as well as slowing the lash growth cycle down", and about nickel: "your eyelid skin is definitely not going to like that" (Y6). | A little. Use the curler gently or not at all. |
| **Rough mascara removal** (**mascara** is the black or brown paint brushed onto lashes for the day) | Scrubbing at waterproof mascara (a kind that resists water and is hard to take off) pulls lashes out. | Dr Dray cites a paper suggesting that "in the case of waterproof mascara an oil-based cleanser might be a better option in terms of lash loss" (Y6). | Indirectly: the brand says users notice lashes "fall out less during makeup removal" (S3, a brand claim). |
| **Blepharitis** (inflamed, crusty eyelid edges) and **Demodex** (tiny mites that live in lash roots) | Blocked oil glands and inflammation damage the roots, so lashes fall out or grow short and brittle. | Dr Rupa Wong: blocked oil glands "can actually cause something called madarosis which is lash loss"; "if that lash root is dead because of… blepharitis… it doesn't matter what lash serum you use" (Y1). Mites get more common with age: in 1,499 Warsaw eye patients, the infected share by age was 8% (age 1–25), 36% (26–40), 50% (41–55), 67% (56–70), 77% (over 70) (S11). | **No.** Treat the lids first (warm compresses, lid cleaning, a doctor's treatment). An oily serum can even make it worse (Y1). |
| **Rosacea** (a skin condition with facial redness) and **eczema** (dry, itchy, inflamed skin) | Both can inflame the lid edge. | Dr Dray: rosacea patients "are at risk for developing a blepharitis… that of course can lead to lash breakage" (Y6); eczema can thin the outer brows (Y7). | No. Treat the skin condition. |
| **Stress shedding** ("telogen effluvium") | About 3 months after a shock to the body (surgery, fever, a virus, big weight loss, emotional stress) many hairs drop at once. | Dr Dray: "It also can lead to loss of more eyelashes", and "once that problem is controlled… the hair growth will go back to normal" (Y6). | Not needed; it recovers by itself. |
| **Medicines** | Some drugs shift the hair cycle. | Dr Dray names starting or stopping birth-control pills, and prednisone (a steroid) (Y7). A German viewer, on a video about lashes over 60 (her age is not stated), lost most of her lashes years ago after taking medicine for a fungal infection; they regrew, "but unfortunately they have also become thinner since then" (@Florence-c1f, 9 likes, Y11, my translation). | Unknown. Ask the prescribing doctor. |
| **Chemotherapy** | Cancer drugs attack fast-growing cells, including hair roots. | Common chemotherapy drugs (taxanes, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide) cause loss "approximately 1 week to 1 month after initiation". | **Trichotillomania** (a habit of pulling out hair) | Lashes pulled out, often in patches of different lengths. | (S5); Dr Dray describes habit-reversal therapy (Y6). | No. The pulling has to stop. |
| **Nutrition** | Real deficiencies (rare) can thin hair. | Dr Dray: "in the absence of a confirmed deficiency… don't waste your time money efforts on supplements for lash growth" (Y6). A viewer: "I was losing my eyelashes, but it has improved since I got my Vit D level up" (@PanamaRose, 156 likes, Y9). | No. |

**Warning signs that need a doctor, not a serum [R, based on S5 and S6]:** sudden loss; loss in patches; loss on one eye only; red, crusty, itchy lids; brows thinning at the outer ends at the same time; lashes falling out with other new symptoms (tiredness, weight change, hair loss elsewhere).

### 1.4 What a lash serum is, and the two families

A **lash serum** is a thin liquid you paint along the upper lash line, usually once a night, to make lashes look longer, thicker or fuller over weeks. It is not makeup: it does nothing on the day you put it on.

There are two families. They work in completely different ways.

| | **Family A: prostaglandin-type ("drug-like")** | **Family B: peptide, vitamin and plant ("conditioning")** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A chemical copy of a body hormone-like substance, **prostaglandin F2-alpha**. The original is a glaucoma eye drop. | A mix of moisturisers, vitamins, small protein pieces (peptides) and plant extracts. |
| Examples [M] (S23) | Latisse (bimatoprost, prescription). Cosmetic ones: GrandeLASH-MD, Babe Essential, neuLASH (isopropyl cloprostenate), RevitaLash Advanced (DDDE) | **Luxe**, The Ordinary Multi-Peptide, GrandeLASH Peptide+, Babe Renewing, Vegamour GRO, Lashfood |
| What it does | Keeps lashes in the **growing** stage longer and wakes up resting roots, so lashes grow longer, thicker and darker. | Moisturises and coats lashes so they **break less** and stay on the lid longer. Any real effect on growth is unproven. |
| Evidence | Strong for Latisse (a trial for the **FDA**, the US government body that approves medicines; below). | "promising data, more studies are needed due to a lack of formal evidence" (S18, J Cosmet Dermatol 2024 review) [M] |
| Main risks | Eye colour change, darker lids, hollow eyes, red eyes and more (below). | Irritation or allergy, like any cosmetic. |
| Legal status in the US | Latisse is a prescription drug. The cosmetic ones are sold as cosmetics. | Cosmetic. |

#### Family A: how prostaglandin-type serums work, plainly

1. **The accident.** Eye doctors gave glaucoma patients eye drops that copy prostaglandin, to lower the pressure in the eye. They noticed the patients grew long, thick lashes. Dr Sam Ellis (dermatologist): "these eye doctors noticed… their patients being treated for glaucoma they developed these gorgeous gorgeous eyelashes" (Y2) [M]. **Glaucoma** = an eye disease where pressure inside the eye damages the nerve of sight.
2. **What it does to a lash [M]:**
   - It makes lashes "spend more time getting longer", and less time resting, "so you're actually going to see more hairs come up over time" (Y2).
   - It enlarges "the base of the hair called the bulb so the lashes grow out thicker" (Y2).
   - It "stimulate[s] production of the pigment molecule… melanin and so the lashes will also appear darker" (Y2).
   - It does **not** create new roots: "you don't get new hair follicles with prostaglandin analogs" (Y2).
3. **Kitchen picture [R].** If a lash root is a bakery oven that is normally on for 6 weeks, the prostaglandin keeps it on for longer and wakes up ovens that were off. Bigger loaves, more loaves.

**The proof: the Latisse trial [M] (S7, the official label).** 278 adults, 4 months, Latisse against a look-alike liquid with no drug in it (the "vehicle").

| Week | Lashes improved by at least one grade: Latisse | Same, vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 15% | 8% |
| 8 | 50% | 15% |
| 12 | 69% | 20% |
| 16 | **78%** | **18%** |
| 20 | 79% | 21% |

At week 16, Latisse lashes were 25% longer (vehicle 2%), 106% fuller (vehicle 12%) and 18% darker (vehicle 3%) (S7) [M].

Other Latisse evidence [M]:
- **After chemotherapy:** 130 people. A person counted as a "responder" only if two things happened together: the doctor rated their lashes at least one grade better, **and** their own score on a lash-satisfaction questionnaire rose by at least 3 points. At 6 months, 46.9% were responders on bimatoprost against 18.2% on the vehicle. In the second 6 months everyone got the drug: at 12 months the responder rate was 61.5% in the group that had stayed on bimatoprost, and 67.6% in the group that had switched from the vehicle to bimatoprost. These are two separate groups, not a range. Most common side effects: eye redness 16.7% and a scratchy eye surface ("punctate keratitis") 9.4%. The study was run with the drug maker (S9) [M].
- **Alopecia universalis** (total body hair loss): 41 people, once a day for a year: "slight, moderate, or complete eyelash growth in about 70.3% of patients" (S5).
- **Eyebrows:** a trial of 357 people with brow loss found fuller, thicker brows on bimatoprost than on the control (S5).
- **When you stop:** "Upon discontinuation of treatment, eyelash growth is expected to return to its pre-treatment level" (S7).
- **More is not better:** "Additional applications of LATISSE® will not increase the growth of eyelashes" (S7).
- **Older users:** "No overall clinical differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and other adult patients" (S7, section 8.5).

#### The side effects of prostaglandin-type serums, explained simply

| Side effect | Plain words | How likely, and does it go away? [M] |
|---|---|---|
| **Iris darkening** | The coloured part of the eye (the **iris**) turns browner. Most visible in blue, green or hazel eyes. | Latisse label: "likely to be permanent"; may take "several months to years" to show (S7). Dr Sam Ellis: "especially if you have blue or green eyes they can turn brown" (Y2). |
| **Darker eyelid skin** | Brown or grey shadow on the lids or under the eye, like a permanent eyeshadow or dark circle. | Label: "reversible upon discontinuation… in most patients" (S7). |
| **Red eyes** | The white of the eye looks bloodshot. Medical word: **conjunctival hyperemia**. | Label: under 4% in the trial (S7); 16.7% in the chemo trial (S9). |
| **Itching, irritation, dry eye** | Itchy, gritty, dry eyes. | Label: each under 4% (S7). An optometrist warns they "can cause permanent changes to the meibomian glands leading to permanent dry eye" (Y10, her opinion). |
| **Fat loss around the eye** | The padding around the eye shrinks; the eye looks sunken, the **lid crease** (the fold in the upper eyelid) deepens, the lid may droop. Medical name: **PAP**, "prostaglandin-associated periorbitopathy". | Reported after approval (S7). Dr Sam Ellis: "it is reversible so once you stop… within four to six weeks you can see a lot of reversal… not everyone gets complete reversal" (Y2). Dr Dray: mostly seen with glaucoma drops put *into* the eye; for store-bought serums "a theoretical possibility but I'm not aware of any cases" (Y4). Risk factors include "being of an older age" (Y4). |
| **Stray hairs** | Hair grows where the liquid runs, such as the cheek. | Label section 5.4 (S7). A viewer: "I grew dark hair on my upper cheekbone where all those tears w/ babe lash mixed in sat & dried" (@poweredbycinnamonmocha, 19 likes, Y1 comments). |
| **Lashes growing the wrong way** (**trichiasis**) | Lashes grow inward and scratch the eye. | Reported after approval (S7). "a short, sharp, stubby second row of lashes on the inside of my eyelids" (@pinkpeony5302, 737 likes, Y8). |
| **Swelling at the back of the eye** (**macular edema**) | Fluid in the centre of the **retina** (the light-sensing layer at the back of the eye), blurring sight. | Label: caution after **cataract surgery** (an operation that replaces the eye's cloudy lens) when the **lens capsule** (the thin bag that holds the lens) is torn, or with other risk factors (S7, section 5.6). |
| **Interference with glaucoma drops** | Using a second prostaglandin may weaken the glaucoma drop. | Label section 5.1 (S7). |

Note: The Budget Dermatologist quotes higher rates (red eyes 10–15%, lid darkening about 18%, iris change 3–8%) (Y15) [M]. She does not give her source; the official label figures are lower. [R] Use the label figures.

**The cosmetic copies [M].** Several cosmetic serums contain prostaglandin-like chemicals with long names. Doctors' tip: look for **"prost"** in the name (Y10, Y15). The common ones:

| Name on the label | Where found [M] |
|---|---|
| Isopropyl cloprostenate | GrandeLASH-MD, Babe Essential, neuLASH (brand pages, S23) |
| Dechloro dihydroxy difluoro ethylcloprostenolamide (DDDE, also "ethyl tafluprostamide") | RevitaLash Advanced (INCIDecoder, a free website that explains cosmetic ingredients; S23) |
| Methylamido-dihydro-noralfaprostal (MDN) | named in the EU safety opinion (S17) |
| Bimatoprost, norbimatoprost | Latisse; found hidden in some online serums (S18) |

- **Hidden ones.** A 2022 Japanese lab test of 64 serums bought online found 4 with bimatoprost, "three [of which] did not indicate their contents on their package labels", and 8 more with other prostaglandin-type chemicals (S18) [M].
- **The EU.** The EU's scientific safety committee concluded in February 2026 that none of these "can be considered safe for use in cosmetic products intended for promoting the growth of eyelashes and eyebrows". A ban was drafted and sent to the EU Council on 22 September 2026, with adoption expected in late 2026. It was **not yet law** on 2026-10-31 (S17) [M].

#### Family B: how peptide and "conditioning" serums work, plainly

These serums try to make the lashes you already have **healthier and less breakable**, so more of them reach their full length and stay on the lid until their natural fall-out date.

- Dr Rupa Wong (ophthalmologist): peptides "give strength to the lashes but not shown in scientific studies as far as i could find as to an actually enhancing lash growth it might just work by preventing your current lashes from breaking and not falling" (Y1) [M].
- She adds: "as far as i could find there are no scientific studies linking any of these ingredients… with enhanced lash growth" (Y1) [M], and "you're not going to see the tremendous growth that you do with the prostaglandins" (Y1) [M].
- Dr Dray (dermatologist) on The Ordinary's peptide serum: "I suspect that any benefits you might obtain… is in conditioning those hairs and reducing breakage" (Y7) [M]. Possible ways peptides might act: "stimulating the production of keratins… stimulating the dermal papilla [the growth centre at the bottom of the root]… or stimulating the release of important growth factors… however there are no formal studies on peptides for lash growth" (Y7) [M].
- Dr Sam Ellis is more positive: "there is anecdotal evidence that those work and some very small studies that they can be effective… I have tons of patients who have tried these and have found a lot of great success" (Y2) [M].
- **Safety:** Dr Dray: "you're not going to likely run into the risk… of changing the color of your eyes there's no concern that you would have orbital fat loss with these ingredients", but "there is always the risk of irritation [and] contact dermatitis" (Y7) [M]. **Contact dermatitis** = a red, itchy skin rash from something that touched the skin.

**Kitchen picture [R].** A peptide serum is like hair conditioner for lashes. Conditioner doesn't make your hair grow faster, but hair that doesn't snap ends up looking longer and fuller.

**What "looks longer" can honestly mean [R]:** lashes that used to break off at half-length now reach full length; fewer lashes lost when you take off mascara; slightly glossier, softer lashes that catch the light. That is real, but it is smaller than Latisse's effect.

**Why people are buying now [R]:**
1. Mascara and false lashes are a daily chore; a serum promises your *own* lashes.
2. Extensions are expensive and many women, especially older women with fine lashes, find they damage or don't suit their lashes.
3. Side-effect fear has grown, so "prostaglandin-free" has become a selling point in itself.
4. Cheap Amazon serums (US$13–30) let people try without a prescription or a $100+ bill.

## 3. How to use it, step by step

2. **Patch test.** An optometrist (Dr Sarah Blackwelder, Vibe Optometry) advises "testing it on the back of your hand… for about five to seven days… because sometimes they can be delayed onset reactions… as long as you don't have any type of irritation itching redness or scaling of the skin i would go for it" (Y10) [M]. **How long one tube lasts:** about 2 months at once a night (60 applications, S2). Replace the tube when empty or after the brand's limit, whichever is first: "replace old bottles every 2 months" and "a shelf life of 4 months" (S2) [M].

### 3.3 Tips for older hands and eyes [R]

Many viewers over 60 find fine work at the eye hard. A German woman over 70: "My hands aren't as steady any more; applying mascara is a challenge for me" (@brigitteschadeberg1584, Y11, my translation) [M].

1. Sit at a table and rest your elbow on it, so the hand doesn't shake.
2. Use a magnifying mirror and a bright light from the front.
3. If you wear reading glasses, a magnifying mirror often helps more, because glasses get in the way of the brush.
4. Look down into the mirror rather than straight ahead: the upper lid shows its edge.
5. Hooded lids: gently lift the brow with the other hand's fingertip to show the lash line.
6. If you can't see the lash line clearly, it is better to paint a little above the lashes than to poke at the eye.
7. Do it at the same time every night (after brushing teeth, for example), so it becomes a habit.

| Mistake | Why it's a problem | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Putting it on the lashes like mascara | The serum needs to reach the **roots** at the lid edge, not the tips. | Paint the lash line like eyeliner. | (S3) [M] |
| Using too much | Runs into the eye, stings, wastes product. | One dip, wipe the brush, both eyes. | (S3) [M]; [R] |
| Putting it on the lower lid too | More irritation risk. | Upper lid only. | (S3), (S7) [M] |
| Applying over mascara or eye cream | The serum can't reach the skin. | Clean, dry lash line first. | (S2) [M] |
| Contact lenses still in | Lenses may soak it up. | Lenses out. | (Y19) [M] |
| Applying twice or three times a night "to speed it up" | No proof it speeds anything up; more irritation. | Once a night. | (S7) [M]; [R] |
| Giving up after 2–3 weeks | Lashes need weeks to months to cycle. | Judge at 12 weeks with photos. | (S5), (S2) [M]; [R] |

**The brand's timeline [M] (S2, S3):**

| Week | Brand says |

**A realistic timeline [R], built from the lash cycle (S5), the Latisse trial (S7) and what users say about peptide serums (Y7 comments):**

| When | What may realistically happen with a peptide serum |
|---|---|
| Nights 1–7 | Nothing visible. This is the week to watch for stinging, redness or itching. |
| Weeks 2–4 | Lashes may feel softer. You may notice fewer lashes on the cotton pad when removing mascara. | Weeks 6–12 | The fair test. Compare photos. Expect, at best, fuller-*looking* lashes from less breakage and fewer gaps. For comparison, even the prescription drug only helped 50% of people by week 8 (S7) [M]. |
| After 12 weeks | If you see no difference in photos, it probably won't do more for you. |
| Long-term | It only keeps working while you use it. |

**What happens when you stop [R]:**
- Nothing drops out the next day. The lashes you have keep to their normal schedule and fall out over the following months.
- Any improvement fades gradually, over about one lash life cycle (several months).

**One warning from the comments [M]:** a German woman wrote that a hyped serum gave "extremely long lashes. But shortly after, they fell out and afterwards they were not as thick and long as before. It was the same for my daughter" (@UrsulaNRW, 15 likes, Y11, my translation). [R] She doesn't name the serum. The likely explanation: many lashes grown at the same time also reach their fall-out date at the same time.

## 4. Where it fits in a routine

### 4.1 The simple rule

**Lash serum = the last thing on the eyes at night, on a clean, dry lash line** [R, built from S2 and S3]. Mascara is for the day; serum is for the night. They never touch at the same time.

### 4.2 Night: what goes before and after

| Order | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Eye makeup remover | Oil-based cleanser or balm for waterproof mascara (Dr Dray, Y6) [M]; the brand prefers "oil-free" (S2) [M]. [R] If you use an oil cleanser, wash it off afterwards with a gentle face wash so no oil is left on the lash line. |
| 2 | Face wash | Gentle; rinse the lid edge with lukewarm water (Y6) [M]. |
| 3 | Lid care, if you have blepharitis | Warm compress then gentle lid massage, "about one to two times a day" (Dr Rupa Wong, Y14) [M]; lid wipes or foams as your eye doctor says. Do this **before** the serum. |
| 4 | Eye drops, if prescribed | [R] Ask your eye doctor how long to wait between drops and the serum. Not found in any Luxe instruction. |
| 5 | Toner, face serums, face moisturiser | Keep them off the upper lid edge [R]. |
| 6 | **Luxe lash serum** | Thin line on the upper lash line; dry 1–2 minutes (S3). |
| 7 | Eye cream or eye balm | Only after the serum is dry (S2) [M]. Keep it below the lower lashes and on the lid bone (the bony ridge under the eyebrow), not on the lash line [R]. |
| 8 | Sleep | The serum "works overnight" (S3). |

### 4.3 Morning

| Order | Step | Notes [R] |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wash face | Removes last night's serum residue. |
| 2 | Skincare, sunscreen | As usual. |
| 3 | Mascara (optional) | Once lashes are clean and dry. A conditioning or tubing mascara that comes off with warm water means less rubbing at night (pack 1). |

### 4.4 Using it with other lash products and treatments

| Product or treatment | Can you use them together? | What sources say |
|---|---|---|
| **Mascara** | Yes, mascara by day, serum at night. | Brand: "Wait until the serum has fully dried if applying additional beauty product" (S2) [M]. [R] The removal is what matters: gentle, no scrubbing. Waterproof mascara is the hardest on lashes at removal time (Y6) [M]. |
| **Lash extensions** | The brand says yes. | Brand: "can be used in conjunction with eyelash extensions since all our serums are oil-free, so they won't damage the extensions" (S2) [M]. [R] The list contains no oils, which fits. Dr Rupa Wong suggests a serum *between* extension sets: "you could do a lash serum if you want in between… just to make them a little bit stronger" (Y12) [M]. Doctors warn about extensions themselves (traction loss, glue allergy, dirt build-up): Dr Dray, "a very frequent complication of lash extensions is something called madarosis" (Y5) [M]. Clean the lash line daily (Y12). |
| **Lash lift** (lash perm) | Yes, but not on the same day. | Salon and brand advice: wait **24 hours** (The Lash Lounge; Silly George FAQ) or 24–48 hours (Divine Lashes) after a lift (S20, search summaries) [M]. | **Lash tint** (dye) | Yes, not on the same day. [R] | The FDA warns that permanent lash and brow dyes "have been known to cause serious eye injuries" (as quoted by Dr Rupa Wong, Y13) [M]. |
| **Lash curler** | Yes, gently. | Dr Dray: "put the lash curler down"; prefer plastic, nickel-free (Y6) [M]. |
| **Strip false lashes** | Yes, on separate days or after removing them. [R] | The glue is a common allergy (Y5) [M]. Never paint serum on top of glue. |
| **Contact lenses** | Yes, lenses out first. | (Y19) [M]; Latisse waits 15 minutes before reinserting (S7) [M]. |
| **Other lash serums** | **No, one at a time.** [R] | Especially never add a prostaglandin-type serum "for extra effect": you lose the whole point of choosing a prostaglandin-free one. |
| **Glaucoma drops** | Ask your eye doctor. | Luxe has no prostaglandin, so the Latisse interaction warning (S7, 5.1) should not apply [R]. The brand says use it under a doctor's care if you are treated for an eye condition (S2) [M]. |
| **Retinol, acids, strong eye creams** | Keep them away from the lash line. [R] | They can irritate the lid edge; the serum's own stinging would be harder to tell apart. |
| **Castor oil or other oils** | Not on the same lash line, if you wear extensions or get styes. [R] | Oils can clog lid glands and cause styes (Y1) [M]. |

### 4.5 Brows

- **Can it go on brows?** The Luxe Amazon listing and FAQ are about lashes. Luxe sells separate products for brows ("Luxe Eyebrow Growth Serum", "Luxe Lash and Brow serum") (S2) [M]. Whether the 3 mL lash serum is the same formula as those: **not found**.
- [R] Nothing in the ingredient list is unsafe for brow skin, which is tougher than lid skin. Users of other peptide serums often use one tube for both: "I also use on my eyebrows and am seeing excellent results" (@mikam8801, 321 likes, The Ordinary, Y17) [M].
- **Why brows thin [M]:** age (S5); underactive thyroid, which typically thins the **outer third** of the brow (S5, Y7); eczema (Y7); years of over-plucking ("missing it since the early 2000s thanks to over plucking", optometrist, Y10); menopause, by viewers' accounts (Y7 comments). A brow grows in a shorter cycle than head hair: growing 2–3 months, transition 2–3 weeks, resting 2–3 months (S5).
- **Evidence for brows:** only bimatoprost has a large trial (357 people, S5). Nothing for peptide serums on brows (not found).

### 4.6 What not to mix [R]

1. Two lash serums at once.
2. Any product containing "prost" in an ingredient name, if the reason for choosing Luxe is avoiding prostaglandins.
3. Lash-lift or tint chemicals on the same day as the serum.
4. Oil-heavy eye products on the lash line, if you are prone to styes or wear extensions.
5. A serum on an eye that is red, infected, has a stye, or has just had surgery.

## 6. Every problem the viewer has, one by one

Each problem: what it is, why it happens, how it feels (real quotes), what people tried and why it failed, and whether Luxe helps. Quotes are [M] with the video code and likes; "helps or not" is [R].

### 6.1 "My lashes are short and I can barely see them"

- **What:** lashes that don't show, even with mascara.
- **Why:** genetics (a short growing stage), plus aging: about 20% shorter by 50–65 (S8).
- **How it feels:** a 66-year-old German woman: "my lashes were too fine and they fell out very quickly [with extensions]… Lash serum… worked noticeably better and at times my lashes were really much thicker and longer again. But you have to apply it daily and that's a bit tedious" (@susannemirjan3959, Y11, my translation).
- **Tried:** mascara (clumps on few lashes), extensions (too heavy for fine lashes), castor oil ("didn't have longer lashes just greasy eyeballs", @sharischmidt4712, 862 likes, Y8).
- **Does Luxe help?** Maybe a little, by reducing breakage. It will not double length. Pair it with a lengthening mascara for the day.

### 6.2 "My lashes fall out when I take my mascara off"

- **What:** a few lashes on the cotton pad every night.
- **Why:** 1–5 lashes a day is normal (Y6); scrubbing waterproof mascara pulls out extra (Y6).
- **How it feels:** "If i have to scrub my eyes to get it off that is a deal breaker" (mascara-video comment, quoted in `01-mascara.md`).
- **Tried:** stronger removers, which mean more rubbing.
- **Does Luxe help?** This is where a conditioning serum is most plausible. The brand itself makes this its weeks 3–4 promise (S3). Also change the removal: oil cleanser, press and hold, wipe down (Y6). A peptide-serum user: "they are no longer, no thicker. Just all of them stay with me until the end" (@sebumpostmortem, 112 likes, Y7).

### 6.3 "My lashes are brittle, dry and break"

- **What:** lashes that snap halfway, uneven lengths.
- **Why:** curlers, lifts, tints, rubbing, age.
- **How it feels:** "I'm ok with it just conditioning and reducing breakage as that is one of my biggest issues" (@BookishBeautyLover, 44 likes, Y7).
- **Does Luxe help?** Plausibly: glycerin, vitamin E and the peptides are conditioners. Unproven, but it is what this kind of product is for.

### 6.4 "My lashes got ruined by extensions"

- **What:** after extensions come off, lashes look stubby and gappy.
- **Why:** weight and pull ("traction alopecia", S19), glue allergy, dirt at the lash line; and partly the shock of seeing your own lashes again ("your brain just gets used to it", Dr Rupa Wong, Y12).
- **How it feels:** "the lash extensions didn't help much. My lashes were too fine and they fell out very quickly" (66-year-old, Y11). A lash tech: "I get clients who come to me with damaged natural lashes from other salon's lash extensions" (@PanDeSal.28, Y2).
- **Tried:** more extensions to hide the gaps, which adds more weight.
- **Does Luxe help?** It may support lashes during a break. Regrowth takes about 3 months whatever you use (Y12).

### 6.5 "I'm scared a lash serum will change my eye colour"

- **What:** fear of the iris going brown.
- **Why:** it really happens with prostaglandin-type serums and is "likely to be permanent" (S7).
- **How it feels:** "I have light blue eyes and I'm scared to ruin my eye color just for longer lashes" (@isie., 51 likes, Y2). "It changed my color from a pretty brown to a an almost black and it'll never change back" (@dk1472, Y9). "My eyes are naturally dark green… After using it for about six months, I noticed my eyes had darkened slightly" (@amitysantalucia6265, Y9).
- **Tried:** going without: "I decided my lashes were ok as is" (@Hopeful-w7h, 1.5K likes, Y8).

### 6.6 "Lash serums made my eyes look sunken and old"

- **What:** hollow upper lids, deeper creases, droop.
- **Why:** prostaglandin fat loss (PAP), on top of natural fat-pad shrinkage with age (Y4).
- **How it feels:** "I had very heavy hooded eyes and lost all the fat in my upper lid so became hollow with tons of loose skin. - **The other side:** "I actually like the slight fat loss… It's like a chemical blepharoplasty" (@modelmindsstudio, 68 likes, Y4).
- **Does Luxe help?** It avoids the cause. It will not refill hollows already there.

### 6.7 "Serums made my lids dark and my eyes red"

- **What:** brown or purple lids, bloodshot eyes.
- **Why:** prostaglandins make more pigment and widen blood vessels (Y2).
- **How it feels:** "it also made my eyes bloodshot. People noticed my red eyes more so then my new long lashes" (@susanwong6471, 32 likes, Y9). "People have asked me if I am wearing red eye-shadow" (@vfonseca91, Y8). "I stopped using Revitalash and brown shadows cleared" (@ILuvSlvrShuz, 47 likes, Y9).
- **Does Luxe help?** It avoids the prostaglandin cause. Any serum can still irritate; one reviewer had burning with Luxe (Y20).

### 6.8 "I don't know which serums are safe, and the labels hide it"

- **What:** confusion and distrust.
- **Why:** prostaglandin copies have long names, sit low on the list (Y10), and some products don't declare them at all (S18).

### 6.9 "I have glaucoma, or had cataract surgery. Can I use anything?"

- **What:** older viewers on eye drops or after surgery.
- **Why:** the Latisse label warns about both (S7).

- **Does Luxe help?** It removes the prostaglandin overlap. The eye doctor should still say yes first.

### 6.10 "My lashes are falling out on one eye only"

- **What:** one-sided loss.
- **Why:** often a local cause: blepharitis, mites, rubbing on one side, a glaucoma drop in one eye, or a medical condition (S5).
- **How it feels:** "I was losing eyelashes on one eye only. The eye doctor told me it was a result of getting older and offered no remedy. - **Does Luxe help?** Not as a first step. See an eye doctor to find the cause.

### 6.11 "I have red, itchy, crusty lids" (blepharitis)

- **What:** inflamed lid edges, flakes at the lash roots, gritty eyes.
- **Why:** blocked oil glands, bacteria, mites; more common with age, rosacea and makeup left on (Y3, Y14, S11).
- **How it feels:** "Please wash around your lash line 2x a day folks, blepharitis is no fun!" (@Rosie-xm7ry, Y6).
- **Does Luxe help?** No. Dr Rupa Wong: with blepharitis "it doesn't matter what lash serum you use" (Y1). Treat the lids first.

### 6.12 "I lost my lashes to chemotherapy" (or alopecia)

- **What:** lashes and brows gone, sometimes slow to return.
- **Why:** cancer drugs attack fast-growing hair cells (S5).
- **How it feels:** not found in the pulled comments for lashes specifically. The medical review notes loss "has been associated with significant emotional and psychologic distress" (S5).
- **Does Luxe help?** It is a cosmetic, not a treatment. The brand's "safe… including cancer patients" is a claim, not evidence. The only trialled option is bimatoprost, with a doctor (S9).

### 6.13 "The good serums cost too much"

- **What:** the serums people trust most are expensive, and a serum has to be bought again and again.
- **Why:** prescription Latisse is about $155 at average retail, and the strong cosmetic serums cost $49–100 a tube (7.1). Any effect lasts only while you keep using it (1.2, S7), so the cost repeats every month or two for as long as you want the result.

### 6.14 "Doing it every night is a chore"

- **What:** the serum has to go on every night, for months, and the result stops when you stop.
- **Why:** lashes grow slowly and in cycles (1.2), so nothing shows for weeks, and gains fade after stopping (S7, Y17). There is no reward on the day, which makes the habit hard to keep.
- **How it feels:** "you have to apply it daily and that's a bit tedious" (66-year-old, Y11, my translation). "I don't like applying the product… as someone who likes a minimal daily routine, I usually leave this out and save it for self-care days" (Vegamour user, reviews file, R-622).
- **Tried, and why it failed:** putting it on twice to speed things up (no benefit for Latisse, S7, and more contact with the eye); stopping and restarting, which blurs any result (3.4).
- **Does Luxe help?** No product fixes this; one 10-second step a night is the minimum. Linking it to tooth-brushing helps [R].

### 6.15 "My hands shake and I can't see close up"

- **What:** painting a thin line right at the lash roots is fiddly, and harder with age.
- **Why:** [R] steady fine hand work and close-up sight both get harder with age, and reading glasses get in the way of a brush at the eye. Hooded lids (5.2) also hide the lash line.
- **How it feels:** "My hands aren't as steady any more; applying mascara is a challenge for me" (Y11, my translation). "It's hard to get close to the lash line without getting it in my eye. I usually have to use a steady hand like I'm applying liquid liner" (Vegamour user, reviews file, R-622).
- **Tried, and why it failed:** giving up eye makeup altogether, or using a curler instead, which can snap lashes (1.3).
- **Does Luxe help?** The fine brush is like a liquid eyeliner, which needs a steady hand [R]. A German buyer found it "doable even for beginners" (L-32). See the tips in 3.3.

### 6.16 "My brows have thinned too"

- **What:** sparse brows, often missing at the outer ends.
- **Why:** age, thyroid (outer third), eczema, over-plucking (4.5).
- **How it feels:** "I'd love to have my eyebrows back…..they all fell out during the menopause transition" (@Katepwe, 33 likes, Y7). "My daughter plucked my brows 7 years ago and with age I have lost my tails my eyebrows are very sparse" (reviews file, Y-211).

A strong brow serum that later caused itching: "My RevitaBrow worked well for over a year, until I began to have very itchy side effects" (reviews file, Y-212).
- **Does Luxe help?** Not sold for brows on Amazon; Luxe has separate brow products. Check the thyroid if the outer third is going (S5).

### 7.3 When to pick Luxe, and when something else [R]

**Pick Luxe when:**
1. You want a prostaglandin-free serum and the cheapest easy Amazon option with a short ingredient list.
2. Your main problem is lashes breaking or falling out at removal.
3. You have light eyes, hollow or hooded lids, glaucoma drops or past eye surgery (with your eye doctor's OK).
4. You want a fine eyeliner-type brush.

**Pick something else when:**
1. You want the strongest proven growth and accept the risks: **Latisse**, through a doctor.
2. You want the best-documented cheap peptide serum, or one for brows too: **The Ordinary**.
3. Your lash loss is sudden, patchy, one-sided or medical: **a doctor**, not a serum.
4. You want an effect today: **mascara**, or a **lift** if you accept the chemicals.
5. You reacted to Luxe on a patch test: a different, short-list peptide serum, after your skin settles.

## 11. Safety, and what you can and can't say on camera

**Safety (short) [R, built on the sources shown]:**

1. Patch-test for about a week before using it on the lash line (Y10).
2. Upper lash line only; keep it out of the eye. If it gets in, "rinse your eyes thoroughly with water" (S2).
3. Stop if it burns, stings, itches or reddens, and don't push through (Y20).
4. Contact lenses out first (Y19).
5. Ask your eye doctor first if you use glaucoma drops, have had eye surgery, or are being treated for any eye condition (S2, S7).
6. Pregnant or breastfeeding: ask your doctor (S2).
7. Sudden, patchy or one-eye lash loss, or red crusty lids: see a doctor, not a serum (S5, Y1).
8. Don't share the tube; replace it after 2 months (S2).
9. Check the box ingredients for any name with "prost" (Y10).

**Before saying "prostaglandin-free" [R]:** photograph the ingredient list printed on the box first. Hidden prostaglandins have been found in some online serums (S18), and the Amazon box's list was unreadable in both review videos (2.4).

**Two more facts that limit what can be said [M]:** no published study backs the "4 weeks", "70% more length" or "clinically proven" claims (none found, S1-S4); and luxe.com advertises rosemary and magnolia extracts, which are not on the 10-item ingredient list (S2, S3), so don't name them as ingredients.

**Can't say (drug-type claims), and what to say instead [M for the FDA rule, S16]:** the FDA treats claims that a product will "restore hair growth" as drug claims (S16).

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Grows your lashes", "regrows", "stimulates growth", "restores natural growth" | "Helps lashes look longer and fuller" |
| "Stops lash loss", "treats thinning", "for eyelash illness", "for chemo or alopecia" | "Conditions lashes so they're less likely to break" |
| "Clinically proven", "70% longer", "results in 4 weeks guaranteed" | "Give it 8 to 12 weeks" |
| "Zero side effects", "safe for everyone", "safe for cancer patients" | "Patch-test first and stop if it stings" |
| "Works like Latisse" | "It's not Latisse; it's the gentle, prostaglandin-free kind" |
