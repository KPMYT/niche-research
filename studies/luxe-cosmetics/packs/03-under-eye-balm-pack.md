## The whole pack on one page

[M: Chopra 2015, PMID 26508650; Hwang 2006, PMID 16432408]
2. **"Tired eyes" is not one problem. It is up to six.** They are brown pigment, blue or purple blood vessels, shadow from a hollow, puffiness from fluid, bags from fat, and crepey, dry, lined skin. 3. **A balm works on the top layer only.** It can make dry, crepey skin smoother for some hours, help concealer sit better and, with the right ingredients, soften fine lines a little over weeks. 4. **The Luxe balm is a twist-up stick**, like a large lip balm, sold for under the eyes. The brand names five "actives". ## 1. The basics from zero

### 1.1 What an under-eye product is, and what it is for

An under-eye product is a cream, gel, serum, patch or balm that is made to be gentle enough for the skin around the eyes. People buy them for six reasons:

1. The skin there feels **dry or tight**.
2. It looks **crepey**: finely crinkled, like crepe paper or tissue paper that has been crumpled and smoothed out.
3. It has **fine lines** and **crow's feet** (the lines that fan out from the outer corner of the eye when you smile).
4. It looks **dark** (dark circles).
5. It looks **puffy** in the morning.
6. **Concealer** (the skin-coloured makeup used to hide dark circles) goes into the lines and looks cakey.

**Do you need a special eye product?** Two dermatologists (skin doctors) say plainly: no. You can use your face moisturiser around your eyes. [M]
- Dr Dray, dermatologist: "The most important takeaway point from this video is that you don't need an eye cream. The moisturizer you use on your face can be used to moisturize the skin around your eyes." (YouTube si9l1VZtzvQ, "The Ultimate Guide To Eye Creams 2025", 421,455 views)
- Dr Sam Ellis, dermatologist: "As a dermatologist, I definitely think you can get away with not using a dedicated eye cream." (XKROvYsp0QM, 148,367 views)

**Why people still buy them [R]:**
- Eye products are usually made without the things that sting, like fragrance and strong acids.
- They come in textures and shapes that are easy to use on a small area, like a stick or a metal roller.
- They are a reminder to look after that area.

Dr Dray: "there are eye creams out there formulated with ingredients that might improve those concerns." [M, si9l1VZtzvQ]

### 1.2 Why under-eye skin is different (the thinnest skin on the face)

Think of the skin on your cheek as a thick winter coat and the skin under your eye as a silk scarf. The scarf is thin, light and moves a lot. You can see what's underneath it, and it creases easily. [R]

| Fact | What it means | Source |
|---|---|---|

That is about 0.76 mm, roughly the thickness of 7 sheets of printer paper. The thickest, on the lower side of the nose, was 1,969 µm, about 2.6 times thicker. ### 1.3 What is under the skin: the layers, in plain words

To understand dark circles, bags and hollows, you need to know what sits under the thin skin. Picture the under-eye as a shelf. From the front to the back: [R, built on Dr Amir Karam Z1P5oKFQokw and Dr Michael Chua nj_VYMVtKrQ]

| Layer, front to back | Plain description | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Skin** | The thin silk scarf. | It shows everything underneath. It gets crepey, dry and lined. |
| 2. **Muscle** (orbicularis oculi) | A ring-shaped muscle round the eye that closes it and makes you squint. It is reddish-purple. | Seen through thin skin, it can look like a dark circle. Doctorly: "that reddish-brown tinge of the muscle can also create a tinge" (74eZe8KsHsw) [M] |
| 3. **Blood vessels** | Small veins and capillaries in and under the skin. | Through thin skin they look blue or purple. These are "vascular" dark circles. |
| 4. **The septum** | A thin sheet of tissue, like a wall or a hammock, that holds the eye's fat in place. | When it weakens with age or genetics, the fat behind it bulges forward. That is a bag. Dr Karam: "think of it as like a wall" (Z1P5oKFQokw) [M] |
| 5. **Orbital fat** | Fat cushions around the eyeball. They are there to protect it. | When it bulges forward you get **fat-pad bags**. |
| 6. **The orbital rim** | The bony edge of the eye socket that you can feel under your eye. | Products should stay on this bone, not above it. With age, the opening of the socket gets wider (section 5.3). |
| 7. **Cheek fat** (the SOOF, "sub-orbicularis oculi fat") | The fat cushion where the eyelid meets the cheek. | It shrinks with age, so a groove appears. That's the **hollow**. |
| 8. **Tear trough** | The groove running down and out from the inner corner of the eye. It is held down by a ligament (a tight band joining skin to bone). | When it deepens it makes a shadow. Dr Chua: "the tear trough which is the ligamentous attachment of skin to muscle and bone on the nasal side of the eyelid" [M] |
| 9. **Lid-cheek junction** | Where the lower lid meets the cheek. | Dr Karam: in youth it is short; with age "the distance increases", so eyes look hollow and tired (Z1P5oKFQokw) [M] |

### 1.4 How an under-eye product works: the science in kitchen-table words

Every under-eye product does one or more of four jobs. [R]

**Job 1: put water in and keep it in (moisturising).** This is the main thing a balm does. Moisturisers use three kinds of ingredient:

| Kind | What it does | Kitchen comparison | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Humectant** | Pulls water into the top layer of skin and holds it there. | A sponge. | Hyaluronic acid (sodium hyaluronate), glycerin |
| **Emollient** | Fills the tiny gaps between dry skin flakes so the surface feels soft and smooth. | Butter smoothed into a cracked cake. | Plant oils (argan), esters (lab-made oils, light and silky), squalane |
| **Occlusive** | Lies on top like a thin film and stops water escaping. | A lid on a saucepan, or cling film. | Waxes, petrolatum (Vaseline), shea |

A balm stick is mostly emollient and occlusive (oils and waxes), so it is very good at the "lid" job. [R; see 1.5 for what rival sticks are made of]

Why moisture alone makes lines look smaller: when the top layer of skin is full of water it swells slightly, like a raisin soaked in water. Fine "dehydration lines" flatten. Dr Dray: a glycerin protectant helps "just by improving water content in the skin, plumping up the outermost layer, smoothing out those little dehydration lines." [M, si9l1VZtzvQ] **This effect is real but temporary.** When the water leaves, the lines come back. A commenter put it plainly: "Remember any "difference" will last a few minutes" (@Phyllida-r7n, 26 likes, hAc7jewvVWE) [M].

**Job 2: slowly change the skin over weeks (the "actives").** Some ingredients send signals to skin cells. Niacinamide, for example, slows the transfer of brown pigment into the surface cells. Retinoids speed up cell renewal and collagen building. These take weeks to months. [M for niacinamide: Dr Dray, si9l1VZtzvQ, "it disrupts the transfer of pigment packets"]

**Job 3: trick the eye (optical effects).** Light-reflecting particles, tints, film-formers that tighten as they dry, and cooling. They work in minutes and wash off. Dr Sam Ellis on "instant" eye creams: "as they dry, the molecules in the product create like a little net or a film that brings the skin together and tightens it... that effect is completely temporary." [M, XKROvYsp0QM]

**Job 4: cool and de-puff.** Cold makes blood vessels narrow, so less fluid leaks out and swelling goes down for a while. A cold stick or metal tip helps a little. Dr Dray: "Simply cool temperatures... can help vasoconstrict and push that fluid back." [M, si9l1VZtzvQ]

**What no cream can do: reach the deep layers.** The skin's outer layer (the "corneal layer", the dead top cells) is a strong barrier. A well-known rule says a molecule must weigh less than about 500 daltons (a unit for the weight of molecules) to get through. "Larger molecules cannot pass the corneal layer." [M: Bos & Meinardi 2000, PMID 10839713] Collagen and DNA fragments are far bigger than that. And even small molecules don't travel down to the fat pads or the bone. That is why every doctor in section 6 says creams can't fix fat bags or hollows. [R]

### 1.5 What a "multi-balm stick" is, and the Korean stick trend

**What it is.** A solid balm in a twist-up tube, like a big lip balm or a glue stick. You rub it directly onto the skin. It is solid at room temperature and softens with the warmth of the skin. [R]

**What it is made of.** Luxe's full list is not published. But the rival sticks publish theirs, and they show what such sticks are mostly made of. [M: medicube.us and drmelaxin.us product pages, saved 2026-10-31 in `data/eye-balm/rivals/`]
- **Medicube PDRN Pink Collagen Volume Multi Balm** starts: "Cetyl Ethylhexanoate, Synthetic Wax, Polyglyceryl-2 Triisostearate, Diisostearyl Malate, Microcrystalline Wax, Hydrogenated Polyisobutene, Octyldodecanol, Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Euphorbia Cerifera (Candelilla) Wax..."
- **Dr.Melaxin Cemenrete Calcium Multi Balm** starts: "Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride, Polyglyceryl-2 Triisostearate, Synthetic Wax, Microcrystalline Wax, Octyldodecanol, Triethylhexanoin, Glycerin, Dipropylene Glycol, Water..."
- **In plain words [R]:** the first ingredients, which make up most of the stick, are **oils and waxes**. Water comes late in the list. The "star" ingredients (adenosine, collagen, PDRN) come far down the list, so they are present in small amounts. A Luxe buyer described hers as "a more solid version of vaseline" (@debbiecook3203, video hVadCZUeLzg; from the review helper's pull) [M]. That fits a wax-and-oil base.

**Why sticks became popular [M unless marked]:**
1. **The Korean "pink stick".** Kahi Wrinkle Bounce Multi Balm took off in Korea around 2021 to 2022 through product placement in K-dramas (Korean TV dramas). Cassandra Bankson, medical esthetician (a skin-care specialist trained to work alongside doctors, not a doctor): "that pink stick that you see everywhere ... took off back in 2022" (LmP5MWmL8cs, 111,788 views). She says its "sales literally went from about a hundred thousand" to "12 million" (the captions write "bombs", probably "balms"). She gives no source for those figures.
2. **"Functional cosmetics" in Korea.** Korea's food and drug regulator (the MFDS) lets products with approved ingredients make official anti-wrinkle claims. Adenosine is one of those ingredients, reportedly at 0.04% (secondary sources only: dermfnd.org, mirai-skin.com, buybeautykorea.com, via WebSearch). The same secondary sources also list retinol, retinyl palmitate and polyethoxylated retinamide (two other lab-made forms of vitamin A) as approved anti-wrinkle ingredients. So "adenosine is the only one" (a Luxe claim) looks wrong [R]. The official MFDS list was not pulled.
3. **The 2025–2026 TikTok Shop and Amazon wave.** Dr.Melaxin's calcium stick is labelled "[TikTok Bestseller]" on its own shop. Medicube's PDRN pink stick was added to its US shop in July 2026.
4. **Ads that promise too much.** The stick wave came with "Botox in a stick" and "filler alternative" ads. They drew a backlash, like James Welsh's "THE BIGGEST SKINCARE SCAM ON TIKTOK?! Who Are Dr Melaxin?" (TybFB4gyKcg, 403,792 views).
5. **The PDRN fashion.** PDRN (salmon DNA) is the 2026 ingredient of the moment. The earlier pads report found PDRN searches up 609.8% year on year (Spate, a company that tracks beauty search trends, via `02-pdrn-toner-pads.md`). Luxe's newer pack says "PDRN extract".
6. **Luxe's timing.** The stick was first published on the luxeresearchlab.com shop on 2 March 2026 (that listing was created on 3 December 2025); a second listing was created on 15 March 2026 [M: shop product data, `data/reviews/eye-balm/luxeresearchlab-products-2026-10-31.json`]. The first independent blog review is dated 8 April 2026 (earlier report §1.2). It is a late entry in the stick wave. [R]

**Why people like sticks [R, with quotes where marked]:**
- **No fingers in a jar.** You hold the tube.
- **Easy to carry** in a handbag. You can touch up over makeup.
- **Easy for stiff or shaky hands**, and with poor near vision you don't need to see a tiny dab of cream.
- **Many uses.** The same stick goes on lips, smile lines, the neck and dry patches. Kahi demo: "you can literally use it all over your face and you can use this after makeup" (DHz_sw_YMTI, 25,594 views) [M].

**Downsides of sticks [R, with quotes where marked]:**
- **Rubbing.** Pressing a stick across thin skin tugs it. Doctors warn against rubbing the eye area (section 6.8).
- **Hygiene.** The same surface touches your face again and again. Cassandra Bankson asked whether it is "sanitary to be rubbing basically a large skincare glue stick on your face" (LmP5MWmL8cs) [M].
- **Heaviness.** Waxy balms can feel heavy, and they can pill (form little rolls) under makeup if you use too much.
- **They "seal things in".** That is good for dry skin, but too rich for oily skin. Bankson: multi balms are "super oil filled... they seal things in" (LmP5MWmL8cs) [M].

#### Adenosine (the lead ingredient)

- **What it is.** A small natural molecule found in all living cells. It is part of ATP, the cell's "battery". In skincare it is usually made in a lab. [R]
- **What it does on skin.** It is used as an anti-wrinkle ingredient. Lab work suggests it nudges skin cells (fibroblasts, the cells that make collagen) to be more active. That lab work was not pulled here, so I mark it [R]. What *was* pulled is a human trial. [M]
- **The evidence [M].** A blind, randomised trial (people were put into groups by chance, and they didn't know which product was which), with a dummy cream for comparison:
  - who and what: 126 women aged 45 to 65. Each woman used two of three products (an adenosine cream, an adenosine film or a dummy cream), one on each side of the face, twice a day for 2 months. So 84 women tried each product, and some women had no dummy side;
  - result: "Both adenosine-containing products led to significant improvements in skin smoothness in the periorbital area. Improvements were evidenced after 3 weeks";
  - the cream also softened frown lines between the brows;
  - funding: L'Oréal ran it.
  - Source: Abella 2006, Int J Cosmet Sci, PMID 18489289.
- **What strength.** Korea's approved anti-wrinkle level is reported as 0.04% (secondary sources; MFDS list not pulled). **Luxe's level: not found.** [M]
- **Kitchen comparison.** Like fertiliser on a lawn, not a carpet laid on top. It works slowly, if at all, and only with steady use. [R]
- **Side effects.** It is usually described as gentle. No eye-area irritation data was pulled. [R]
- **What this means for Luxe [R].** If there is a meaningful amount of adenosine in it, this is the ingredient most likely to give a small, real smoothing of fine lines after about 3 to 8 weeks of twice-daily use. The brand's line that adenosine "triggers collagen synthesis" goes beyond what the trial measured. The trial measured surface smoothness, not collagen.

#### Niacinamide (vitamin B3), named on luxe.com only

- **What it is.** A form of vitamin B3, one of the most-studied skincare ingredients. [R]
- **What it does.** It strengthens the skin barrier, calms redness, and helps even out brown pigment by slowing the hand-over of pigment from pigment cells to surface cells. Dr Dray: "it disrupts the transfer of pigment packets from the pigment producing cells and melanocytes to the surrounding skin." (si9l1VZtzvQ) [M]
- **The evidence [M]:**
  - **12-week face trial.** 50 women put 5% niacinamide on one half of the face and a dummy on the other, twice a day for 12 weeks. Result: "reductions in fine lines and wrinkles, hyperpigmented spots, red blotchiness, and skin sallowness (yellowing)", with better elasticity (Bissett 2005, Procter & Gamble, PMID 16029679).
  - **4-week eye-cream trials.** An eye cream with niacinamide, peptides and caffeine improved crow's-feet smoothness at 4 weeks (Kaczvinsky 2009, P&G, PMID 19735523).
- **For dark circles.** It helps only the **brown (pigment)** kind, slowly. It does nothing for blue vessels or shadows. Doctorly names niacinamide among the ingredients "that are going to fade hyperpigmentation" (74eZe8KsHsw) [M].
- **Side effects.** Some people find it stings. Dr Dray: "for those of you who are sensitive, you find niacinamide burns and stings, be aware of that." (si9l1VZtzvQ) [M]
- **For Luxe [R].** Its amount is unknown, and the sister shop's list leaves it out, so you can't count on it being there. At typical balm levels, expect a small effect on brownness over 2 to 3 months at most.

#### Hyaluronic acid / sodium hyaluronate (named on both shops)

- **What it is.** A sugar-chain molecule that the body makes itself. It holds a lot of water. Sodium hyaluronate is the salt form most creams use. [R]
- **What it does.** It is a humectant (sponge). It pulls water into the top layer and plumps it, so fine dry lines look softer at once. [R]
- **The evidence [M].** 76 women aged 30 to 60 put 0.1% HA cream around one eye and a dummy around the other, twice a day for 60 days:
  - every HA cream "demonstrated a significant improvement in skin hydration and overall elasticity";
  - only the small-molecule versions (50 and 130 kDa; kDa, kilodaltons, is a unit for the weight of large molecules) also made wrinkles measurably shallower.
  - Source: Pavicic 2011, J Drugs Dermatol, PMID 22052267.
- **A sticking point [R].** HA dissolves in water, and a balm is mostly oil and wax, so it is usually present only in small amounts in a stick. Its type and size in Luxe: not found.
- **Not the same as filler.** HA filler is the same molecule, cross-linked (chemically tied together, like knitting loose threads into a net) into a gel and injected by a doctor. Rubbing HA on the skin is nothing like filler. Dr Karam explains that natural HA "breaks down in about 24 hours" in the body, which is why fillers are cross-linked (Z1P5oKFQokw) [M].
- **Side effects.** Rarely irritating. One commenter says she is allergic: "For those of us with allergies to vitamin c and hyaluronic acid can't use any of the products you mentioned" (@JKayShill, 11 likes, eA9YIs-49sc) [M].

#### Bisabolol (named on both shops)

- **What it is.** A soothing oil-like compound first found in **German chamomile** flowers. It is now often made in a lab or taken from the candeia tree. The US Library of Medicine's chamomile entry lists bisabolol as one of chamomile's ingredients (LactMed, the library's database on medicines and herbs during breastfeeding; PMID 30000867, saved in `data/eye-balm/pack-sources/pm/abs3.txt`) [M]. The candeia-tree source is my background knowledge [R].
- **What it does.** It is used to calm redness and irritation, and helps other ingredients feel gentler. [R] Medicube's and Dr.Melaxin's sticks both contain it too [M: their INCI lists].
- **Evidence.** No trial specific to the eye area was pulled. Treat it as a "helper", not a fix. [R]
- **Side effects.** Chamomile extract can cause allergy in sensitive people: "Topical chamomile is a known sensitizing agent" (LactMed). Pure bisabolol is generally considered low-risk. [M for chamomile; R for bisabolol]

#### Vitamin E (tocopherol), named on both shops

- **What it is.** A fat-soluble vitamin and antioxidant (it stops "rusting" damage from sunlight and air). In balms it also stops the oils going rancid. [R]
- **What it does.** It protects oils and the skin surface, and softens. It is a supporting ingredient. Dr Dray mentions vitamin E only in passing, alongside vitamin C. [R]
- **Evidence on allergy [M].** At Mayo Clinic Arizona, 18 of 2,950 patients patch-tested over 20 years (0.61%) reacted to alpha-tocopherol: "Vitamin E appears to be a relatively rare contact allergen" (Adams & Connolly 2010, PMID 20646670).
- **For Luxe [R].** It is good for keeping the balm fresh and the skin soft. It won't change dark circles or lines by itself.

#### Argan oil (named on luxeresearchlab.com and on one claim image)

- **What it is.** An oil pressed from argan tree nuts in Morocco. It is rich in fatty acids and vitamin E. [R]
- **What it does.** It is an emollient: it softens and smooths dry skin and adds slip. [R]
- **The evidence [M].** 60 post-menopausal women all rubbed argan oil on one forearm for 60 days (half also ate it). The skin's elasticity readings improved where it was applied (Boucetta 2015, PMID 25673976). That was forearm skin, not eyelids, and there was no untreated comparison arm for the rubbed oil.
- **For Luxe [R].** It probably helps the stick glide and makes the skin feel softer. Plant oils can very occasionally irritate. It is a nut oil, so people with tree-nut allergies may want to ask a doctor before using it.

#### "PDRN extract" (current pack and Amazon title)

- **What it is.** PDRN means polydeoxyribonucleotide: fragments of DNA, usually from **salmon** (milt, that is, sperm). In clinics it is **injected** (the "salmon sperm facial", Rejuran). There are also plant "PDRN" versions. Which kind Luxe uses: not found. [M for definitions: earlier pads report `02-pdrn-toner-pads.md`]
- **The evidence [M]:**
  - **Injected:** a review of 9 small, low-to-moderate-quality studies (219 patients) found promising results for **injections** (Lampridou 2025, PMID 39645667).
  - **On the skin:** mostly unproven. Dr Dray in the pads report: "highly, highly, highly doubtful that these skincare products with PDRN do anything above and beyond moisturize the skin" (earlier report). The fragments are far too big to soak in (500-dalton rule, PMID 10839713).
- **Allergy note.** Salmon-derived PDRN could matter to someone with a fish allergy. [R]
- **For Luxe [R].** It is a fashionable name on the pack. Expect it to add nothing you can see.

#### "Collagen extract" (old pack, Amazon title)

- **What it is.** The protein that holds skin together, taken from animal or fish skin. "Vegan collagen" is usually a plant protein that imitates it. [R]
- **What it does on skin.** It forms a moisturising film on top. **It cannot get in and become your collagen.** Collagen is a huge molecule, and molecules must be under about 500 daltons to pass the outer skin (PMID 10839713) [M]. Only a lab study using fat bubbles (liposomes) on artificial 3D skin showed more collagen staying in the outer layers (PMID 37415450) [M].
- **For Luxe [R].** A moisturiser. Nothing more.

#### What is probably holding it all together (the base), my reading only

Every stick needs a base. Luxe doesn't publish one. If it matches its rivals (see 1.5), the base will be [R]:
- **Waxes** (candelilla, microcrystalline, synthetic): these make it solid. Candelilla is a plant wax; microcrystalline is a petroleum wax, like the wax in Vaseline-type products.

| **1–2 weeks** | Less flaky, tight or dry skin, *if* dryness was your main problem | A better-sealed barrier | [R] |
| **3–8 weeks** | Possibly slightly smoother crow's-feet texture | Adenosine (trial: smoother skin from week 3, kept up to week 8) | PMID 18489289 [M]; the Luxe dose is unknown |
| **4 weeks** | Possibly smoother fine lines from HA and niacinamide-type ingredients | Measured in eye-cream trials at 4 weeks and 60 days | PMID 19735523, 22052267 [M] |
| **8–12 weeks** | Possibly slightly less brown pigment | Niacinamide (12-week face trial) | PMID 16029679 [M]; only if Luxe has enough niacinamide |

**The brand's own timeline, for comparison [M]:** "Most women notice a visible difference within 7-10 days... Puffiness and dark circles typically improve first, followed by smoother texture and reduced fine lines over 3-4 weeks." [R] The order is the wrong way round for a balm. Texture is the first and most likely change; puffiness and dark circles are the least likely.

## 4. Where it fits in a routine

### 4.1 The order of steps

A rule that works for every routine: **thinnest to thickest, water before oil, sunscreen last in the morning (before makeup).** [R]

| Order | Morning | Night |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rinse or gentle cleanse | Remove eye makeup gently, then cleanse |
| 2 | Toner or hydrating serum (optional) | Toner or hydrating serum (optional) |
| 3 | Treatment serum for the face (optional, e.g. vitamin C) | Treatment for the face (optional, e.g. retinol). Keep it off the eye area or buffer it (4.3) |
| 4 | Face moisturiser | Face moisturiser |
| 5 | **Luxe balm under the eyes** | **Luxe balm under the eyes, last** |
| 6 | Sunscreen (mineral near the eyes) | — |
| 7 | Concealer and makeup | — |

Note: the second Luxe shop says to put the balm "before moisturizer". Either works. Under the eyes the balm can simply be the moisturiser, so there is no need to put face cream there too. [M; R]

### 4.2 What it goes with

| Goes well with | Why |
|---|---|
| A gentle, fragrance-free cleanser | Less irritation of the eyelids. |
| A hydrating serum with hyaluronic acid or glycerin, underneath | Water in, balm on top to seal. |
| Mineral sunscreen (zinc oxide) | Dr Dray: easier to tolerate on eyelids (si9l1VZtzvQ) [M]. Sunscreen sticks are handy near the eyes. Dr Dray: they are "less likely as you are applying it to run into your eyes" but "make sure you do a couple of coats" (Wu0-dqcm1wY) [M] |
| A morning caffeine serum or gel, for puffiness | The balm has no caffeine named. Caffeine goes first (it's watery), then balm. |
| A cold compress or chilled spoons, for puffy mornings | Cold first, then balm. |
| Colour corrector and a little concealer | The balm is a smooth base. |
| Sunglasses and a wide-brimmed hat | Less sun damage and less squinting. Dr Sam Ellis: "the other reason it's important to protect your eyes from the sun is so you're not squinting so much" (XKROvYsp0QM) [M] |

### 4.3 What not to mix, or mix with care

| Combination | Risk | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Retinol / retinal / tretinoin near the eyes** | Stinging, dryness, peeling, dry eye | Use the retinoid at night on alternate nights, or "buffer" it: balm first, retinoid on top. Dr Sam Ellis: "applying your moisturizer first under your eyes... and then layering the normal facial retinoid... the moisturizer underneath it will kind of act as a buffer" (XKROvYsp0QM) [M]. People with dry eye: skip retinoids near the eyes. Dr Sam Ellis: "for people who are prone to dry eye, I don't recommend putting a retinoid near your eyes" [M] |
| **Exfoliating acids** (glycolic, lactic, salicylic) and acid pads on the eyelids | Stinging, redness | Keep them on the face, away from the lids. Dr Ellis does say AHAs can be brought "up under your eyes... always stay over the bone" [M]. For beginners I'd say: don't [R] |
| **Another balm with retinol or essential oils** (e.g. Medicube's PDRN stick) | More chances of irritation | Use one balm, not two [R]. |
| **Instant-tightening creams** (film-formers) | Water-based products break the film. Dr Sam Ellis: "as soon as you add water to that ... it breaks that netting apart" [M]. A balm on top may also stop it setting [R]. | Use a tightener on its own on special days, not layered with balm. |
| **Eye drops** | Balm on the lids can get into the eye with the drops | Put drops in first and wait a few minutes before applying balm [R]. |
| **Contact lenses** | Balm on fingers smears the lens | Put lenses in with clean hands *before* the balm [R]. |
| **Lash serum** (e.g. Luxe's lash serum, upper lash line at night) | Two products crowding the lash line | Serum on the upper lash line; balm below the lower lashes, on the bone. Don't overlap [R]. |
| **Heavy powder over the balm** | Cakey, creased look | Little or no powder. |

### 4.4 Example routines

**A. The absolute beginner (any age) [R]**
- Morning: rinse face → balm under eyes → sunscreen.
- Night: wash face → balm under eyes.

**B. Woman over 60 with dry, crepey under-eyes, reading glasses and some dry eye [R]**
- **Morning**
  1. Rinse with lukewarm water and pat dry.
  2. A fragrance-free hydrating serum or face moisturiser on the face, not the lids.
  3. Luxe balm: one light swipe per side on the bone, tapped in with the ring finger, nowhere near the lashes.
  4. Mineral sunscreen (a stick is easiest), two coats around the eyes.
  5. If wanted: a peach corrector or tinted eye brightener tapped on the inner corner only, then a touch of concealer. Little or no powder.
  6. Sunglasses outdoors.
- **Night**
  1. Take off makeup gently: let the remover sit for 10–20 seconds.
  2. Gentle cleanse.
  3. Face moisturiser.
  4. Luxe balm under the eyes.
  5. If you use a retinoid: on the face only, 2–3 nights a week, and not near the eyes if you have dry eye.
- **Weekly:** one look in the same mirror and the same light, to judge honestly.
- **Why this shape [R]:** thin, post-menopausal skin dries easily (section 5.3). Retinoids near the eyes raise the odds of dryness and burning (PMID 42708560). Older women get dry eye more often: Dr Rupa Wong says "dry eyes unfortunately is more common in older women 50s 60s and those are the women that tend to be using the retinol" (N7Zl2keLo1M) [M].

**C. Puffy every morning [R]**
- Morning: cold compress or chilled spoons for 1–2 minutes → caffeine serum → balm → sunscreen.
- Night: less salt and alcohol in the evening, sleep with the head slightly raised, balm under eyes.
- Source for the habits: Dr Michael Chua, nj_VYMVtKrQ: "it also helps to elevate your head when you sleep"; salt "causes fluid retention particularly around your eyes" [M].

**D. Concealer wearer whose makeup creases [R]**
- Morning: balm → wait 2 minutes → sunscreen → concealer on the inner corner only → wait 5 minutes → wipe out the creases → only a little powder.

**E. Retinol user with lines [R]**
- Night A: balm under the eyes first, then a gentle eye retinoid on top (buffered).
- Night B: balm only.
- Morning: balm → sunscreen.

**F. Sensitive, allergy-prone or eczema-prone eyelids [R]**
- Patch-test first.
- Fragrance-free everything, and treat the allergies (antihistamine tablets or drops, after asking a pharmacist or doctor).
- Some dermatologists prefer plain, well-tested protectants here. Dr Dray: petrolatum, or a glycerin skin protectant such as "La Roche-Posay Cicaplast Gel" (si9l1VZtzvQ) [M].

### 5.2 How needs change with age, decade by decade [R, with sources in 5.3]

| Age | What usually changes under the eye | What helps most |
|---|---|---|
| 20s–30s | Genetic dark circles or bags show up if you have them. Puffiness after late nights, salt or crying. | Sleep, sunscreen, cold, concealer. |
| 40s | First crepiness and fine lines; hollows start; first loss of cheek fullness. | Sunscreen, moisturiser, a gentle retinoid if tolerated. Small amounts of filler for some people (Karam: "people in their 30s for the most part maybe early 40s"). |
| 50s (menopause) | Estrogen drop leads to drier, thinner, crepier skin. Fat bags often grow. The lid-cheek junction lengthens. | Rich moisturiser or balm, sunscreen, retinoid if tolerated, concealer technique. Procedures if wanted. |
| 60s | Thinner skin shows veins and muscle (more blue and purple). Bone round the eye recedes. Extra skin. Dry eye more common. | Gentle moisturising, eye-friendly products, a light hand with makeup, a medical opinion for bags or loose skin. |
| 70s+ | Very thin, fragile skin; loose lower lids; more visible oil glands and bumps. | Gentle, plain moisturisers; light tapping; medical help for lid problems (e.g. a lid turning outwards). |

### 5.3 What changes in the eye area after 50 and 60, and why

**1. The skin thins and dries, and menopause speeds this up.**
- After menopause, skin collagen content, skin thickness and bone mass each fell "1-2% per year" (Brincat 1987, Obstet Gynecol, PMID 3120067) [M].
- A second study by the same group found the fall in skin collagen was "preventable in women who were on sex hormone replacement therapy" (PMID 3828252) [M].
- Dr Dray: "especially in women around perimenopause, menopause. With the decline in estrogen, we lose some of our skin's water retaining ability" (si9l1VZtzvQ) [M].
- Dr Sam Ellis: estrogen "makes the skin supple. And when you have a drop in it, it causes crepey skin everywhere on the body, including the under eyes" (XKROvYsp0QM) [M].
- **In plain words [R]:** each year after menopause the skin's "mattress springs" thin a little more, and it holds less water. Under the eye, where the skin is already the thinnest, this shows first.

**2. Thinner skin shows what is underneath.** Blood vessels and the purple muscle show through more, so darkness looks bluer and purpler with age. Dr Idriss at 42: "Some of it is literally because of my skin is getting thinner under my eyes and the blood vessels are coming through" (c6G0kjKQNGw, 129,176 views) [M]. Dr Sam Ellis: "as that skin thins out, you then can see all the other structures that exist under the eye, like the fat pad and the blood vessels" [M].

**3. The fat behind the eye grows forward and the "wall" weakens.**
- An MRI study of 40 people found the soft tissue below the eye grew with age: 99 mm² at ages 12–29, 103 at 30–54 and 131 at 55–80. "The largest contributor to this size increase was fat expansion" (28, 31 and 43 mm²) (Darcy 2008, Plast Reconstr Surg, PMID 18766060) [M].
- Doctors also describe the septum weakening. Dr Michael Chua: "the tissue that's supposed to be keeping the fat in its place loses its tightness and strength and as it loosens that fat can herniate forward" (nj_VYMVtKrQ) [M].
- **In plain words [R]:** bags in your 60s are usually real fat, sitting forward. No cream can melt it.

**4. The cheek fat below shrinks, so a groove appears.** Dr Karam: the cheek fat pad (SOOF) "starts to diminish and as a result of that eyes start to look hollow". He describes the over-50 eye as "a combination of excess soft tissue and deficient fat": bags above, hollow below (Z1P5oKFQokw, 1,223,488 views) [M].

**5. The bone changes.**
- In CT scans of the same 96 adults taken about 11 years apart, the "Orbital aperture area (+91 mm2)" (the opening of the eye socket) grew with age (Sifil & Kahn 2026, Aesthet Surg J, PMID 40658792) [M].
- In 240 orbits, "The female bony orbit expands with age and is associated with a more anterior position of the globe" (the eyeball sits further forward) (PMID 35749164) [M].
- Dr Idriss: "our bone structure, the frame holding our face starts to get hollower and starts to slowly recede" (RVADMAuXNKM) [M].
- **In plain words [R]:** the "picture frame" of bone around the eye gets bigger, so the soft tissue sinks into it. That deepens the hollow.

**6. Extra, loose skin.** Dr Karam: "excess skin which is termed dermatochalasis again fancy word for just excess skin of the lower eyelid" [M]. The lower lid can also loosen and droop [R].

**7. Decades of smiling and squinting set the lines.** Crow's feet start as lines that come and go with expression and become permanent. Dr Dray: "those little wrinkles that you can see at first when you squint and then with time they just kind of seem a little bit more persistent" (a2Hot5ZsYU8) [M].

**8. Bumps become visible.** As skin thins, oil glands show through as a pebbly texture. Dr Dray: "as the skin thins out those globs underneath the skin those sebaceous lobules ... you can see them as a little bump" (Wu0-dqcm1wY, 299,999 views) [M].

**9. Dry eye becomes common, and products near the eye matter more.** Dr Rupa Wong: "dry eyes unfortunately is more common in older women 50s 60s" (N7Zl2keLo1M) [M]. Dr Alan of Doctor Eye Health: preservatives and retinol near the lids can damage "the oil glands in the eyelids" (hql6k88BKP8, 9,090,726 views) [M].

**10. New sensitivities appear.** Dr Sam Ellis: "it's not uncommon as you get older to develop sensitivities to certain things that you used to be able to tolerate on your eyes" (XKROvYsp0QM) [M].

**11. Practical changes [R].** Stiffer fingers, shakier hands and needing reading glasses make tiny dabs of cream hard. A stick can be easier. A magnifying mirror and good front-facing light help.

## 6. Every problem the viewer has, one by one

### 6.0 First, find out which problem you have: four simple home tests

Dr Idriss: "looking tired is a description and that is not a diagnosis" (c6G0kjKQNGw) [M]. These tests sort the problems.

| Test | How | What it tells you | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pinch test** | Gently pinch up a little under-eye skin between two fingers. | If the pinched skin is still brown, it is **pigment**. If it looks like normal skin, the darkness is a **shadow or hollow** (or vessels underneath). | Dr Dray: "if you pinch the skin up and the skin between your fingers looks like background skin, not hyperpigmented, guess what? It's likely hollows" (si9l1VZtzvQ) [M] |
| **Overhead-light test** | Hold a light straight above your face (or face a window head-on). | If the darkness disappears with flat, front-on light, it is a **structural shadow**. If it stays, it is **colour** (pigment or vessels). | Dr Idriss: "If all shadowing disappears, then you, my friend, have a structural issue" (RVADMAuXNKM) [M] |
| **Morning-versus-evening test** | Photograph your eyes on waking and at 6 p.m. | Worse in the morning and better by afternoon: **fluid puffiness**. The same all day, every day: **fat bags**. | Dr Michael Chua: "if you notice that your eye bags are always present then it's more likely an anatomic issue" (nj_VYMVtKrQ) [M] |
| **Stretch test** | Gently lift the outer corner of the eye, or look up. | If darkness turns more blue or purple and fades a little when stretched, it is **vessels or thin skin** [R]. | My reading, based on Doctorly's thin-skin explanation (74eZe8KsHsw) |

Most people have more than one. Dr Idriss: "most of us, myself included, have more than one" (c6G0kjKQNGw) [M]. In a Korean study of 100 people with dark circles, 35% were vascular and 54% mixed (pigment plus vessels) (PMID 26346687) [M].

### 6.1 "I look tired all the time" (the umbrella complaint)

- **What it is.** A general look of fatigue, made up of the problems below.
- **Why it happens.** A long lid-cheek junction, hollows, bags, darkness and crepey skin (sections 5.3 and 6.2–6.10).
- **How it feels [M].** Clinicians report patients worrying about "under-eye hollows (91%), dark eye circles (80%), and looking tired (60%)" (survey of 39 practitioners, PMID 38489829). Dr Idriss: "you have been personally victimized by the question, "Are you tired?" Despite the fact that you had 10 hours of sleep" (RVADMAuXNKM). The Luxe page itself leads with: "If you sleep 7 hours and still look tired, it's not the sleep."
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** More sleep, more concealer and pricey creams each fix at most one layer.
- **What this balm can do [R].** It fixes the "dry, crepey surface" layer, which is often the layer concealer sits on. It does not fix the others.

### 6.2 Dark circles: the brown (pigment) kind

- **What it is.** Extra brown pigment (melanin) in the under-eye skin. Sometimes it's a sharp line of darker skin, common in deeper skin tones.
- **Why it happens [M]:**
  - genetics: Doctorly, "A lot of people have this just genetically" (74eZe8KsHsw);
  - sun: "sun exposure with time ... can lead to basically tanning of this area, and a lot of people neglect to put sunscreen on the areas around their eyes";
  - rubbing, eczema and allergy, which leave dark marks: "allergies to your skin care products ... Allergy to your nail products, actually very common cause of eyelid reactions";
  - in deeper skin tones, "pigmentary demarcation lines" (Dr Dray, si9l1VZtzvQ).
- **How it feels [M].** "I have fought DARK circles ALL MY LIFE!! I just turned 60" (@kristengomerimhof4633, vQ1wyKafvWI, from `05` report). "I have hereditary dark circles and now eye bags/ lines at 48." (@dominiquedeangelo2616, 21 likes, QbvzKn9GG_w).
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** Thick concealer: covers the colour but shows texture (Wayne Goss: concealer makes skin "look more lined"). Expensive "brightening" eye creams: slow, and useless without sunscreen.
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Daily sunscreen**, tinted if possible (the iron oxides block visible light): "use a tinted sunscreen... Tinted because they contain iron oxides" (Doctorly).
  - **Brightening creams**, slowly: niacinamide, vitamin C, azelaic acid, kojic acid, tranexamic acid, alpha arbutin, retinoids (Doctorly; Dr Dray).
  - **Hydroquinone** only with a dermatologist: "you don't really want to use that longer than 2 months without really talking to a dermatologist" (Doctorly).
  - **Chemical peels and lasers.** The systematic review: "Various topical creams and chemical peels are useful in treating pigment-based POH" (PMID 32740208).
- **This balm [R].** It helps only if niacinamide is truly present at a useful level. It can't be checked, and one Luxe shop leaves it out. **Weak.**

### 6.3 Dark circles: the blue or purple (vascular, thin-skin) kind

- **What it is.** Veins and congested blood, and the purplish muscle, showing through thin skin.
- **Why it happens [M].** Thin skin: "underneath the eyelids, we have very prominent vasculature... blood vessels underneath this thin skin that causes a tint" (Doctorly). Allergies and congestion: Dr Sam Ellis, "you also can get a little bit of congestion of the blood vessels under the eyes, which makes dark circles worse". Ageing thins the skin further (5.3).
- **How it feels [M].** "Im 50, perimenopausal with blue/purple issue round the eyes" (@Luvbug64, eA9YIs-49sc, from `05`).
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** A skin-coloured concealer alone: blue shows through as grey. Brightening creams: they target brown, not blue.
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Colour corrector first, then concealer.** Kim Sullins: an "orange-based corrector... is going to cancel out those dark blue, brown undertones" (eA9YIs-49sc).
  - **Caffeine and vitamin K creams**, a little and temporarily: "caffeine-based creams that cause vasoconstriction of the blood vessels ... at least temporarily while these caffeine creams are on" (Doctorly). One small trial (11 women) of a 3% caffeine + 1% vitamin K pad found less darkness and fewer wrinkles at 4 weeks (PMID 25625116), but it was small and single-blind.
  - **Treat allergies**: an antihistamine (Dr Sam Ellis; Doctor Eye Health).
  - **Retinoids** to thicken the skin slowly: "they will thicken the skin in the area ... over long period of time" (Doctorly).
  - **Lasers** that target vessels: "If you want to eliminate it permanently, then laser that targets blood vessels" (Doctorly); Dr Idriss names the pulsed dye laser. The review: lasers are "mildly to moderately beneficial for both vascular and pigment types" (PMID 32740208).
  - **PRP** injections: Dr Sam Ellis says "It really helps with dark circles and improving the quality of the skin".
- **This balm [R].** **Poor.** Nothing in its named list targets vessels. A moist surface reflects light a little more evenly, which is a tiny optical help.

### 6.4 Dark circles: the shadow kind (structural)

- **What it is.** Not a colour at all. A dip below the eye catches a shadow, like a dent in a wall.
- **Why it happens [M].** Bone shape, a deep tear trough, loss of the fat pad, and bags casting a shadow below them. Doctorly: "the shadow coming from the orbital rim, the bony structure here to just cast a shadow underneath the eye... that could just be your lighting". Dr Idriss: "Think of your under eyes like a dent in a wall. You can paint over it... But if that dent is still there, you will always see a shadow" (RVADMAuXNKM).
- **How it feels [M].** "Hollowing under the eye is a big problem." (@FLgurl100, 18 likes, T-qO5qhE82s, from `05`).
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** Concealer: it lightens the colour but can't fill a dent, and it creases in the groove. Creams: they can't add volume.
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Lighting and makeup tricks.** Face your light: "If you take photos, you want your camera your light to be facing you" (Doctorly).
  - **Procedures:** "Soft tissue fillers and autologous fat grafting are most effective in treating dark circles due to volume loss" (PMID 32740208).
- **This balm [R].** **None** for the shadow itself.

### 6.5 Puffiness (fluid, worse in the morning)

- **What it is.** Swelling from water collecting in the soft tissue under the eye. It comes and goes.
- **Why it happens [M]:**
  - **Salt:** "a lot of salty food that's going to cause water to accumulate in your tissues leading to swollen puffy eyes" (Doctor Eye Health, hql6k88BKP8).
  - **Crying:** "the salt content of your tears ends up drawing water into the tissue" (same).
  - **Allergies:** "histamine ... cause[s] your blood vessels and your tissues to swell up" (same).
  - **Lying flat**, too little sleep, **alcohol**: Dr Michael Chua.
  - **Age:** "Fluid accumulates there" as support weakens (Dr Dray).
- **How it feels [M].** "I have pretty puffy lower bags... Looks like I'm keeping my bags packed for the duration of my trip on earth." (@rtbrain, 267 likes, hql6k88BKP8). "I've noticed that my eye bags tend to minimize and even go away when I do a lot of cardio and sleep right." (@hartsickdisciple, 5.5K likes, hql6k88BKP8): that it comes and goes is the sign of fluid.
- **What people try [M]:**
  - cucumbers, which only work by being cold: "it's really probably just the coolness of having a cold cucumber" (Doctor Eye Health);
  - tea bags (the caffeine helps a little);
  - expensive "de-puffing" creams.
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Cold.** "cold compresses will help shrink the blood vessels around the eye" (Doctor Eye Health); "put some spoons in the refrigerator" (Dr Chua). A viewer warns against frozen spoons: "the extreme cold on a metal spoon will actually freeze burn the surface, a cold spoon will suffice" (@petebailey1652, 95 likes, hql6k88BKP8).
  - **Head raised in bed.** Dr Chua: "it also helps to elevate your head when you sleep". A viewer: "sleep with your head elevated... Also put a cold wet washcloth on your eyes every morning for at least 30 seconds" (@alanmorris7669, 3.3K likes, hql6k88BKP8).
  - **Less salt and alcohol at night** (Dr Chua).
  - **Treat allergies**, and don't rub. "do not rub your eyes when you're having eye allergies it'll feel good at first but really it just makes things worse" (Doctor Eye Health).
  - **Caffeine products**, for some people: "Some people's puffiness will not be touched by caffeine" (Dr Dray).
  - **Exercise and sleep**, a lay observation: "my eye bags tend to minimize and even go away when I do a lot of cardio and sleep right" (@hartsickdisciple, 5.5K likes, hql6k88BKP8).
- **See a doctor when [M].** The swelling is under one eye only, painful, itchy, red, or new and lasting. Cleveland Clinic: "if you have a dark circle or swelling under just one eye, call a healthcare provider" (my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/23128-dark-circles-under-eyes, last reviewed 05/25/2022). Doctorly: puffiness "can be associated with fluid retention, thyroid diseases, then nothing that you do cosmetically is really going to help" (iwyLM5aJ7mE).
- **This balm [R].** **Weak.** No caffeine is named. The cool stick, if kept in the fridge, plus gentle tapping may help for an hour. The brand's claim that "Puffiness... typically improve[s] first" is not supported.

### 6.6 Bags (fat pads)

- **Why it happens [M].** Genetics and age. An MRI study showed fat expansion with age (PMID 18766060). Dr Karam calls them "pseudo bags", from a "weaker orbital septum" (Z1P5oKFQokw).
- **How it feels [M]:**
  - "I've had eye bags since I was in grade school. I think I'd have to have them cut out." (1.1K likes, iwyLM5aJ7mE)

### 6.7 Hollows and tear troughs

- **What it is.** A sunken groove from the inner corner of the eye down and out, sometimes all round the lower rim.
- **Why it happens [M].** The cheek fat pad shrinks, bone recedes, and the tear-trough ligament tethers the skin (sections 1.3 and 5.3). Some people are born with deep troughs: "some people are just born with deeper tear troughs" (Doctorly).
- **How it feels [M].** "Hollowing under the eye is a big problem." (@FLgurl100). "When I turned 48, my face just fell 😩 Bags, jowls, the whole 9!" (@RioGirl16, 31 likes, vQ1wyKafvWI; from `05`).
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** Plumping creams and "filler in a stick" balms. They can't add volume. The plumping is surface water.
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **HA filler, in small amounts.** A meta-analysis of 31 reports (2,556 people) gave a pooled satisfaction rate of 91.0%. Side effects: swelling 19.2%, bruising 18.4%, redness 7.1%, lumps 5.3%, blue tint (Tyndall effect) 0.9% (Liu 2024, PMID 37684413).
  - **Doctors' cautions on filler:**
    - Dr Karam: overfilled HA under the eye "becomes puffy and it actually becomes boggy and blue looking". He suggests "between a quarter to a third of a syringe of filler per side". Filler suits mostly "people in their 30s... maybe early 40s".
    - Dr Idriss: "a tiny amount can look beautiful in the right person... but too much of it can make somebody look puffy, heavy, or actually more tired" (c6G0kjKQNGw).
    - Dr Sam Ellis says she "often recommend[s] against" under-eye filler (XKROvYsp0QM).
  - **Fat transfer:** your own fat moved from the thighs or stomach. Dr Karam calls it "a permanent way to replace lost volume". Dr Idriss had nano-fat under the eyes at 42 (c6G0kjKQNGw).
  - **Treating the cheek** to support the area. Practitioners agree "that midcheek volume was critical when treating tear-trough depression" (100% of 39 surveyed, PMID 38489829).
- **This balm [R].** **None.** At most, a moist surface makes the groove's edges a little softer to look at.

### 6.8 Crepey skin

- **What it is.** Thin skin with very fine criss-cross wrinkles, like crepe paper. It is most visible when you smile or look up.
- **Why it happens [M]:**
  - Thin skin with less collagen and elastin: "crepey skin happens to basically everyone" (Dr Sam Ellis).
  - Sun.
  - Menopause (5.3).
  - Rubbing: "when you rub the skin, it almost creates like a defense mechanism and it causes something called epidermal hyperplasia... the thickening of the top layer of the skin actually leads to the appearance of more lines and wrinkles" (Dr Sam Ellis).
  - Lifelong squinting: a viewer, "I've squinted my whole life so even as a 13 year old I noticed crepe skin under my eyes" (@CateHammers, QbvzKn9GG_w).
- **How it feels [M].** "My creepy eyelids make me crazy." (@roseport20001, T-qO5qhE82s, from `05`). "I get so afraid of using new products on my face but the under eye wrinkles are really upsetting and I'm so over it." (@francessimon2296, 18 likes, QbvzKn9GG_w).
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** Instant tighteners (gone with water or by evening). Strong retinol (stings). Heavy concealer (sinks into the crepe).
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Moisturise** (an instant, temporary smoothing).
  - **Sunscreen and sunglasses** for prevention. A viewer: "Started SPF 50 under my eyes from about age 38 and now at age 58 have hardly any under eye lines." (@maxie-q4r, 292 likes, QbvzKn9GG_w).
  - **Retinoids** if tolerated, buffered.
  - **Peptides**, hydrating serums, and AHAs kept on the bone (Dr Sam Ellis).
  - **Prescription topical estrogen**: "it's particularly effective for the undereye area because that skin is so thin and responsive to estrogen" (Dr Sam Ellis).
  - **In-office treatments** (Dr Sam Ellis):
    - "one or two units of Botox right under the eye skin" for fine rippling;
    - PRP or PRF;
    - microneedling, light peels and lasers. "CO2 lasers ... are the most powerful things we can do to resurface the under eye area".
  - **Surgery**: a "skin pinch" to remove extra skin (Dr Karam).
- **This balm [R].** **Its strongest use.** It smooths the look of crepiness for hours; adenosine may add a little over weeks; and being retinol-free makes it easy to use every day.

### 6.9 Fine lines and crow's feet

- **What it is.** Fine lines are shallow wrinkles under the eye. Crow's feet fan out from the outer corners.
- **Why it happens [M].** Expression plus collagen loss plus sun: "chronic creasing of the skin from expression throughout our lifetime coupled with age related decline in collagen, collagen loss due to well good oldfashioned sun" (Dr Dray, si9l1VZtzvQ). Poor sleep: "people who are sleep deprived have more prominent signs of photo aging and they have deeper wrinkles" (Dr Dray, a2Hot5ZsYU8).
- **How it feels [M].** "62, no amount of collagen creams or fancy serums are going to make my skin bounce back to the skin of a 20 year old." (@K9que, 257 likes, hAc7jewvVWE).
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Topical vitamin A**, "probably your best bet" (Dr Dray, a2Hot5ZsYU8). Use a gentle eye-strength version: "if you're using a retinol on your face it's ... very likely it's too strong for the thin skin of the eyelids". Dr Dray recommends fragrance-free retinol eye creams such as Neutrogena Rapid Wrinkle Repair eye and Avène's retinaldehyde eye cream.
  - **Moisturisers with humectants**: "moisturizers have humectants like hyaluronic acid that plump up those superficial skin cells" (Dr Dray).
  - **Sun protection and sleep** (Dr Dray: "aim for eight hours").
  - **Adenosine** (one trial, PMID 18489289). **Peptides**: a small trial (21 women, 8 weeks) found palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 cream beat the dummy for crow's feet (PMID 36909866).
  - **In-office:** Botox for crow's feet; radiofrequency and resurfacing (Dr Dray, a2Hot5ZsYU8).
- **This balm [R].** **Fair.** Instant softening from moisture; possibly a little more over 3–8 weeks.

### 6.10 Dryness and dehydration lines

- **What it is.** Tight, flaky or papery skin with very fine lines that fade when the skin is moisturised.
- **Why it happens [M].** Few oil glands; less water-holding after menopause (Dr Dray); harsh cleansers; dry air [R].
- **How it feels [M].** "Super dry skin, regardless of trying to consistently hydrate" (@Luvbug64, eA9YIs-49sc, from `05`).
- **What can fix it [M].** Occlusive protectants. Dr Dray: "petroleum jelly does not cause milia", and a glycerin skin protectant at night has been "an absolute gamechanger... for keeping eyelid skin hydrated" (si9l1VZtzvQ). Kim Sullins: "you have got to hydrate your under eyes. It's one of the most important things you can do for yourself." (eA9YIs-49sc).
- **This balm [R].** **Good.** This is what a wax-and-oil stick does best.

### 6.11 Concealer creasing and caking

- **What it is.** Concealer collects in lines when you smile, leaving streaks and a dry, cakey look by midday.
- **Why it happens [M]:**
  - The skin moves and bunches. Wayne Goss: "when we smile our cheeks lift up and that causes the lines underneath the eyes or the skin to bunch up" (g8fziC5EtOY).
  - Too much product, and powder "goes into lines and wrinkles as the day goes on" (same).
  - Dry skin "drinks" the concealer (Lisa Eldridge: "some people really drink it up").
  - Everyone creases differently: "It's unique. It's like a fingerprint." (Lisa Eldridge).
- **How it feels [M]:**
  - "Every sales rep at every major cosmetics counter just sold me a concealer and told me to put it under my eyes which, of course, emphasized the bags." (@Anne-ux3ex, age 64, 509 likes, mx6mhz5cqjU; from `05`)
  - "Concealer can make them look worse. It sits on top, settles into fine lines, and creases" (Kim Sullins's video description, eA9YIs-49sc)
- **What people try, and why it fails [R].** More powder: it looks drier. "Crease-proof" concealers: everyone still creases somewhere (Lisa Eldridge).
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - **Hydrate first.** One viewer: "I usually swipe a bit of aquaphor lip balm under my eyes and then I use just a little Mabeline instant age rewind... the Aquaphor helps to keep the concealer from getting chalky." (@barbarascully5451, eA9YIs-49sc).
  - **Less concealer, and only where it is dark**: inner corner, the area that "doesn't move" (Wayne Goss).
  - **Wait, find your creases, and wipe them out** (Lisa Eldridge).
  - **Little powder**, or a setting spray on a brush (Kim Sullins).
  - **A sheer brightener or tinted eye cream instead of concealer** (Kim Sullins, in her pinned comment on eA9YIs-49sc: "Concealer covers and sits on top. A brightener is sheer and uses light, so it moves with your skin as we age").
- **This balm [R].** **Good**, as the smooth, moist base. This is the most honest everyday reason to use it.

### 6.13 Stinging, watery eyes, eyelid rashes (sensitivity)

- **What it is.** Burning, stinging, redness or itch when products go on; eyes that water.
- **Why it happens [M]:**
  - Thin skin and product creeping into the eye.
  - Fragrance, preservatives, nickel and nail products. The AAO list: "Balsam of Peru..., cinnamic alcohol, fragrance mixes, gold, methylisothiazolinone, nail care products, neomycin, nickel, and personal care products" (PMID 40119863).
  - Retinoids near the eyes (PMID 42708560).
- **How it feels [M].**
  - "What helps me is to not let ANY product touch the water line of my eyes... My eyes used to water all day long" (662 likes, iwyLM5aJ7mE).
  - On the Medicube stick: "Did it burn anyone else's face" (HxsOvh5HVzQ).
  - Even dermatologists react: Doctorly's two doctors, trying an eye cream on camera: "It's burning me. Me, too." (iwyLM5aJ7mE).
- **What can fix it [M]:**
  - Fragrance-free, simple products.
  - Patch testing (formal patch tests at a dermatologist for repeat rashes).
  - Barrier protectants (Dr Dray).
  - Allergy control.
  - Keep products on the bone.
- **This balm [R].** **A real plus, if the claims are true.** Retinol-free and fragrance-free are the right choices for sensitive lids. The catch is that the full list can't be checked, so allergy-prone people can't rule out their personal triggers.

### 6.14 Dry eye made worse by products

- **What it is.** Gritty, burning, watery or tired eyes from a poor tear film.
- **Why it happens [M].** Age and sex: "more common in older women 50s 60s" (Dr Rupa Wong). Retinol and some preservatives near the lid rims affect the meibomian glands (Dr Rupa Wong; Doctor Eye Health). "THANK YOU for mentioning how retinol may cause dry eye." (commenter, iwyLM5aJ7mE).
- **What can fix it [R].** An eye doctor's care (drops, lid hygiene). Keep all creams and balms off the lid margin; choose retinol-free products near the eyes.
- **This balm [R].** Retinol-free is a plus. Waxy balm on the lid rim is not. Keep it low.

### 6.15 Loose skin, hooding and festoons (briefly)

- **What it is.** Extra skin on the upper or lower lids (dermatochalasis); swelling pouches on the upper cheek (festoons or malar bags).
- **Fix [M].** Surgery for extra skin (Dr Karam's "skin pinch"; blepharoplasty). Hooding of the upper lid is mainly surgical; some instant-tightening products give a few hours (Doctorly on RoC's two-part eye cream with a "glue polymer" for the upper lid).
- **This balm [R].** **None.**

## 9. Myths and truths

| Myth | Truth | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "You need a special eye cream." | A gentle, fragrance-free face moisturiser works. Eye products are mainly gentler. | Dr Dray si9l1VZtzvQ; Dr Sam Ellis XKROvYsp0QM [M] |

## 11. Safety, and what you can and can't say on camera (short)

**Safety [R, with sources in sections 3–6]:**
1. Keep it on the bone below the eye, never on the waterline or lid rim.
2. Patch-test first (jaw or behind the ear, 2–3 days). No full ingredient list was found, so people with allergies can't check it. Named ingredients include a nut oil (argan), a chamomile-derived ingredient (bisabolol) and possibly salmon-derived PDRN.
3. Stop if it stings, itches or swells. Rinse with cool water.
4. Don't use it on an eye infection, eyelid eczema or a fresh surgery site. Throw the stick away after an infection, and don't share it.
5. See a doctor for swelling under one eye only, sudden or painful swelling, swelling with vision changes, or bumps that grow or bleed (Cleveland Clinic; Doctorly).

**What can't be said on camera (one line):** don't say it "lifts", "firms", "removes bags", "fills hollows", "triggers collagen", "fixes collagen loss", "fixes pigment" or "replaces filler/Botox". Those are drug-type or unsupported claims. Say "moisturises", "looks smoother", "smooth base for concealer" instead. The detailed rules are in `05-brand-audience-and-selling-rules.md` §4.6.
