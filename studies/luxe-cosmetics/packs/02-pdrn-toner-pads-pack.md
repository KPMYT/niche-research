## In one minute

1. **What it is.** A jar of 140 round gel pads soaked in a pink, watery gel. You lay one on your face for 5 minutes like a tiny mask, then wipe with the other side. It is a **moisture (hydration) product** [M: ingredient list].
2. **What's really in it.** Mostly water plus "water magnets" (glycerin and friends), a good dose of **niacinamide** (vitamin B3), a small amount of **salmon DNA (PDRN)**, and traces of hyaluronic acid, peptides, collagen and plant extracts. **No acids, no retinol, no fragrance, no alcohol** [M].
3. **It is the same 45 ingredients as Anua PDRN 100 Hyaluronic Acid Glow Pad**, a Korean pad that states its PDRN at **100 ppm = 0.01%** [M: dodoskin.com].
4. **What it honestly does.** Skin looks plumper, dewier and smoother for some hours. With daily use, niacinamide may slowly help tone look more even. That's the believable part [R, based on the studies in section 2.3].
5. **What it can't do.** Lift sagging skin, firm a crepey neck, rebuild collagen or "repair cells". PDRN works when injected; on the skin surface the evidence is very thin [M: Dermatology Times 11 Jul 2026; Dr Dray].
6. **Who it suits.** Dry, dull, easily irritated skin, especially women over 50 who can't tolerate retinol or acids [R].
7. **Who should not use it.** Anyone with a **fish allergy** (salmon DNA; the collagen source isn't stated) or soy allergy, and vegans [M: ingredient list; R: advice].
8. **Price.** $59.95 for 140 pads on the brand site = **$0.43 a pad** [M]. The identical Anua pad is $23 for 60 = $0.38 a pad [M].

## 1. The basics from zero

### 1.1 What skin is, in kitchen-table words

Your skin is the biggest organ you have. Think of it as **three layers stacked like a cake** [M: StatPearls skin anatomy, NCBI; R: the comparisons].

| Layer | What it is | Kitchen comparison | Why it matters for this pad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epidermis** (top layer) | Thin as a sheet of paper on the face. New cells are made at its bottom and travel up, flattening and dying as they go. StatPearls lists five sub-layers, from the bottom: basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum (palms and soles only), corneum [M] | A conveyor belt of cells that ends at the surface | This is the only layer a pad really touches |
| **Stratum corneum** (the very top of the epidermis) | About 15–30 layers of flat, dead cells (sources differ on the count) glued together with fats [M: StatPearls; Wikipedia] | A **brick wall**: dead cells are the bricks, fats are the mortar | This is "the skin barrier". Most skincare works here |
| **Dermis** (middle layer) | Thick, living layer full of **collagen** (strength), **elastin** (snap-back) and **hyaluronic acid** (holds water). Blood vessels and oil glands live here | The **mattress springs and stuffing** | Wrinkles and sagging start here. Creams and pads barely reach it |
| **Fat layer, muscle and bone** (underneath) | Fat pads, muscles and the skull | The **couch frame and cushions** | Sagging jowls and hollows come from here. Skincare can't change it |

Dr Shereene Idriss (dermatologist, "Over 50? Your Skincare Routine Should Change Like This", 753,067 views [M]) uses the same picture: *"we should think of our faces as couches. Our skin is the fabric of the couch. The skin care can affect the fabric of the couch, but our skincare is never going to fill our couches and plump them up with feathers and our skincare is never going to fix the foundation of our couch, i.e. our bone structure"* [M: transcript ve54izFiAUY].

**How long the conveyor belt takes.** Cells take roughly two to four weeks to travel up and flake off. It slows with age: one review gives about 20 days in young adults against 30 or more in older adults [M: secondary source in search results; peer-reviewed Journal of Cosmetic Science review confirms "the rate of SC turnover decreases with age", exact figures vary]. Slower turnover means dead cells pile up, and skin looks **dull** (it reflects light less evenly).

### 1.2 The skin barrier, hydration and moisture

- **The skin barrier** is the brick wall at the top. Its mortar is three kinds of fat: **ceramides, cholesterol and fatty acids** [M: Dr Sam Ellis and Doctorly transcripts]. A good wall keeps water **in** and irritants **out**.
- **A damaged barrier** has cracks in the mortar. Water escapes (doctors call it **TEWL, transepidermal water loss**: water evaporating out through the skin), and things that never stung before now sting [M: Dr Dray, "Skincare in Your 40s and 50s", cmKERM23uhs].
- **Hydration = water** in the skin. **Moisture (oil) = the fats** that seal the water in. People mix the words up. Skin can be:
  - **Dry**: short of oil (a skin *type*; common after menopause).
  - **Dehydrated**: short of water (a *condition*; anyone can have it). It feels tight and looks dull or finely lined.
- **The three kinds of moisturising ingredient** (Dr Dray lists them in the menopause video [M]):

| Kind | What it does | Kitchen comparison | Examples in this pad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Humectant** | Grabs water and holds it in the top layer | A sponge | Glycerin, butylene glycol, hyaluronic acid, urea-type, sugars. **The pad is mostly these** [M: INCI] |
| **Emollient** | Smooths and softens, fills the gaps between dead cells | Butter spread on rough toast | Very little in this pad |
| **Occlusive** | Forms a seal so water can't escape | Cling film over a bowl | **None** in this pad |

**Why this matters for the pad [R]:** the pad is almost all humectant. Humectants pull water in, but without a seal on top the water can evaporate again. So the pad needs a **moisturiser after it**, especially on dry mature skin. A Korean doctor (Seoul Beauty, "The ONLY 3 Things You Need for Real Glass Skin", 343,348 views) says: *"those water-grabbing ingredients pull moisture in, sure, but if you don't put a cream over the top, it just evaporates right away"* [M: transcript 864b16vfJQk].

### 1.3 What a skincare routine is, and why the order matters

A routine is simply the products you put on, in order, morning and night. Dermatologists at Doctorly ("Cleanse, Treat, Protect", 1,192,728 views) boil it down to three jobs [M: transcript rcIDAuIiSaI]:

| Job | What it means | Products | Morning | Night |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Cleanse** | Wash off oil, dirt, sunscreen and makeup | Cleanser (face wash, cleansing milk or oil) | Optional, gentle | Yes |
| **2. Treat** | Put on the things that do a specific job | **Toner, toner pad, essence, serum**, retinoid, eye cream | Yes | Yes |
| **3. Protect** | Seal moisture in and block the sun | **Moisturiser**; **sunscreen** in the morning only | Moisturiser + sunscreen | Moisturiser |

The five products people name most:

| Product | What it is, plainly | Texture |
|---|---|---|
| **Cleanser** | Soap's gentle modern cousin | Gel, cream, foam, oil |
| **Toner** | A watery liquid used after washing (see 1.4) | Like water |
| **Serum** | A small bottle of concentrated "treatment" liquid | Thin to slightly gooey |
| **Moisturiser** | A lotion or cream that softens and seals | Lotion to thick cream |
| **Sunscreen (SPF)** | Blocks the sun's UV rays, the biggest cause of spots and wrinkles | Lotion or fluid |

**Why order matters: thinnest to thickest.** A dermatologist Short ("When Should You Use Toner Pads? Dermatologist Explains", 58,000 views): *"generally speaking, it's a good idea to go from thinnest texture to thickest. So, liquids go on first after cleansing"* [M: transcript EmpVHOGyYLw]. The idea: a watery product can't get through a layer of thick cream, the way water beads on a waxed car. Doctorly admit it's a rule of thumb, not hard science: *"there's no science behind this... thinnest to thickest, and that's generally how we layer our products"* [M: transcript ZGB8BDIGHE4]. Sunscreen always goes **last** in the morning so nothing smears it [R].

**For this pad:** it's the thinnest thing after cleansing, so it goes **first** after washing.

### 1.4 What toner is

- **Where toner came from.** Old soaps were very alkaline (high pH) and left a film. Toner was invented to wipe off the leftovers and bring skin back to its normally slightly acidic state. A dermatologist Short ("Are toners necessary?", 477,000 views): *"originally toners were used for pH balance after using a cleanser... these days cleansers are better formulated... so toners really don't have any function anymore"* [M: transcript jo9nvX8NkK4].
- **What "toner" means now.** Any watery product used after cleansing. Doctorly: *"There's no like, you know, government definition of what a toner is"*; toners, essences and serums are *"purely marketing language"* that mostly tells you the **texture** [M: ZGB8BDIGHE4]. What matters is what's inside.
- **The three kinds of toner today [R]:**

| Kind | What's in it | What it does | Good for mature dry skin? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Astringent toner** (old-school) | Alcohol, witch hazel | Tightens, de-greases, can sting | Usually no, too drying |
| **Exfoliating toner** | Acids: AHA (glycolic, lactic), BHA (salicylic), PHA | Loosens dead cells, smooths, can sting | Sometimes, a few times a week |
| **Hydrating toner / essence** | Water + humectants (glycerin, hyaluronic acid), soothers | Adds water, plumps, calms | **Yes. The Luxe pad is this kind** |

Dr Idriss recommends exactly this kind after 50: *"Essences and quote unquote toners, i.e. hydrating toners not exfoliating toners"* [M: ve54izFiAUY].

### 1.5 What toner pads are

**A toner pad is a toner that comes already soaked into pads, in a jar.** Instead of pouring toner onto cotton wool, you lift out a wet pad with tweezers [M: dermatologist Short EmpVHOGyYLw: *"this is a liquid toner in a pad"*].

**Why people like them [M: EmpVHOGyYLw unless noted]:**
- Easy: no bottle, no cotton.
- **Precise and less dripping**: *"you can apply them to the skin and avoid dripping the product into your eyes."*
- **More even**: *"you get a more even application with a pad versus just pouring the toner into your hands."*
- **Two jobs in one**: wipe *and* mask. A beauty Short ("Are Toner Pads a waste of money?", 82,000 views): *"do you need to use them as face masks because that's the real value in them for me"* [M: jxM3Vtfz4Wc].

**Why some people don't [M]:**
- **Waste and the environment.** Dermatologist Short "DOWN WITH TONER PADS!!!" (3,600,000 views): *"I don't think anybody should use them because they're bad for the environment. Microfibers... Facial sprays are a much better option"* (L9Va9IG6zG8; a near-identical Short, 5F4RSrN46DM, "BETTER THAN TONER PADS!!!", 1,000,000 views).
- **Cost.** Pads cost more per use than a bottle of the same liquid [R]. Each pad is used once.
- **Too wet.** A commenter: *"They are way over saturated and the amount of toner in them could work for many applications per pad"* (@Cinnam0r011Archiv3, EmpVHOGyYLw).
- **They dry out at the bottom of the jar** [M: two comments in the first round, e.g. *"i think EVERY toner pad should come with a seperate serum or toner for when it becomes dry"*, @Aasya_10_Reads, 29 likes].

**The kinds of pad (the types inside the category) [R, from the product survey in `02-pdrn-toner-pads.md` section 5]:**

| Type | What the pad is like | What's in the liquid | Main job | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Three ways people use pads [M: product instructions; Shorts]:**
1. **Wipe**: sweep across the face like a toner.
2. **Mask** ("pad mask"): lay pads on the cheeks or forehead for 5–10 minutes. Mediheal: *"leave it on like a mini mask for 5–10 minutes"*; Luxe/Anua: 5 minutes.
3. **"Korean cream mask"**: lay soaked pads on, then spread a gel or milk cream over them, then peel off (Short ThJz4gHQOhk, 73,000 views).

### 1.6 Korean skincare and "glass skin"

- **K-beauty** is short for Korean beauty: skincare and makeup from South Korea. It is known for gentle, layered routines, lots of hydration, sunscreen, sheet masks and new "hero" ingredients (snail mucin, centella, and now PDRN) [R].
- **The "10-step routine".** Charlotte Cho, who founded the shop Soko Glam in 2012, says she learned the many steps Koreans use and *"coined"* the phrase "10-step skincare routine" for Americans; it caught on around 2014 [M: search results citing Soko Glam's "Our story" page and press]. Steps typically: oil cleanser, water cleanser, exfoliant, toner, essence, serum/ampoule, sheet mask, eye cream, moisturiser, sunscreen [R]. Even Soko Glam now says it's "not about using more products".
- **"Glass skin"** means skin so smooth, clear and dewy that it shines like glass. The Korean phrase is *yuri pibu* (유리 피부). The English term went viral in **2017** after a tweet by the make-up artist Ellie Choi (Cosmetics Business, 21 Nov 2017) [M: search results; much of the background comes from brand blogs].
- **What glass skin actually is, per doctors:**
  - A Korean doctor (Seoul Beauty, 343,348 views): *"glow isn't really one thing, it's the three. A smooth surface, good hydration both inside and on top, and honestly, what's happening under the hood... Your sleep, your stress"*. His comparison: two walls with the same paint; *"The smooth one kind of shines... the rough one just looks flat"* [M: 864b16vfJQk].
  - Doctorly ("The ULTIMATE Routine to Achieve Glass or Dewy Skin", 356,359 views): hydration *"plumps the skin, it makes it look just shiny, full"*; *"It's not a permanent effect, but it's an immediate effect"*; and *"perfect skin does not exist"* [M: fHhTXLPdSFE].
- **Where the pad fits [R]:** a hydrating gel pad delivers the "water on top" part of glass skin right away. It doesn't smooth the surface the way an exfoliant does, and the glow fades over hours.

### 1.7 What PDRN is

**The name.** PDRN stands for **polydeoxyribonucleotide**. In plain words: **short pieces of DNA**, the same stuff genes are made of [M: Dr Dray transcript XC2OZDLpEJ0; Kim 2025].

**Where it comes from.** Mostly **salmon (or trout) sperm or testes**, purified. That's where the nickname "salmon sperm facial" comes from [M: Dr Dray, "Viral Salmon DNA Skin Booster Injections", 97,481 views, 9fdKDS1Zdi0: *"polynucleotides actually come from testes whereas poly deoxy ribonucleotides come from sperm"*].

**What it's called on a label.** **Sodium DNA** (sometimes Hydrolyzed DNA) [M: Kim ST, *Pharmaceutics* 2025;17(8):1024; One Eye Beauty: *"its INCI name is sodium DNA. Much easier, right?"*].

**How it's supposed to work (lab theory).** PDRN switches on a "receptor" (a lock on the surface of cells) called adenosine A2A. In cells in a dish, wounds and animals, that calms inflammation and nudges **fibroblasts** (the collagen-making cells) to work [M: Dr Dray XC2OZDLpEJ0: *"it can have modulatory effects on tissue regeneration, wound healing pathways, have anti-inflammatory effects, stimulate [fibroblasts] to produce collagen"*]. Kitchen picture [R]: like a doorbell on a factory; ring it and the workers start up. The catch: the doorbell is deep inside the house (the dermis), and a pad leaves the PDRN on the doorstep.

**Medical use (real and approved).** Korea's drug regulator (MFDS) approved PDRN as an **injected medicine for tissue repair and wound healing**; Italy's Placentex has been sold since the early 1990s [M: Kim 2025]. Dr Dray: *"Probably one of the most intriguing, compelling uses of PDRN is not for anti-aging. It's for healing stubborn diabetic foot ulcers"*; in a trial it was **injected** around the wounds and they healed faster than with placebo [M: XC2OZDLpEJ0].

**PN vs PDRN.** PN (polynucleotide) = longer, heavier DNA chains; PDRN = shorter ones. **Rejuran**, the famous "salmon sperm" injection, is a PN product classed in Korea as a medical device. The terms are used loosely: *"no standardized criteria have been established to delineate molecular weight thresholds"* [M: Kim 2025].

**Injections (the "salmon sperm facial", Rejuran) [M: Dr Dray 9fdKDS1Zdi0 unless noted]:**
- What happens: numbing cream, then either an injector "gun" or hundreds of tiny injections into the face. Usually 2–3 sessions, then top-ups about every 6 months. Kim Kardashian made it famous.
- The evidence: small studies. A 5-patient study; a 44-patient trial on thyroid-surgery scars (better scars); and a 27-patient split-face trial (2022) of polynucleotide against a hyaluronic-acid injection, where the polynucleotide side improved pores and roughness more, *"however these differences were not statistically significant"* [M: Dr Dray]. Dermatology Times (11 Jul 2026) describes a 27-person split-face trial in which Rejuran, compared with an HA filler, scored higher on a global aesthetic scale at 16 weeks [M]. These may be the same trial reported on different measures [R].
- Dr Dray's verdict on injections for ageing: *"purely anecdotal at this point. No good clinical trials"* [M: XC2OZDLpEJ0]. A commenter: *"I had PDRN injected under my eyes and it did absolutely nothing beyond the temporary swelling"* (@Criticalbeauty411, 161 likes) [M].
- Safety of injections: *"PDRN injections have not been clinically associated with serious adverse effects"* [M: Kim 2025]. Bruising, swelling and infection risk come from the needle [M: Dr Dray].

**Creams, serums and pads (what Luxe is) [M]:**
- **Size problem.** *"PDRN fragments range from 50 to 1,500 kilodaltons"*; *"molecules larger than approximately 500 Daltons are unlikely to penetrate intact human skin"*; *"PDRN applied topically without an active delivery mechanism is unlikely to reach the fibroblasts in the viable dermis"* (Dermatology Times, Marie Bosslett, 11 Jul 2026). Kitchen picture [R]: the skin's "door" lets through things the size of a grain of sugar; PDRN pieces are 100 to 3,000 times heavier, like trying to push a football through a letterbox.
- **Human evidence for creams:** only *"a single small study using a specific low-MW plant-derived PDRN that is not representative of the majority of products on the market"* (Dermatology Times).
- **Dr Dray** ("PDRN Skincare: The Science Behind the Hype", 144,164 views): *"applying salmon derived PDRN to human skin for anti-aging benefit. Nothing there."* and *"I'm highly, highly, highly doubtful that these skincare products with PDRN do anything above and beyond moisturize the skin."* She still liked a PDRN cream *"as a really nice moisturizer"* because of its niacinamide, ceramides and humectants [M: XC2OZDLpEJ0].
- **The other side:** One Eye Beauty (a UK-based Korean skincare coach in her 40s, "Best & Worst PDRN Skincare 2025 – What Actually Works for Mature Skin?", 39,117 views) argues that even if salmon PDRN *"just stays on our epidermis... It will still help with skin hydration and barrier support. So, it's not completely useless"* [M: PEpiVhBo_g8]. This is her opinion; no study is cited.

- **ppm = parts per million.** 100 ppm = 0.01%. Kitchen picture: 100 ppm is about **1 gram (a quarter teaspoon of sugar) stirred into a 10-litre bucket**.

| Product | PDRN listed |

| Medicube PDRN Pink Collagen Gel Toner Pad | 1 ppm = 0.0001% |

- **"99% purity PDRN"** (on the Luxe page) describes the raw material, not how much is in the pad. One Eye Beauty on Medicube's cleanser: *"it says it has 99% high purity salmon PDRN... looking at the ingredients list, this is far from the truth"* [M: PEpiVhBo_g8].

**Plant "PDRN".** DNA extracted from ginseng (VT), carrot, seaweed, rose, matcha and others, also labelled "Sodium DNA". Dr Dray: *"there's no actual clinical data on it"* [M]. **Luxe uses salmon** [M: jar: "Salmon-Derived PDRN COMPLEX"].

## 3. How to use it, step by step

### 3.1 Before your first use: a patch test [R]

1. Take one pad. Wipe a small patch on the side of the jaw or behind the ear.
2. Leave it. Do this once a day for **2–3 days**.
3. If you get redness, itching, bumps or burning, don't use it.
4. **If you have a fish or soy allergy, don't patch-test: ask your doctor or allergist first** (section 11).

### 3.2 The everyday steps (the brand's "mask then wipe" method)

The brand's own steps are quoted in bold [M: image `tonnerfixed_6`]; the rest is plain-English detail [R].

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wash your hands. | You'll be touching the face and the jar |
| 2 | Wash your face with a gentle cleanser and pat it almost dry with a clean towel. | **"After cleansing"** [M]. Slightly damp skin takes up water better (Seoul Beauty doctor: *"put your hydrating stuff on while your skin's still slightly damp"*) [M] |
| 3 | Open the jar and lift out **one** pad with the tweezers or a clean fingertip. Press it against the side of the jar to let extra gel drip back. | Keeps the jar clean; stops dripping |
| 4 | Close the lid at once. | Pads dry out if the lid is left open (Medicube: *"Keep the lid closed and sealed to prevent the pad from drying out"*) [M] |
| 5 | Find the slippery gel side. Lay it flat on one cheek, or tear/fold it to cover both cheeks or the forehead. | **"apply the 'Peptide Gel Side' to your skin"** [M] |
| 6 | Leave it **5 minutes**. Sit, brush your teeth, or dry your hair. | **"Leave it on for 5 minutes like a mini sheet mask"**; **"Use as a quick morning mask while drying your hair!"** [M] |
| 7 | Peel it off, turn it over, and **gently** sweep the fabric side across the whole face, from the centre outwards. Avoid the eyes and lips. | **"Use the 'Plain Side' to wipe along your skin texture"** [M]. Anua says *"excluding the area around your eyes and mouth"* [M: dodoskin] |
| 8 | Pat in what's left with your fingertips. Squeeze the pad onto your neck or the backs of your hands rather than waste it. | **"Lightly pat in the remaining essence"** [M] |
| 9 | Wait about a minute until it's no longer wet. | Moisturiser spreads better on tacky, not wet, skin [R] |
| 10 | Put on your moisturiser. In the morning, finish with sunscreen. | Seals the water in; protects from the sun (section 4) |

**How often:** the brand says **"Can be used every day, both AM & PM"** [M]. For dry, sensitive or older skin, once a day (night, or morning before makeup) is plenty to start [R].

### 3.3 The two other ways

- **Wipe only (fast, about 30 seconds):** skip step 6. Sweep the face with one side, then pat. Less hydration, quicker [R].
- **Mask only (calming):** lay the pad on dry or red patches for 5–10 minutes, then pat in; don't wipe [R].

### 3.4 Common beginner mistakes

| # | Mistake | Why it's a problem | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scrubbing** with the pad | Thin mature skin tears and reddens easily. A beauty Short: *"rubbing too roughly can irritate your skin and cause even more redness"* [M: KuFEiyuZEdk] | Glide or press; never scrub |
| 2 | **Skipping moisturiser** because "one pad replaces your routine" | Humectants without a seal let water evaporate; skin can end up tighter (One Eye Beauty described a humectant serum that *"made it feel even more dry"*) [M: PEpiVhBo_g8] | Always follow with a moisturiser on dry skin |
| 3 | **Skipping sunscreen** and expecting spots to fade | Sun keeps making spots. Dr Dray: sunscreen *"will not only help in preventing more sunspots... but it also will help in fading existing"* ones [M: cmKERM23uhs] | Sunscreen every morning |
| 4 | **Leaving the lid open** | Pads dry out; the bottom of the jar goes dry | Close at once; store upside down for a while if the top pads dry (common tip [R]) |
| 5 | **Using fingers dipped straight in the jar** | Germs go in | Use the tweezers or wash hands |
| 6 | **Reusing a pad** the next day | Bacteria grow on wet fabric | One pad, one use |
| 7 | **Leaving it on for 20–30 minutes** "for more effect" | The gel dries and can start pulling water out of the skin as it does (as sheet masks can) [R] | 5–10 minutes max |
| 8 | **Wiping over the eyes and lips** | Stings and wastes product | Go around them |
| 9 | **Stacking it with acid pads, scrubs and retinol on the same night** | Too much at once irritates mature skin | This pad is gentle, but keep other strong steps on separate nights at first |
| 10 | **Expecting a facelift** | Skincare *"can only do so much"* (Dr Idriss) [M] | Expect plumper, dewier, calmer skin |
| 11 | **Judging it after one week for spots** | Niacinamide studies ran 4–12 weeks [M] | Take a photo on day 1 and at week 8 in the same light |
| 12 | **Calling a breakout "purging"** | Purging only happens with acids or retinoids; this pad has neither [R] | If you break out, stop and patch test |
| 13 | **Using it if you're allergic to fish** | Salmon DNA, collagen of unknown source | Ask a doctor first |

### 5.2 How skin changes after 50 and 60, and why

**The big driver is menopause.** Menopause officially starts 12 months after the last period; the skin changes start years earlier in perimenopause [M: Dr Sam Ellis, N7SgoUm_YDE]. Estrogen, which helps keep skin thick, plump, oily and quick to heal, falls [M: Dr Sam Ellis; Dr Dray cmKERM23uhs].

| Change | What happens, and why | What it looks and feels like | Can this pad help? [R] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collagen loss** | *"nearly a third lost in the first 5 years and a decline of 2.1% per year over the 15 years following menopause"* [M: Kamp et al., *Clin Exp Dermatol* 2022;47(12):2117]. ## 11. Safety, and what you can and can't say on camera

**Safety [M: INCI; R: advice]**
- **Fish allergy:** contains salmon DNA; the collagen source isn't stated. BestReviews: *"People with fish or seafood allergies should avoid salmon-derived PDRN pads."* [M]. Experts disagree on whether purification removes fish proteins; no study was found [M: search]. Say: "If you're allergic to fish, ask your doctor first."
- **Soy and corn:** soybean ferment, possibly soy lecithin; corn extract.
- **Not vegan.**
- **Patch test** 2–3 days; stop if it stings, burns, itches or causes spots.
- **Keep out of the eyes and off the lips.** Press, don't scrub, on thin skin.
- **New or changing spots** should be seen by a doctor, not treated with pads [M: Dr Dray].

**What you can't say (drug-type or unsupported claims) [R, per `SUMMARY.md` section 5]**
- Can't say: "repairs skin at the cellular level", "regenerates", "rebuilds collagen", "boosts collagen", "lifts", "firms", "fades dark spots in 4 weeks", "clinically proven", "the same as Korean clinic injections", "exfoliates with PHA", "vitamin C", "99% PDRN", "vegan", "hypoallergenic", "zero side effects", "125% more hydration" or "100% saw results" (those are Anua's test numbers).
- Can say: "a gentle, fragrance-free, acid-free hydrating gel pad"; "skin looks plumper and dewier right after"; "use it as a 5-minute mini mask"; "contains niacinamide, which can help skin tone look more even over weeks"; "contains salmon-derived PDRN; the science on PDRN in skincare is still early".
