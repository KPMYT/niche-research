**Tier C: high ceiling, hit or miss.** Electrical explainers: 521 videos, typical only 1.5K, but 33 over 1M, and the explainer is where persona and faceless channels have won lately: "Volts, Amps & Watts Explained — and Which One Kills You" (The Apprentice's Guide, 841K), "What Does the Neutral Wire Actually Do" (Modern-Day Edison, 520K, 925 times that channel's typical video), "Solid vs. Stranded Wire" (History of Simple Things, 459K). ## 4. Emotions and villains

From `emotions.md` (about 1,000 titles labelled; a blind re-label agreed on the first emotion 78% of the time).

**The emotion to lead with:** pride in fixing it yourself. In outlet and switch titles it led 22 times out of 49; safety fear appeared in 9 but led only once.
**What backs it up:**
- safety fear: fire and shock as the stakes
- "you've been doing it wrong"
- myth-busting ("settle the debate")
- how-it-works curiosity ("so that's why")
- nuisance: "make it stop" (the breaker that keeps tripping, the chirp)
**Money worry:** almost absent (4 of 262), and out by decision anyway.

**Villains** (70-80% of videos have none, so use them lightly):
- **The last owner's or handyman's bad wiring**, found in the walls.
- **The cheap product:** knock-off connectors, Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels, bargain testers.
- **Never the electrician or the fire service.** Viewers treat them as the trusted insiders. "Electricians HATE This" titles got 31K and 375 views.

**Gap to fill later:** how the scripts voice each emotion (the words, where the fear comes in). The operator moved this to the script SOP work.

## 5. Titles and thumbnails

**Titles:** take a proven title in the chosen formats and improve it. Shapes that carry the winners:
- "How to [fix the thing] (the right way / without...)"
- "Why does my [thing] keep [doing Y]?"
- "[X] vs [Y]: which one [stakes]?"
- "What [the part] actually does"
- "[N] [topic] mistakes..." now and then

**Thumbnails:**
- **Education videos:** the character beside a blue blueprint-style explainer board (`thumbnails/thumbnail-text.html`). Nobody in this niche does this yet. The nearest: The Wiring Guide's real man beside drawn wire lines with "EXPLAINED" (1.0M), and a plain blueprint "WHICH ONE KILLS YOU?" (841K). The look is open and unproven.
- **Everything else:** the character reacting to or pointing at the problem. This has worked for AI personas recently: Bertha's Clean Home 710K, Frederick 439K, Ray Dutton's Garage 406K, Ramón Delgado 375K.
- **Borrowing from winners:** take a winning object close-up and add the character beside the object.
- **What to avoid:** making the dark AI scene with something burning at night the default. It is the look of most small persona channels (their typical top video: 5K), though Frederick's one hit (439K) used it.
- **Pictures:** real-looking, with the object close up.

**Thumbnail text** (82 recent winners, `data/thumb-text/`):
- About 4 words.
- Commands ("STOP DOING THIS", "START HERE!") and questions ("WHICH ONE KILLS YOU?", "Why 120V?") typically got about twice the views of plain topic labels: 531K and 520K against 211K.
- Whether the text repeats the title or adds to it made no clear difference.

## 6. Where the gaps are

1. **Everyday fix topics are under-supplied.** Outlets, switches, fans, lights, connectors and GFCIs have the biggest audiences, yet their share of new uploads has halved. New channels are piling into fire lists, mistakes lists and explainers instead (mistakes are 21.9% of new uploads against 10.8% of older ones; fire habits 8.9% against 4.4%).
2. **No persona owns electrical how-to.** Every AI persona channel in the census is small, and most are firefighter or fire-marshal personas making fire lists (`channels.md`, presenter table). The channels that win on how-to are real people. The open lane is a persona that does the evergreen fix topics well.
3. **The explainer board look is unused** in this niche (section 5).
4. **"Why does it keep doing that"** has a high typical (22K) and only 73 videos.

## 7. How to stand apart

1. **Make the fix topics, not the fear lists.** Make the viewer feel capable, with fire and shock as the reason it matters.
2. **Insider authority, quietly.** Say "after all the houses I've worked in" rather than a job title; name the retired-electrician background only when it's needed.
3. **Explain what the testers show, without testing.** Take the topic of a big demonstration and explain the result and what it means for the viewer's house.
4. **Thumbnails that look like neither the giants nor the AI personas.** Use the character with the explainer board, or the character with a real-looking problem close up, plus a 2-4 word command or question.
5. **Improve on the original.** Each idea starts from a proven video in this niche and fixes what it lacks: a clearer "why", the stakes, the villain in the walls.

## 8. What the scripting pack should take from this

For the scripting chat in `KPMYT/script-system`, as inputs, not rules. The script chat decides how.

- **Open on the problem the viewer has right now:** the outlet that's dead, the breaker that trips, the fan that wobbles.
- **Lead with pride and capability.** Bring in fire and shock as the stakes, plainly and briefly; the viewer should leave feeling able, not scared.
- **Villains:** the last owner's wiring, the cheap product. Never electricians, inspectors or firefighters.
- **Voice:** a quiet insider, background mostly unspoken.
- **How-to shape:** the problem, why it happens, how to check it safely, the fix, how to know it's right, when it's a job for a licensed electrician.
- **Explainer shape:** the question, the common wrong answer, what's really going on, what it means in your house.
- **Safety in every how-to:** power off at the breaker, test before touching, and where permits or a licensed electrician are required. The claims policy in the core rules applies.
- **No money framing.** Long-form only: typically 8 to 17 minutes.
- **Track emotions per section** and, later, how the words voice them (operator, 2026-10-26).
