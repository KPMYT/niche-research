## 3. Emotions and villains

- **Strongest emotions:** pride in doing it yourself, safety fear, "you've been doing it wrong", myth-busting ("Settling a Debate! Wire Nut VS Wago" 2.2M), and the satisfaction of a clear fix. Three new ones were added: nuisance (the chirping alarm you want stopped), how-it-works curiosity, and spectacle (watching things spark).
- **Money worry barely appears** (4 of 262 fire and explainer videos).
- **Villains are rare:** 70-80% of videos have none. Electricians and the fire service are never the bad guy; firefighters appear as the friendly insider. The villain that works is **the last owner's bad wiring** found in the house, and **the cheap product** (knock-off Wago, Federal Pacific panels). "Electricians HATE This" titles flopped (31K and 375 views).
- **How far to trust this:** a blind re-label of 90 videos agreed on the first emotion 78% of the time. Some emotions are solid enough to count; others only "present" (see `emotions.md`).

- The channels that perform are **real electricians and inspectors on camera**.
- **AI-persona channels in this niche are all small so far.** Real on-camera channels' top videos: typical 368K; AI-persona channels': typical 5K. (In HVAC, the persona channel Ray Dutton's Garage did take off: 1.2M on one video.)
- **The fire investigator angle:** people do watch fire-investigation videos (Primal Space "How Investigators Find the Source of a Fire" 2.2M), but the big ones are true crime and disasters on big channels. Nobody turns the investigator's eye on the viewer's own house. The few personas that put "fire marshal" or "investigator" in the title get the same ~200 views as the firefighter lists, so the label alone adds nothing. Untested, not proven.

## 5. Thumbnails (see the page)

- The biggest videos use **real photos, the object close up, short text (2-4 words), few arrows, no AI.**
- AI-made pictures appear almost only in recent uploads; among recent videos, real pictures still do somewhat better.
- AI-persona channels share one look: an AI older man pointing at a burning object at night, red arrows and circles, 3-word commands ("NEVER LEAVE THIS ON"). Those channels are the small ones.

## Questions for you (answer "1 yes, 2 …")

1. **Lead with electrical, fire as the payoff?** Everyday electrical problems (outlets, switches, breakers, lights, fans, connectors) where the stakes are fire and shock. The alternative, fire-prevention-first, is the crowded, small lane. My suggestion: yes.
2. **Frederick's background:** the evidence favours a trade insider who inspects homes. My suggestion: **a retired electrical inspector**. It fits the inspection-walkthrough format (39K typical), the "last owner's bad wiring" villain and the fire stakes, and nobody owns it. The alternatives: electrician (proven, but crowded with real people), fire investigator (an untested gap), firefighter (crowded with tiny personas). Which?
3. **The persona risk:** every AI persona in this niche is small so far. Should the plan lean on what a persona can do well (tests explained, inspection stories, "why it keeps tripping") and real-looking thumbnails, and avoid the AI-man-pointing-at-fire look? My suggestion: yes.
