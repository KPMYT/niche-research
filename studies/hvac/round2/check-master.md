## How the check was done

1. **Sample.** Python `random.seed(2410)`, run once, in this order: the 6 named A rows set aside; **A sweep-only:** 15 drawn from the 38 other A rows whose `sources` is exactly `SW`; **A other:** 15 drawn from the remaining A rows (any source; 5 of these are also sweep-only, so 20 of the 30 random A rows are sweep-only); then **B 15, C 15, D 10, X 10**, each drawn from its tier. 86 channels in all.
2. **Pulls.** For each channel, logged-out, about 1 s between requests: the Videos tab default (Latest) sort, 3 pages (up to 90 uploads), and the Popular chip's first page, using the page-walking functions of `data/tools/yt_allsorts.py` (one change: it takes the grid's continuation token, the first one, not the last, which on these pages is a header token and returns nothing); and the About panel by the `master_about.py` / `yt_water1.py about` method. Python run with `-I`. Two channels failed on the first pull (DuC, Dan Carters Workshop: no page data) and were pulled again successfully. 9 channels offer no Popular chip (their chips are Latest / Members only / Public); for them the "top 10" is the 10 most-viewed of the latest 90 uploads, marked in the table.
3. **HVAC share of the latest 30 long-form.** Long-form = tab duration 5:00 or more; the latest 30 such videos (fewer when the channel has fewer; n is always shown). I read every title myself. A first-pass word filter flagged titles and I corrected it by eye; every title's final mark is in the raw JSON (`hvac_checker`). **Counted:** AC, heat pumps, furnaces, boilers, radiators, thermostats, filters, ducts and vents, HVAC parts, refrigerant, humidity / dehumidifiers / indoor air, bath and attic fans, insulation and drafts when the title is about heat, cooling or the bill, the energy bill, wood / pellet / diesel / space heaters and "free heat / free cooling" tricks, and staying warm or cool in an outage. **Not counted** (scope rules in `README.md` and `labels.md`): water heaters and frozen pipes (plumbing), generator and power-station reviews (operator rule), car / truck AC, PC cooling, ceiling-fan remotes and wiring (electrical), patio heaters, pellet grills, and words that only look like HVAC ("Furnace" the Clash Royale card, "Ac Ac Ac Dance", "Heat" the team).
4. **Top 10.** The same marks on the Popular sort's top 10 (any length, as the tab shows them).
5. **Type.** The 3 most recent long-form thumbnails (`hqdefault.jpg`) of every channel, downloaded to scratch and viewed as contact sheets, classified by `labels.md` section 5 (no face in 3 recent = faceless; AI-made images of a named character = faceless persona). Company channels are marked "brand" with what the thumbnails show.
6. **My tier** = the master's own tier definitions (`channels-master.md`) applied to what the channel posts now: A HVAC-first for homeowners; B general home DIY with a real HVAC share; C the adjacent lanes; D trade-facing or a brand / contractor / retailer; X off-niche. **Rule tier** = the objective rule proposed below, computed by script from the counts.

Readings vs measurements: subscriber counts, countries, durations, view counts and titles are measured (YouTube, logged out, 2026-10-24). Each title's HVAC mark, the thumbnail type and both tiers are my readings.

## Proposed objective rule

Computed from each channel's own Videos tab (Latest sort, latest 30 long-form >= 5:00; Popular top 10), with titles marked HVAC by the scope list in step 3 above.

1. **D (trade or brand) comes first.** D when either (a) the channel is a company: the name or the About text names a contractor, retailer, manufacturer, dealer or parts business ("Heating & Air", "Supply", "Parts", "Stoves", "LLC", "showroom", "family business"), or (b) 30% or more of the latest 30 titles address the trade ("techs", "technicians", "contractors", "installers", "training", "EPA", "sales", "gas engineers"). In my test a word list alone over-catches (The Thought Provocateur's contractor *interviews* hit 12/30; "parts" and "services" in About texts hit Richard Lloyd and Project Farm), so the census should treat (a) and (b) as a flag and confirm with one look at the About page. In this sample I applied D by that confirmed reading.
2. **S = HVAC share of the latest 30 long-form.** If the channel has fewer than 10 long-form videos, pool them with the top 10 and mark LOW-N. If it has none in the latest 90 uploads, use the latest 30 of any length and say so.
3. **A** if S >= 50%.
4. **B or C** if 10% <= S < 50%: **C** when 30% or more of the latest 30 titles carry an adjacent-lane word (off-grid, grid, outage, blackout, no power, prepper, survival, emergency, stockpile, solar, battery, generator, power station, old-timer, Amish, Japanese, ancient, forgotten, homestead, free heat, NASA, wood stove, pellet, diesel heater, frugal, net zero, cleantech, Dollar Tree, Walmart, "Top 5 Best"); otherwise **B**.
5. **X** if S < 10%. Add **LEGACY** when the top 10 is 30% or more HVAC (keep the channel for outlier mining, not as a competitor).
6. **DRIFT:** an A whose newest 10 long-form hold 2 or fewer HVAC titles drops to X and is flagged.
7. Keep the lane (off-grid, outage, old-ways, energy, hacks, brand verdicts) as its **own column** rather than inside the tier, so an off-grid channel that is 70% heating stays visible as both "A" and "off-grid".
