# HVAC study: label definitions

**Status:** draft, written 2026-10-23 by the labelling helper (round 1, Silver Cymbal seeds). Not yet re-checked by a second helper. The new labels (E19, E20 and the new formats and sub-niches) need the operator's yes before they are treated as fixed. The electrical study should use the same labels (`RESEARCH-BRIEF.md` section 3), so any change here should be offered to that chat too.

**Every label is a reading, not a measurement.** Mark label columns "(reading)" in every table.

## How labels are read (all four kinds)

1. **Title first,** then **thumbnail text** (download `hqdefault.jpg` and look).
2. The **description** only confirms or breaks a tie, and the table notes when it did. It never adds a label the title and thumbnail don't support.
3. A **transcript** only when title, thumbnail and description still can't decide. Say which videos were checked. (Round 1: none needed; descriptions were read for all 22 seeds and 19 borderline rows, and thumbnails viewed for all 41.)
4. Label what the video **sells in its packaging,** not what happens in it.

## 1. Emotions (E1 to E20)

Up to three per video, strongest first. E1 to E18 are the plumbing study's triggers (`script-system/studies/plumbing/comparison/05-emotions.md`, section 1, the "What" line of each, summarised). The HVAC trigger is my example of what would set each one off in this niche (illustrative, not a real title).

| Code | Emotion | Plumbing definition (summarised) | HVAC trigger (example) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Fear of the big bill | Dread of a repair far bigger than the problem looks; the four-figure surprise for people on planned budgets. Almost always cheap fix now against expensive repair later. | A full-system replacement quote for what turns out to be a cheap part; also, in HVAC, **the energy bill** ("SAVE money", "low electric bill"). HVAC note: E1 covers both the repair bill and the monthly bill. |
| E2 | Resentment of the service call | The sting of paying a stranger a lot for a short visit; the call-out fee and being at someone's mercy. | The tune-up visit or no-heat call that "just" swapped a part; in HVAC it also covers the installer or contractor who did a poor job. |
| E3 | Distrust of the store shelf | Suspicion that products are sold to look like they work, or cause the next problem. | Filters, "miracle" coil sprays, and in HVAC **equipment and brands**: the heat pump sold as working in any cold, the spray foam can used wrong. |
| E4 | Dread of silent, hidden damage | Fear of something failing out of sight, inside a wall, a floor or a pipe. | A clogged condensate line leaking into the ceiling; a cracked heat exchanger; mould in ducts or the crawlspace. |
| E5 | Emergency dread and urgency | Fear of the bad night: no heat in January, the holiday rate; helplessness and emergency pricing. | "No heat" on a freezing night; the AC quitting in a heat wave; a winter power outage. |
| E6 | Frustration of failed attempts | "I did everything and it still didn't work"; the problem back in three weeks. | Changed the filter, reset the thermostat, still blowing warm air. |
| E7 | Disgust | The "ugh" of slime, hair, smells, what lives in the tank. | Black gunk in the drain pan, a mouldy blower wheel, mouse-soiled insulation. |
| E8 | Satisfaction of the clear payoff | The pleasure of a result you can see or hear. | The condenser coil hosed clean, the drain line gushing clear, the install finished in a day. |
| E9 | Embarrassment and household shame | Worry about what guests notice, or that a smell means a dirty house. | A musty "dirty sock" smell from the vents when family visits. |
| E10 | Insider curiosity | The pull of a secret: a simple answer someone has been sitting on. | "The #1 problem", "the setting nobody uses", "3 unknown features". |
| E11 | Being let in (generosity, kinship) | The warmth of being treated as an insider by someone in the trade. | "What I do on my own furnace", "what I told my own mother to set her thermostat to". |
| E12 | Vindication and the myth-bust | "I always did it this way and I was right", or "what you were told is wrong". | "Oil heat is safer than you were told"; "you've been changing your filter wrong". |
| E13 | Pride in self-reliance | Handling it without help and not being taken advantage of; a generation that fixed things. | "Do it yourself", "install your own AC", "1 person!", "for homeowners". |
| E14 | Relief and reassurance | The release when a feared expense or harm isn't needed. | "It's probably not the compressor"; "an easy fix"; "safe to run all night". |
| E15 | Nostalgia and inherited wisdom | Warmth for old ways, old products and the people who taught us. | The old cast-iron radiator or boiler that still works; how granddad heated the house. |
| E16 | Protectiveness (family, pets, parents) | Keeping loved ones safe; this audience is often the caregiver. | Carbon monoxide and the grandchildren; an elderly parent in a cold house. |
| E17 | Safety fear | Fear of being hurt by the fix itself: mixing cleaners, carbon monoxide. | Capacitor shock, gas leaks, CO from a furnace, a space heater fire. |
| E18 | Rule-curiosity and mild defiance | "Why is this banned?", irritation at rules, worry about inspections, warranties or insurance. | Refrigerant rules (R-22, R-410A), "is it legal to install it yourself?", "voids your warranty", "legal too!". |
| **E19** | **Comfort (NEW)** | Not in the plumbing list. Defined below. | "Not cooling", "make it cold again", "keep anyone cool", "leaving people cold", "keeps you warm". |
| **E20** | **New-gadget wonder (NEW)** | Not in the plumbing list. Defined below. | "This tiny AC blows them away", "most powerful ever made", "first look at the new...". |

### E19. Comfort (NEW, 2026-10-23)

**What:** the misery of a house that's too hot or too cold, and the wish to feel right in your own home again. For older viewers, heat and cold are felt harder (sleepless summer nights, a cold bedroom), so the body's comfort is the point, not the fix itself.

**How to tell it apart:**
- **E5** is the *emergency* (no heat at all on a freezing night, the system dead).
- **E8** is the *pleasure of seeing the fix work.*
- **E19** is the everyday *temperature* itself: weak cooling, "cold again", "stay cool", "left cold".

**Why a new one:** none of E1 to E18 covers a body that's too hot or too cold. Plumbing never needed it, because pipes don't make a room hot.

**Seeds that needed it (9):** `_b6p0fkWhCY`, `o8KQu_MlpoY`, `DSNUyxZtrmo`, `nsRHhU6q5RY`, `BP1iDvKpNZA`, `NsPBamTmOPM`, `uObGbs4sGQY`, `S8XO4wFhrPU`, `OGVXmIZtEG8`.

### E20. New-gadget wonder (NEW, 2026-10-23)

**What:** excitement at a new, surprisingly small or surprisingly powerful product ("tiny", "most powerful", "first look", "just got better"). It rides on curiosity about what technology can do now.

**How to tell it apart:**
- **E10** is a hidden *answer.*
- **E8** is the *payoff of a fix.*
- **E20** is the pull of the *product itself.*

**Why a new one:** the plumbing study had no product-review videos. Silver Cymbal's portable-AC and power-station reviews have nothing else that fits.

**Seeds that needed it (6):** `leEsPGaaru8`, `NsPBamTmOPM`, `uObGbs4sGQY`, `yZavrHJsBAw`, `S8XO4wFhrPU`, `OGVXmIZtEG8`. Borderline rows (5): `yNBXE3BszEM`, `_G7ZPjmR5ck`, `3x5t4Zvkqms`, `KV4tWIPLFc8`, `IgeexSKIEOQ`.

**Note (reading):** this looks like an on-camera reviewer's emotion. It may matter little for a faceless "retired pro" whose audience distrusts being sold to. It's kept so the reviews can be labelled honestly.

## 2. Villains

**One main villain per video,** or "none", or "insider". Read it from the title and the thumbnail. The villain must be named or plainly implied (a red X on a product counts; a vague "save money" doesn't). If two fit, take the one the title names.

| Villain | What it is | How to decide (title or thumbnail says or plainly implies...) |
|---|---|---|
| the HVAC company | The trade as a business, or a contractor or installer who overcharges, cuts corners or does a bad job. | "HVAC companies hate this", "my installer ruined it", "ripped off by the contractor". (In the borderline table, the solar contractor goes here.) |
| the service call | The visit itself and its fee: paying someone to come out. | "Avoid the repairman", "no electrician needed", "before you call a tech", "don't pay for a tune-up". |
| the upsell | Being pushed to replace or add on when a fix would do. | "They'll tell you to replace it", "don't let them sell you", "what your salesman didn't tell you", "you don't need a new system". |
| the bill-sender (utility) | The power, gas or oil company, or the energy bill itself, cast as the enemy. | "The power company doesn't want you to know", "your electric bill is robbing you". A plain "save money" or "low electric bill" tick is **not** enough. |
| the maker and the store shelf | Products, brands and equipment that don't do what they claim, or sold products used wrong. | "Why these are failing" (on a brand's unit), a red X on the store product, "Dreo claims...", "features they forgot to tell you about". |
| the last owner or the flipper | Whoever left the house wrong: the previous owner, the flipper and (study note) the old builders. | "The last owner did this", "flipper special", "they were wrong" about how the house was built. |
| your own habit | The viewer's own mistake or routine. | "Stop wasting money", "you're doing it wrong", "the right way", "mistakes you're making". |
| none | No adversary in the packaging. | The default. Most plain how-to and install videos. |
| insider | Not a villain: the friendly technician sharing what the trade knows. | "An HVAC tech's tip", "what techs do at home", "a pro showed me". Mark "insider" only when no villain is named; if both are, record the villain and note the insider. |

## 3. Formats

**One main format per video.** Go down the list below and take the **first** rule the title matches. Use the thumbnail text only when the title is just a topic label (for example "Furnace Filters"). The brief's ten formats keep their names; the four marked NEW are added for HVAC.

**A list is not a format.** "Top 10", "3 unknown features" and "#1 problem" are list shapes. The main format is still decided by the rules below; note the list shape in a comment if it matters.

| Order | Format | Decision rule | Example title |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | test | Something is put through a stated trial or measurement: "testing", "I tested", "how safe is X?", "surviving N hours with X". | "How Safe are DREO Electric Space Heaters?" (`ZJQjKOnbLKU`); "Surviving a 36 Hour Power Outage with the BIGGEST ECOFLOW DELTA PRO ULTRA" (`_G7ZPjmR5ck`) |
| 2 | X vs Y | Two named options are compared ("vs", "better than", "instead of" with both named). | "Why Heating Oil is BETTER than GAS - Explained" (`GdTOl1WcHV0`) |
| 3 | the only X I'd buy | A verdict naming the one or best product or brand: "the best X", "the only brands worth buying", "never buy X, buy Y". | "The BEST Portable Air Conditioner Just Got BETTER for 2026" (`OGVXmIZtEG8`) |
| 4 | review (NEW) | One specific product (named in the title, or the subject of the thumbnail) is shown and judged: "review", "first look", "is it worth it?", specs and claims. If the video mainly installs it, use install/DIY project instead. | "Ecoflow Wave 2 Solar Battery Powered Portable Air Conditioner Heater Review" (`yZavrHJsBAw`) |
| 5 | myth-bust | Says a common belief or practice is wrong: "myth", "doesn't work", "stop doing", "they were wrong". | Illustrative: "Closing Vents Doesn't Save You Money" (not a real title) |
| 6 | Y doesn't want you to know | Promises knowledge someone keeps back: "secret", "they don't tell you", "what techs won't say", "hidden" or "unknown features". | "3 Unknown Features on Mr Cool Mini Split Air Conditioners" (`cC-J0ZCUH28`; thumbnail: "TRICKS THEY FORGOT TO TELL YOU ABOUT") |
| 7 | mistakes | Names the viewer's mistakes or wrong ways: "X mistakes", "you're doing it wrong", "never do this to your AC". | Illustrative: "5 Thermostat Mistakes That Raise Your Bill" (not a real title) |
| 8 | before you do X | Tells the viewer to stop before a step (buy, call, replace, sign): "before replacing", "don't buy X before...", "watch this before...". | "Fix & Repair Stuck Circulator Pump Before Replacing NO HEAT" (`yCZqfnlBYW8`); "Don't Buy a Rooftop Solar System Before Seeing my Disaster" (`Roz9OXl5uEU`) |
| 9 | news-warning (NEW) | A change or trend presented as news the viewer must hear: "after learning this news", "why X are now failing", new rules, bans, tax credits. | "Why Heat Pumps are now leaving people COLD" (`BP1iDvKpNZA`); "Everyone will be buying pellet stoves after learning this news" (`9d9TL02idO4`) |
| 10 | cheap hack | A cheap substitute or trick in place of a dearer product or service: "use THIS instead", "$5 trick", "cheap & easy way". | "Spray Foam is Expensive. Use THIS Instead!" (`sHL4lBAhklA`); "Cheap & Easy Way to Connect a Generator to Your House - Legal Too!" (`pPMm0reEBPM`) |
| 11 | symptom-fix (NEW) | Names a symptom or failure the viewer has, and promises the fix: "not cooling?", "no heat", "water stains on ceiling", "blowing warm air", "smells funky". | "#1 Problem & Quick Fix with Central Air Conditioning Not Cooling" (`_b6p0fkWhCY`); "Window Air Conditioner Not Cooling? EASY Common Fix" (`o8KQu_MlpoY`) |
| 12 | install/DIY project (NEW) | Installs or builds a whole system, appliance or project from start to finish, often with a time claim: "install your own", "in 4 hours", "in 1 day", "start to finish", "transformation". | "Install your own AIR CONDITIONING in 4 HOURS! DIY Mini Split MR COOL" (`Wejop_Rjpv0`) |
| 13 | how-to | A maintenance or repair procedure with no symptom named: "how to clean", "how to replace", "the right way". | "How to Clean Your Mini Split AC Condenser - Make it Cold Again" (`DSNUyxZtrmo`); "How to Replace a Circulator Pump on a Boiler" (`flpbqJu2sT8`) |
| 14 | explainer (NEW) | How something works, or why, with no procedure: "how it works", "understand the basics", "explained". | "Oil Heat Boilers - How it works - Understand the Basics" (`5GbXgEYw5TE`) |
| 15 | story | A first-person event or saga, when no rule above applies: "surviving the storm", "what happened when...". | "SURVIVING the Worst Snow Storm in 30 Years" (`OYM8aSESAY8`) |

**Close calls in round 1** (so later helpers decide them the same way):

- "Make it cold again" is an outcome, not a symptom, so those videos are **how-to**. "Not cooling" is a symptom, so it's **symptom-fix**.
- "Before Replacing ... NO HEAT" is **before you do X**, because rule 8 comes before rule 11.
- "Spray foam is expensive. Use THIS instead" is **cheap hack**: X vs Y needs both options named.
- "Say Goodbye to BIG Air Conditioners - This Tiny Portable AC Blows them Away!" is **review**: one product is the subject of the thumbnail, and "big ACs" is a class, not a named option.
- "How to Install a Mr Cool 18,000 BTU AC" names a product but is mainly an install, so it's **install/DIY project**.

## 4. Sub-niches (HVAC)

From the topic list in `RESEARCH-BRIEF.md` section 1, refined. One per video: the equipment or topic the title is about. A single product review stays in its equipment's sub-niche; "brand verdicts" is only for videos about choosing between brands.

| Sub-niche | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| AC: central | Central split-system AC: outdoor condenser, coils, refrigerant, condensate drain and pan, "not cooling". |
| AC: window | Window units: cleaning, installing, choosing. |
| AC: portable | Portable ACs, including battery-powered units and portable heat pumps (EcoFlow Wave, Dreo, Midea). |
| mini-split | Ductless mini-splits, including DIY kits (MrCool) and heat-and-cool combo units, when the mini-split itself is the subject. |
| heat pumps | Heat pumps as a heating choice: cold-climate performance, ducted heat pumps, "do heat pumps work?". |
| furnaces | Forced-air furnaces (gas, oil, electric): ignition, flame sensor, blower, no heat. |
| boilers and old-house heat | Boilers, radiators, baseboard, steam, circulator pumps, zone valves, oil burners. |
| heating fuel and fuel choice | Oil, gas, propane, electric: costs, safety and comparisons between fuels. |
| thermostats | Thermostat settings, types, smart thermostats, wiring. |
| filters | Furnace and AC filters: type, MERV rating, how often to change. |
| ducts and vents | Ducts, registers, returns, airflow, balancing, duct cleaning and sealing. |
| parts homeowners touch | Capacitors, contactors, disconnects, fuses, condensate pumps, when the part is the subject of the title. |
| seasonal maintenance | Tune-ups and checklists across the whole system ("do this before summer"). |
| energy bills | The bill as the subject: running costs, settings and habits that save. |
| drafts, insulation and air sealing | Insulation, drafts, weatherstripping, spray foam, windows. Operator rule (2026-10-23): it counts as an HVAC seed only when the title is about heating, cooling or the bill. |
| humidity, ventilation and indoor air | Dehumidifiers, humidifiers, mould, crawlspaces, exhaust and range-hood fans, air purifiers. |
| heat and power in an outage | Staying warm (or cool) with no power. Operator rule: generator videos count only when they're about keeping the house warm; generator and power-station product reviews are out. |
| alternative and supplemental heat | Pellet and wood stoves, diesel heaters, space heaters. Space-heater safety is also the electrical study's. |
| brand verdicts | Which brands or models to buy or avoid, across a category. |
| buying and replacing a system (NEW) | Quotes, sizing, repair or replace, contractor choice, replacement costs. Added under "anything else the search turns up"; none in the seeds so far. |
| adjacent or out of scope | Water heaters (plumbing), frozen pipes (plumbing), solar, cars. Recorded as "adjacent: ..." or "out of scope: ..." so they can be seen but not counted. |

## 5. Channel types

As in `RESEARCH-BRIEF.md` deliverable 1. One per channel.

| Type | What it is | How to decide |
|---|---|---|
| on camera | A real person presents on camera: their face shown talking or working. | The face appears in recent videos or thumbnails (Silver Cymbal: his face in the `sHL4lBAhklA` thumbnail). |
| faceless | No presenter's face. Real or stock footage, hands only, screen or text, with a human or AI voice and no named character. | No face in 3 recent videos. Cross-check NexLev `check_faceless_channel` (a helper's tool, not proof). |

The planned Roger the HVAC Guy is this type. The brief calls Ray Dutton's Garage a faceless "retired HVAC pro", so it is probably this type too; the census should check it. |

## 6. The multiple

**The multiple = a video's views ÷ the median views of that channel's long-form videos (300 seconds or more) from the last 12 months.** If the channel has fewer than 10 such videos, use the median of its last 30 long-form videos instead. Always say which rule was used, the n, and the baseline figure (`RESEARCH-BRIEF.md` section 3).

- **Example:** Silver Cymbal, 12-month rule, n = 18 long-form videos (2025-10-23 to 2026-10-23), baseline 79,807 views. So "#1 Problem & Quick Fix with Central Air Conditioning Not Cooling" at 4,307,728 views is 53.98x (`seeds/silver-cymbal.md`).
- **Caution:** an old video measured against a recent baseline gets a high multiple partly from age, and partly because the channel's recent videos are weaker. Note the upload year beside any multiple you compare.
