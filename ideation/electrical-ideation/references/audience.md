## 6. Real mechanisms that support big titles (the honest floor under exaggeration)

All source-checked by the brief checkers (`ideation/briefs-work/check-c1/c2/c3.json`, 2026-10-27); the source is in brackets.

- **Backfeed:** a generator on a male-to-male cord pushes power out through the meter, which disconnects nothing, to the pole transformer. 240 V into the secondary comes out at roughly 7,200 V on the primary, a 30-to-1 ratio (Eversource: 7,200 V primary, transformers "don't care which way the power is flowing"). CPSC: an electrocution risk to utility workers and to neighbors on the same transformer, and it bypasses some built-in household circuit protection. No confirmed lineman death case was found, so "can kill the lineman" is a recognized hazard, not a documented case. CPSC Release 26-363 (31 March 2026): male-to-male cords "have no legitimate household use", and listings were removed from Walmart, eBay and AliExpress.
- **Loose connections heat up:** "A loose connection cannot carry much current without getting hot"; "Loose-fitting plugs can cause overheating and fires" (CPSC Pub. 513). This is the honest bridge from a nuisance to fire as the stakes.
- **Worn receptacle contacts:** spring brass contacts fatigue from use, angled tugs and heat until they stop gripping. CPSC 513's answer: have the outlet replaced. A replacement must meet today's GFCI, AFCI, tamper-resistant and weather-resistant rules (NEC 406.4(D)(3)-(6), 406.12). Spreading the plug blades is not a fix (reasoning).
- **Backstab limits:** push-in terminals are limited to 14 AWG solid copper on 15 A circuits (UL 498 guide information; IAEI 2023 summary). Loop the wire clockwise about three-quarters of a turn (Leviton). Strip length is 5/8 to 3/4 inch for side wiring, depending on the device.
- **Short wires:** at least 6 in. of free conductor and 3 in. past the opening of a box smaller than 8 in. (NEC 300.14). Since 2023 a spliced pigtail counts toward it (EC&M).
- **GFCI downstream protection:** a GFCI wired through its LOAD terminals protects ordinary outlets downstream, which have no buttons and may sit in another room. A GFCI won't reset with no power, with line and load reversed, or at end of life. CPSC says test monthly. A tripped GFCI or AFCI breaker can rest mid-position and look on.
- **Glowing LEDs:** capacitive coupling between the switch leg and the always-hot conductor in the same cable lets microamps through. Cheap capacitive-dropper LED drivers light on that. Lighted switches (Pass & Seymour) and no-neutral smart switches do the same. The dangerous case is a switch on the neutral (barred by NEC 404.2(B)), which leaves the socket at 120 V. NEC 404.2(C) requires a neutral only at new switch locations.
- **Aluminum branch wiring:** homes wired 1965 to the mid-1970s (CPSC Pub. 516). CPSC calls CO/ALR devices "at best, an incomplete repair" and recognizes COPALUM or AlumiConn repairs, made by an electrician.
- **Federal Pacific and Zinsco:** independent testing linked FPE Stab-Lok breakers to failures to trip, and CPSC closed its 1983 investigation without a recall. Zinsco breakers fusing to the aluminum bus rests on inspector reports only, so say "inspectors report".
- **Also verified:** a bootleg ground fools a three-light tester and is barred (NEC 250.142(B)). The breaker protects the wire (14 AWG 15 A, 12 AWG 20 A, 10 AWG 30 A; NEC 240.4(D), with an AC and motor exception at 240.4(G)). A standard breaker does not trip on a low-level ground fault. Transfer equipment must be listed (2023 NEC 702.5). A generator with a bonded neutral adds a second bond on equipment that does not switch the neutral (Generac). NM cable is barred in wet locations, and the inside of underground conduit is wet (NEC 334.12(B)(4), 300.5(B)). Solder-seal connectors carry no building-wire listing. Plug-prong holes are optional and for manufacturing (NEMA), and the wartime-rationing story doesn't fit.

## 7. Safety boundaries (flag inside briefs where relevant)

- **Power off at the breaker and verified dead with a tester** before any box is opened. Check a non-contact tester on a known live outlet before and after; silence never proves a wire dead. A split receptacle can be fed by two breakers, so verify both halves (C12, A33 checks).
- **Panel interiors and service work are a licensed electrician's job.** With the dead-front off, the service conductors and lugs stay live even with the main off (A30 check). The same goes for meter, service and utility-side neutral problems.
- **Permits:** many jurisdictions require one for new circuits, extensions, generator connections and outdoor runs; the inspector has the final say (A1, A7, B10). A generator connection is a permit job for a licensed electrician.
- **Aluminum wiring repairs are an electrician's job** (CPSC 516). So are scorched, melted or brittle wiring and moving a switch off the neutral.
- **Generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from doors, windows and vents, never in a garage, with battery CO alarms inside** (CPSC 2017 alert). Connect through listed transfer equipment only (NEC 702.5).
- **Never a male-to-male cord, and never anything that reads as how to backfeed** (CPSC Release 26-363).
- **Never claim a demonstration.** The channel explains; it never says it tested, measured or burned anything (operator: "we cannot do exact demonstrations"). Generated visuals never demonstrate technique (core `rules-and-specs.md` §2).
- **Never upsize a breaker,** and never strip extra insulation to make a short wire reach.
