# Panel 2: the ADHD Feed-Scroller lens

**Who's scoring:** two real viewer types from Roger's comment section, played in turn.

- **Linda, 72.** Widow with a 1962 ranch house. She watched the drain-habit video (980K) and bought "100 Old-House Fixes". At night she binges cleaning videos and "grandma's tricks".
- **Dale, 66.** Retired lineman with a 1948 farmhouse. He watches generators, mowers, HVAC and "they don't make them like that" videos. (He is a viewer. He has nothing to do with the operator's Dale Oakfield woodworking persona.)

Both of us have short attention spans, want something new, and want a quick payoff. A repeat bores us. When something clicks, we watch five in a row.

**The question:** would Roger's real viewers click it, binge it, *and* follow it over from a Roger video?

**How we read the evidence:** a big outlier on a channel we've never heard of doesn't mean we'd follow it from Roger. Cross-cutting finding #1 is the one that scares us: Eli Yoder The Plumber got about 590 subs a month, and Uncle Kenji got 4.1K. We don't follow a name. We follow a character we already met, or a topic Roger made us worry about.

## Our TOP 8, in order

1. **U, Roger's Job-Site Stories (9).** Every video is a new story, so we never get the "seen it" feeling, and stories are what we play back to back at night. The title can put Linda in it ("a widow was quoted $4,000"), and Dale gets the crooked-contractor villain. It's Roger's own voice and memories, so it doesn't feel like a stranger's channel. The hand-off writes itself ("I'll tell you about that job on my other channel").
2. **Q, Roger's Bill & Scam Watch (8).** Fear plus anger is the quickest hook there is, and Linda knows she's a target. Roger already distrusts upsells, so following him over feels like "Roger's looking out for me". Keep it to home-repair and utility scams and you stay out of tax-advice trouble.
3. **AA, The Plumber's Wife, June (8).** Linda's people are the fastest-growing type in Roger's graph: Bertha, Japanese Home Hacks, Otis's Kitchen. June is the one spin-off with a built-in way for Linda to *meet* her inside Roger's own videos. That beats any collab byline. It has to have a voice that doesn't sound like text-to-speech; 129 people already liked that complaint.
4. **I, Roger Around the World, with G as season 1 (8).** It's the best format for short attention spans: same trusted judge, new country every time, ranking verdict. Open with Japan, because Linda and Dale already watch the Japanese Method. Rotating countries means it never repeats. That's the thing that kills C, K and AB for us.
5. **H, Roger Goes Amish (8).** We're sick of "the Amish system they buried". Roger *trying* it on a real old farmhouse is new enough to click. It's Dale's favourite on the list. Eli Yoder already shows this audience bingeing Amish heat and water content.
6. **J, Roger's Toilet Through Time (8).** It's gross, it's history and it's told by a plumber, which is easy to click on. The night-binge habit matters most here: a 2-hour "fall asleep to Roger" version fits how Linda really watches. The hand-off is weaker than U or Q, so Roger needs to mention it inside drain videos ("this goes back to the Great Stink...").
7. **X, Roger vs. the Flippers (7).** Anger at bad work holds Dale through three videos easily, and Linda fears buying or fixing a botched house. Without footage it has to be a narrative of what Roger has found, not "look at this". That costs a point.
8. **P, Walt the Appliance Man (7).** It's the most practical binge chain (washer, dryer, fridge, dishwasher, water heater) and the most real fear: being upsold on a $1,400 machine. A brother-in-law is a character Roger can introduce. "Only 4 X I'd buy" is flooded, though, so Walt's personality has to carry it.

(Near misses at 7: D Water Guy, E Dry Taps, F Buck the Bug Man, A Old House, V Frontier Fixes.)

## KILL LIST (don't build these as separate channels)

- **L, Roger's Old Advice.** There's no trick and no payoff, so we don't binge it. One sermon is enough for us. The format is saturated and it spikes then fades (Peter Whitmore decayed to ~13.6K views a month).
- **O, Roger's Garage.** A plumber on cars breaks our trust, and the feed is full of mechanic AI channels getting 9 to 293 views.
- **Y, Roger the Body Plumber.** We'd click, then feel uneasy. It risks the main brand and YouTube's medical policy for a plumbing gag.
- **Z, Roger Tries It.** A faceless "test" with no test shown. We'd catch it in one video. Keep it as a Roger main-channel series of verdict rankings like his own 176K "Ranks All 4".
- **R, Roger's Penny.** Frugal Frannie already serves Linda, and the lists repeat.
- **T, Roger's Pipe Smoker.** It promises something physical ("I welded it") that faceless b-roll can't show. James J. Conner already owns the old-pitmaster seat.
- **AC, Roger on the Road.** It serves too thin a slice of us, and the name collides with an existing channel.
- **AD, The Sarge.** The evidence shows one hit per channel and no binge, and it collides with the operator's Navy-vet persona.
- **C as a standalone channel.** It's the fifth peroxide channel in our feed. Its content goes to AA and to Roger's own playlists.

## One idea nobody listed: "Roger After Dark" (a sleep-and-night-binge channel)

Linda binges at night. The evidence already holds the pieces:

- The J lane found sleep-history RPMs of 6.8 to 15.7 (Mr&Mrs HISTORY 13.5, Goodwin 12.4), which is higher than Roger's own 8.13.
- That lane found "plumbing for sleep" fails *without an existing audience*. Roger has one, and his top commenters are women 70+.
- Roger's voice is already calm and unhurried, and he already makes ~22-minute long-form videos.

**The idea:** a channel of 2 to 3 hour slow-narrated compilations. Each one strings together Roger's job-site stories (U), toilet and sanitation history (J) and "old house through the decades" segments (A) for falling asleep.

Why it might work for us:
- It isn't a new topic for us to get bored of. It's the Roger we already like, in the format we actually use at 11pm.
- The hand-off is one line in Roger's end screen: "Can't sleep? I've got a long one for you."
- It reuses scripts the other channels already paid for.

The risk is that the TTS-voice complaint gets louder over 3 hours, so it needs the best voice available.
