# Panel 4: The Dyslexic Big-Picture Storyteller

**Lens:** I skip the tables and look at the whole shape. My question: does this make sense for **Roger as a character**, and does it make the Roger universe richer and easier to love?

**Score = character logic + story engine + memorability**, on a 1-10 scale. One gate applies: the evidence has to show that this audience already watches the underlying topic. Weird is welcome when it passes the gate. Realistic but dull scores low.

## Who Roger is (what I scored against)

- Blunt and unhurried. 45 years in the trade, so he is in his sixties.
- Pro-homeowner and allergic to upsells.
- He has a little ritual: a drain check on the first Saturday of the month, with his coffee.
- He owns an old house and wrote a book about old houses.
- His canon (`personas/roger-the-pipe-guy.md`) is very tight:
  - the coffee ritual is the **only** approved personal detail
  - his experience stays general
  - he tells **no customer stories, no tests and no demonstrations**

So every family member below is a new canon fact the operator would have to approve. I am proposing a cast, not writing it into the persona.

## Top 8

1. **AA. Dottie, the plumber's wife (9).**
   - Every great "old man" character is better with the person who rolls her eyes at him.
   - She opens the channel the women in Roger's comments actually want: cleaning, laundry, pantry, "what a plumber's family never buys".
   - She absorbs the Cupboard (C), Pantry (S) and Penny (R) ideas into one living person instead of three dead shelves.
   - "Cheap Roger" becomes her running joke rather than his channel.

2. **G. Roger's Japanese Obsession (9).**
   - The boldest idea that still passes the evidence gate.
   - A blunt old US plumber who distrusts everything shiny, except Japanese bathrooms, is a contradiction people remember and repeat.
   - Each video ends with his verdict ("would I put it in my house?"), which no insider persona can give.
   - The engine runs a long way: toilets, the ofuro, unit baths, humidity and mold, tankless heaters, peroxide.
   - When Japan runs dry, it becomes "and Everywhere Else" (I).

3. **F. Buck the Bug Man, Roger's younger brother (8).**
   - The sibling rivalry is the story engine, and the drain is the shared border: drain flies, roaches through traps, mice in pipe chases.
   - Huge demand, served today by grannies and the Amish, with no trusted trade veteran.
   - Low RPM is a money problem, not a character problem.

4. **AB. Uncle Virgil up the holler (8).**
   - Roger's origin. Where he learned it, and why he is the way he is.
   - It gives the old ways (K) and the frontier stories (V) a family to belong to: "Great-granddad's buried pipe", "Mamaw's borax".
   - The heat-without-a-furnace lane is in season right now.

5. **J. Roger's Toilet Through Time (8).**
   - Roger's private nerd hobby: reading sanitation history at night.
   - Endearing, weird and wide open: no plumber narrator, and the best Great Stink videos are 6-9 years old.
   - His unhurried voice turns the sleep-length arm (RPM 6.8-15.7) into a natural second product.

6. **U. Roger's Job-Site Stories, told by his grandson (8).**
   - The single best "make them love Roger" device: the widow, the $12 part, the crooked contractor.
   - It must not be Roger in the first person (canon). It works as a grandson telling Grandpa's stories, every one clearly labelled as a story with names changed.
   - See the new idea below.

7. **P. Lou the Appliance Man, Dottie's brother (7).**
   - The in-law sits exactly at the plumber's border: water heaters, washers, dishwashers.
   - The "Only X brands I'd buy" format is proven (2.0M) but flooded with nameless clones. A family character with history is what lifts it out of the pile.

8. **T. Roger's Pipe Smoker (7).**
   - The most visual, lovable picture in the research. It shows Roger off the clock, and the cookout is where the whole family gathers.
   - That makes it the universe's annual crossover (the Fourth of July episode in every channel).
   - Proven BBQ demand. The pipe-welded smoker is the hook Conner doesn't have.

Just missed: X (Roger Inspects, 7) and A (Old House, 7; best used as the hub set, not a sibling).

**How the universe holds together:**

- **Introduce characters on Roger's channel first, like a TV backdoor pilot.**
  - Cross-cutting finding 1 is the warning: Eli Yoder The Plumber gets about 590 subs/mo against the parent's 29K, and Martha Yoder has 104 subs.
  - A name in the title moves nobody. A character the viewer has already met 3-5 times does.
  - So Dottie, Buck and Virgil each get mentioned and quoted in several Roger videos before their channel exists.
  - This needs the persona's "one personal detail" rule widened, which is the operator's call.
- **Keep a universe bible.** One file holds the fixed facts: names, who is older, years married, the town, the holler. Every channel draws on the same file so the facts never contradict each other.
- **Voices matter more as the cast grows.** A top comment already flags Roger's TTS (129 likes). Every new character is another chance to sound robotic, and Dottie's voice matters most.
- **Annual crossovers:**
  - the Fourth of July cookout at the pipe smoker
  - the first hard freeze, when Virgil phones about pipes and Buck about mice

## One idea nobody listed: the grandson narrator ("Grandpa Roger's Job-Site Stories, told by Cody")

**The problem it solves.** U is the strongest love-the-character engine, but Roger's canon forbids him telling customer stories in the first person, and it should. The main channel sells trust and a book.

**The solution.** Put the stories in the grandson's mouth, in the third person, framed as family stories ("Grandpa told this one every Thanksgiving; names changed"). That keeps Roger's own channel clean, it makes Roger the hero rather than the boaster, and it adds a younger voice to the universe.

**The evidence:**

- **The grandson-narrator device is already working in Roger's own audience graph.** The Hillbilly Way is narrated by a Kentucky grandson passing on his Papaw's and Mamaw's ways. It hit two videos at 9-10x in its first 5 weeks.
- **Stories with an old-tradesman hero are some of the biggest faceless outliers right now:**
  - Black Dad Chronicles 101K at 80.7x
  - Pearl Stories 175K at 33.6x
  - Michael's 1948 wrecker story 667K at 29.0x
  - Iron Hook Stories at RPM 7.6
- **No plumber story channel exists.** "Plumber horror stories" searches return nothing above 6.2K.

**The universe glue** (a ritual, not a channel): **the First Saturday Club.**

- Roger's biggest video is his monthly habit (980K, 22.9x).
- Ray Dutton's biggest videos are seasonal routines ("Before You Turn On Your Heat This Fall, Do This" 926K).
- So every channel gets its own first-Saturday check: Dottie's fridge and washer gaskets, Buck's perimeter walk, Lou's water-heater flush, Virgil's chimney.
- The family's channels cross-link on that day. It gives the deliberate hand-off finding 1 asks for, wrapped in the character's best-known habit.
