### Group HVAC with insulation and energy bills (10:20 answer)

- "Of the 19 channels that make insulation videos, 17 also make HVAC videos."
- The insulation hits are bill videos: "THIS is Why Your Heat Bill is So High! Rim Joist Insulation" 5.8M (14x); "Can This Device Actually Lower Your Electric Bill By 30%??" 2.6M (33x).
- HVAC plus insulation keeps the strength: 42% of videos hit 2x or more (HVAC alone 43%).
- What works inside insulation: spray foam 3.6x, weatherstripping and drafts 3.3x, garage 2.6x. What doesn't: energy audits 0.2x, air sealing 0.6x. "A cheap fix plus a lower bill works, and building-science explainers don't."
- Outage-heat videos fit too ("Why Buy A Generator? You Have One Parked In Your Driveway" 2.4M); generator product reviews don't.
- The insulation sample is small (87 videos, 26 from Matt Risinger).

### HVAC's villain (Frederick chat, 09:46)

"Both, depending on the topic. The villain is whoever takes the viewer's money in that video":
- **Repair and maintenance topics:** the HVAC company. "HVAC Companies HATE That You Can Do This Yourself" 739k; "5 AC Mistakes HVAC Companies Hope You Keep Making" 588k.
- **Running-cost topics:** the power company. "The Utility Company HATES This Air Conditioner" 317k; "Your Power Company Doesn't Want You To See This!" 128k.
- **The technician is never the villain;** "he works better as the friendly insider" ("This SECRET AC Trick is Only Known by 1% of HVAC Technicians" 193k).

The six villain types it proposed for a DIY pack:
1. the service call
2. the upsell
3. the bill-sender (power, water or gas company)
4. the maker and the store shelf
5. the last owner or the flipper
6. your own habit (not a villain; the viewer recognises themselves)

Its rules: "One main villain per video, picked by where the title's money goes"; "Hit the price and the practice, never a named company and never a person"; "Titles can claim motive; scripts don't." **Never approved; not a rule.**

## 2. Electrical (Frederick)

### Electrical is not framed on money (10:22 answer)

"No. On these channels the money-saving, beat-the-electrician angle is the weakest way to frame an electrical video. Electrical is sold on 'you're doing it wrong' and 'here's something you didn't know', not on cost." From 575 recent electrical videos:

| Framing | Videos | Score | Hit 2x+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mistakes / doing it wrong** ("7 FATAL Wiring Mistakes", "Never use Wire Nuts Again") | 79 | **1.40x** | 46% |
| **Hidden / secret / "why"** ("Hidden Tool in an Outlet Few Know", "Why Do Electric Plugs Have Holes?") | 50 | **1.37x** | 34% |
| Plain how-to for a common job | 342 | 1.20x | 35% |
| Fear / danger alone (fire, shock, deadly) | 39 | 0.81x | 26% |
| Money / beat the electrician | 16 | 0.51x | 12% |

- The biggest electrical videos: "How to Solder Wires Together" 15M; "Add An Outlet To A Finished Wall" 13M; "7 FATAL Wiring Mistakes Most DIYers Don't Know They Are Making" 12M; "What Happens When You Plug a SUICIDE CORD in a LIVE OUTLET?" 11M; "Why Do Electric Plugs Have Holes?" 8.6M. Circuit-breaker mistakes 3.7M.
- Why it differs: "people don't call an electrician every year the way they call a plumber or an HVAC tech, so there's no recurring bill to resent. Their worry about electricity is getting it wrong or getting hurt."
- The money exceptions are energy bills: "Convert Fluorescent Lights to LED | Save Money on Energy Costs" 1.2M; "DIY Sub Panel Install… Save Thousands!" 1.0M.
- Its suggestion for older faceless audiences: "a mistake plus what it costs you", with the money "in the payoff, not the headline". Examples: "The Outlet Mistake in Old Houses That Costs You a $400 Service Call"; "7 Wiring Mistakes Electricians See in Every 1960s House".

### The hooks in electrical (10:30 answer)

Distrust and disgust: under 6 videos each, so no figure. Mistakes 1.37x, secret 1.34x, **fear of damage 1.26x** (it lifts here, unlike HVAC), easy 1.12x, money 0.53x. "Electrical runs on fear and mistakes. Money doesn't work there." Together with the table above: fear works as the payoff of a mistake or a secret, but "fear / danger alone" in the title scores 0.81x.

- **FixItWithFil:** "This Kitchen Outlet Nearly Caught Fire — Here's What Went Wrong" 269K.
- **Remote Electrician** (7.75K subs): "How to Change Electrical Outlet As a Beginner" 479K.

The old chat's concept for this lane: "outlet, cord and power-strip habits that start fires", "old panels (Federal Pacific, aluminum wire)", "what to check yourself (no live work), and the electric bill". The risk: "liability and safety. Avoid DIY live-wire instructions, keep to 'spot it, call it' and fire prevention."
